Smoke-Free Generation: Tobacco and Vapes Bill [B2]

Fumare è un’abitudine letale. Per evitare che i più giovani sviluppino una dipendenza da questo vizio, il Regno Unito ha vietato l’acquisto di tabacco per i nati dopo il 2009.

Talk to any smoker and they will tell you that they wish they had never started. Smoking kills more than seven million people a year worldwide and puts a massive burden on healthcare systems. Governments have tried to curb the habit with various measures over the years: information campaigns, indoor smoking restrictions, increased age limits, advertising bans, scary medical photos printed on packaging, but none have had a meaningful impact. Vaping, first introduced as a method to wean people off cigarettes, has people hooked just the same. The problem is addiction. Nicotine is said to be as hard to quit as heroin.

BORN TO BE FREE

in a bid to stop the smoking habit once and for all, the UK has taken a drastic measure. In April 2026, Parliament passed the Tobacco and Vapes Bill, making it illegal for anyone born on or after 1 January 2009 to purchase tobacco products. Smoking or vaping will not become illegal, however. The law only bans the sale of these products to people born after 2008 — in other words, anyone aged seventeen or less today. Vaping is subject to an advertising ban, a new tax on vaping liquids, and restrictions on flavours and packaging designed to appeal to youth. This landmark legislation, which will take effect on 1 January 2027, aims to create a smoke-free generation. Current Affairs

MALDIVES FIRST

The UK is not the first to introduce a generational smoking ban. The Maldives did it last year and it is even stricter there. People born after January 2007 are not only prohibited from buying tobacco products; they are also not allowed to have them or use them. Retailers selling to this group are fined 50,000 rufiyaa (€2,767). New Zealand also made an attempt at legislation in 2022. The ban was enacted but repealed soon afterwards under a new government, following criticism from retailers and the tobacco industry.

WILL IT WORK?

Will the new law break the cycle of addiction? Wes Streeting, the UK Secretary of State for Health and Social Care, said it is all about prevention: “For too long, smoking has claimed lives, widened inequalities and placed avoidable pressure on our health services. This law changes that trajectory — protecting young people from ever starting, while backing current smokers with the support they need to quit for good.” The UK now faces the complications of enforcement and the backlash from the tobacco lobby. As the rest of the world watches, time will tell if this marks the beginning of the end for smoking.
