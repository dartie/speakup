Will Spain win the World Cup? Is there going to be a recession this year? Who will win the 2028 US presidential election? These are just some of the things you can [bet](## "scommettere") on prediction markets. Popular since the [early 2020s](## "primi anni ‘20 del 2000"), prediction markets are online platforms where people can [bet](## "scommettere") on real-world events; that is, on almost anything in politics, economics, sports, and popular culture. They have exploded in popularity since 2024, when many people used them to [bet](## "scommettere") on who would win the US presidential election, and now register billions of dollars in transactions a week.

## YES OR NO SHARES
So how do prediction markets function? People [bet](## "scommettere") on the [outcome](## "risultato") of future events by buying [shares](## "azioni"). Most markets focus on ‘yes or no’ questions, for example, “Will 2026 be the hottest year on record?” Shares cost [bet](## "scommettere")ween $0 and $1. If a ‘yes’ share costs 65 cents, it means the market believes there is a 65 per cent probability that this will be the hottest year on record. 
Kalshi is the first regulated prediction market in the US. US TV networks CNN and CNBC have partnered with Kalshi to integrate its probability data into their news stories.
Participants buy and sell ‘yes’ or ‘no’ [shares](## "azioni"), based on what they believe, and prices fluctuate based on [supply and demand](## "domanda e offerta"). If 2026 is the hottest year on record, those who [bet](## "scommettere") ‘yes’ will win $1 per share; those who [bet](## "scommettere") ‘no’ will lose their [investment](## "investimento"). 

## PREDICTING THE FUTURE
Opinion [polls](## "sondaggi") are often more [accurate](## "accurati") than individual experts, in part because of the ‘[wisdom of the crowds](## "saggezza delle folle")’ theory which states that a large group of diverse and independent individuals will collectively make [bet](## "scommettere")ter decisions and provide more [reliable](## "affidabili") predictions than any single expert.

What’s more, prediction markets are often more [accurate](## "accurati") than opinion [polls](## "sondaggi"), in part because participants have ‘skin in the game’, meaning they must risk their money to [back](## "sostenere") their predictions, and so are financially motivated to predict [accurate](## "accurati")ly.

## PROBLEMS
However, prediction markets are not always [accurate](## "accurati") because of in[accurate](## "accurati") or incomplete information, market manipulation, [biases](## "pregiudizi")… And they are not always [fair](## "giuste, eque"), or perhaps even legal. Although federally legal in the US, many states consider them a form of [unregulated gambling](## "gioco d’azzardo non regolamentato") and are trying to [ban](## "proibire") them. They are also susceptible to illegal [insider trading](## "abuso di informazioni privilegiate"), where people with access to confidential information use it for financial gain.
This year, Kalshi, one of the most popular prediction markets, [allowed](## "permettere") users to [bet](## "scommettere") on whether Iran’s Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, would be [removed from power](## "destituire") by a predetermined date. After Khamenei was killed in February 2026, it refused to pay out approximately $54 million in winnings to those who had [bet](## "scommettere") ‘yes’, because, it said, Khamenei’s death didn’t qualify as being [removed from power](## "destituire") and because it prohibits [bet](## "scommettere")ting directly on death.
