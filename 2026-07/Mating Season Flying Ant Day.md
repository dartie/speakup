It’s that time of year again in the UK, when [ants](## "formiche") [sprout wings](## "sviluppare le ali") and emerge from their colonies to [mate](## "riprodursi"). This natural phenomenon is known as Flying Ant Day, though the title is actually a [misnomer](## "termine improprio"), since flying [ants](## "formiche") emerge over several weeks, when the weather is warm and humid. During this period, [swarms](## "sciami") of flying [ants](## "formiche"), known as ‘[alates](## "alati")’, invade homes and gardens as they participate in what’s known as the ‘nuptial flight’.

## VIRGIN QUEENS AND DRONES
Flying Ant Day normally takes place in July and August in the UK, and mostly involves a species called the black garden ant, or Lasius niger. So are all black garden [ants](## "formiche") flying [ants](## "formiche")? No. Once a year, however, many of their colonies produce reproductive females, called virgin queens, and males, called [drones](## "fuchi"), which develop wings.
Flying [ants](## "formiche") from different colonies then emerge at the same time, during optimal weather conditions — [hence](## "da qui, quindi") the phenomenon known as Flying Ant Day. This synchronised emergence maximises their chance of mating with [ants](## "formiche") from other colonies, thus reducing [inbreeding](## "endogamia"). And it offers them protection from predators, because predators cannot consume all the [ants](## "formiche") at once.
A cross section of an ant colony.

## HUNDREDS OF THOUSANDS
Queens are larger than [drones](## "fuchi"), and the two are often seen flying together. The queen normally [mate](## "riprodursi")s with more than one drone. Incredibly, this single nuptial flight [enables](## "permettere") her to produce fertilised eggs for the rest of her life — often hundreds of thousands of eggs over a [lifespan](## "aspettativa di vita") of up to twenty or even thirty years. Once she’s finished mating, she [chews off](## "staccare a morsi") her own wings and then starts a new colony underground. She does this because she will never fly again, and will instead use the nutrients from her wings to survive during the first critical weeks, until the first generation of [ants](## "formiche") — all female workers — are ready to [forage](## "cercare cibo") for food. The only male [ants](## "formiche") ever produced in a colony are the [drones](## "fuchi"). After they have served their reproductive [purpose](## "scopo") and the nuptial flight is over, they live for another day or two, and then die.

## BENEFICIAL IMPACT
Although Flying Ant Day is most famously observed in the UK, similar events happen all over the world. They often involve different species of [ants](## "formiche") and take place at different times of the year, depending on the local cli[mate](## "riprodursi").
Although many find [swarms](## "sciami") of flying [ants](## "formiche") a [nuisance](## "fastidio"), they are [harmless](## "innocui") to humans and play an important ecological role. They provide a food source for birds and other predators, and the tunnelling and foraging activities of the queens’ colonies help improve [soil](## "suolo, terreno") quality. So Flying Ant Day is something for us all to celebrate!
