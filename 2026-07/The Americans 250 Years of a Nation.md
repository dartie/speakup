Defining a national identity is not easy. In the case of the United States of America, the question is also an interesting one because American identity is mainly ideological and cultural [rather than](## "piuttosto che, anziché") ethnic. Being American means accepting fundamental constitutional principles developed over the past 250 years, principles which have shaped the nation’s identity to a significant degree.

## FOUNDING CREED
These principles, which became America’s [founding creed](## "credo fondante"), originated in three 18th-century documents: the Declaration of Independence (1776), the Constitution (1787) and the [Bill of Rights](## "dichiarazione dei diritti") (1791). The Declaration’s famous words, “all men are created equal… with certain una[lie](## "giacere, stare")nable Rights, that among these are Life, Liberty and the [pursuit of Happiness](## "ricerca della felicità")”, would both guide the country and help form the character of its people. The iconic opening phrase of the Constitution’s preamble, “We the People”, signified that government power in the new nation originated with its citizens. This concept is central to American democracy.
Key figures who have contributed to the promotion of American identity: recording artist Elvis Presley; boxer Muhammad Ali; and pilot Amelia Earhart.

## NATION OF IMMIGRANTS
Also fundamental to American identity is the vision of the US as a nation of immigrants, with people from all over the world journeying to the country in search of a better life. America has been built by immigrants. First were the group known as American Indians (or Native Americans), who crossed the Bering [Strait](## "stretto") to Alaska and migrated down the West Coast thousands of years ago. Two centuries after the 17th-century [settlers](## "coloni") from Britain, Germany and Holland arrived, waves of immigrants from across Europe followed. In the last sixty years people from Latin America and Asia have also settled in the country.

## DIVERSE POPULATION
As a result, the US has a racially and ethnically diverse population, which now numbers around 342 million. An approximate [breakdown](## "suddivisione, analisi") gives us: White: 59 per cent; Hispanic or Latino: 20 per cent; Black: 13 per cent; Asian: 6 per cent; and Native American: 1.5 per cent. This diversity is an essential part of the American identity. The fusion of cultures, usually described as a ‘[melting pot](## "miscuglio di culture (lett. calderone)")’, has created a very [distinct](## "distinte, diverse") US culture. In recent years some commentators have begun referring to a ‘salad bowl’; a [blend](## "miscela") of American cultures and identities that remain [distinct](## "distinte, diverse") yet create a diverse national identity.

## COMMON BELIEFS
Freedom, equality and democracy are common be[lie](## "giacere, stare")fs in this ‘[melting pot](## "miscuglio di culture (lett. calderone)")’ or ‘salad bowl’. The First [Amendment](## "emendamento") (part of the [Bill of Rights](## "dichiarazione dei diritti")) guarantees [freedom of speech](## "libertà di parola"), religion, press, assembly, and petition. Americans value the idea of a political community in which people have reciprocal responsibilities. They have a strong be[lie](## "giacere, stare")f in the idea of America and participating in democratic processes at every level. In these common be[lie](## "giacere, stare")fs, however, [lie](## "giacere, stare") the worst [stain](## "macchia") on America’s past. Slavery, often called “America’s [original sin](## "peccato originale")”, lasted approximately 246 years and is the foundational moral [failing](## "fallimento") of the country. Americans’ historical murderous treatment of the Indigenous Peoples is also a source of [shame](## "vergogna") for many.

## THE AMERICAN DREAM
Another concept fundamental to the American identity is an idea famous all over the world: the ‘American Dream’, which originally represented the idealism of the American experiment, as the creation of the United States of America is often called, has over time come to be associated with [upward mobility](## "ascesa sociale"). This is the idea that, through hard work and determination, anyone can succeed, [regardless](## "indipendentemente") of their [background](## "origine, provenienza") or circumstances.

## THE AMERICAN CHARACTER
The American identity inevitably includes aspects of personality. But what exactly is the American character? Generalising in this minefield of potential clichés, we can say that it consists of a number of [traits](## "caratteristiche"). Individualism — personal freedom and [self-reliance](## "autosufficienza") — is fundamental, supported by optimism, hard work, and an entrepreneurial spirit. Americans are passionate about their country’s potential and have a strong sense of patriotism. American flags are [ubiquitous](## "onnipresenti"), adorning homes, gardens, buildings and even clothing. Consumerism is equally important, a key focus of American life. Consumerism reaches its peak every year on Black Friday, with record online spending last year of $11.8 billion.

## A LOVE OF SPORTS
Another [binding](## "unificante") element is a passionate love of sports, especially baseball, basketball and American football (or just 'football' for them.) One influential social commentator recently described American football as “the most important single thing” in the US, acting as a unifying idea of the country and what it [stands for](## "rappresentare, difendere"). Ninety-three of the one hundred most-watched TV programmes in 2023 were football games. The most-watched game had 57.2 million viewers, about 16 per cent of the population!

## WORLD POLICE
There is also a geopolitical element to being American: for eighty years, since the end of the Second World War in 1945, America has acted as the dominant global power, leading the rules-based international order and often behaving as the world’s policeman, intervening militarily and politically to (in theory) maintain order and promote democracy.

## A RADICAL MOMENT
As they celebrate 250 years as a country, Americans’ idea of themselves is evolving at [breakneck speed](## "a tutta velocità") as the nation experiences its most radical moment since the Civil War. Americans’ core be[lie](## "giacere, stare")fs are under real pressure for the first time in history because of a brutal [push for change](## "spinta al cambiamento") coming from the Government. Added to this, political polarisation, which began in the 1970s, is now becoming more and more [bitter](## "aspra, dura").
The country is also very unbalanced economically: the bosses of major companies are now earning up to three hundred times the salary of a typical worker, an enormous jump from the 1960s’ figure of about 21-to-1. America’s famous social mobility is also in trouble. In 2020, the US was only 27th in the Global Social Mobility Index. Be[lie](## "giacere, stare")f in the [fabled](## "leggendario, mitico") American Dream is also decreasing. In a January 2024 [poll](## "sondaggio"), only 27 per cent be[lie](## "giacere, stare")ved it was still true (down from 50 per cent in 2010), with 69 per cent disagreeing. Finally, immigration, a key part of America’s history, has now become a cause of violent disagreement, with thousands of people being arrested and deported in chains. Being American is now, sadly, a source of both pride and pain.
