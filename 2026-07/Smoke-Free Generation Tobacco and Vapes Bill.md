Talk to any smoker and they will tell you that they wish they had never started. Smoking kills more than seven million people a year worldwide and puts a massive [burden](## "carico, peso") on healthcare systems. Governments have tried to [curb](## "frenare, ridurre") the habit with various measures over the years: information campaigns, indoor smoking restrictions, increased age limits, advertising [bans](## "divieti"), scary medical photos printed on packaging, but none have had a meaningful impact. Vaping, first introduced as a method to [wean people off](## "ridurre gradualmente") cigarettes, has people [hooked](## "dipendenti, assuefatte") just the same. The problem is addiction. Nicotine is said to be as hard to [quit](## "smettere, abbandonare") as heroin.

## BORN TO BE FREE
[in a bid](## "nel tentativo di") to stop the smoking habit [once and for all](## "una volta per tutte"), the UK has taken a drastic measure. In April 2026, Parliament [passed](## "approvare") the Tobacco and Vapes [Bill](## "legge"), making it illegal for anyone born on or after 1 January 2009 to [purchase](## "acquistare") tobacco products. Smoking or vaping will not become illegal, however. The law only [bans](## "divieti") the sale of these products to people born after 2008 — in other words, anyone aged seventeen or less today. Vaping is subject to an advertising ban, a new tax on vaping liquids, and restrictions on flavours and packaging designed to appeal to youth. This [landmark](## "storica") legislation, which will take effect on 1 January 2027, [aims to](## "avere come obiettivo, mirare a") create a smoke-free generation.

## MALDIVES FIRST
The UK is not the first to introduce a generational smoking ban. The Maldives did it last year and it is even stricter there. People born after January 2007 are not only prohibited from buying tobacco products; they are also not allowed to have them or use them. [Retailers](## "rivenditori") selling to this group are fined 50,000 rufiyaa (€2,767). New Zealand also made an attempt at legislation in 2022. The ban was [enacted](## "promulgare") but [repealed](## "abrogare") soon afterwards under a new government, following criticism from retailers and the tobacco industry.

## WILL IT WORK?
Will the new law break the cycle of addiction? Wes Streeting, the UK Secretary of State for Health and Social Care, said it is all about prevention: “For too long, smoking has [claimed lives](## "causare morti, mietere vittime"), widened inequalities and placed avoidable pressure on our health services. This law changes that trajectory — protecting young people from ever starting, while [backing](## "sostenere") current smokers with the support they need to [quit](## "smettere, abbandonare") [for good](## "per sempre").” The UK now faces the complications of enforcement and the [backlash](## "reazione negativa") from the tobacco lobby. As the rest of the world watches, time will tell if this marks the beginning of the end for smoking.
