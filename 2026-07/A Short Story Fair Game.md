# A Short Story: Fair Game   [B2]

Tra scommesse online e scherzi poco eleganti, Hans diventa una vera e propria celebrità. Qualcuno, però, lo ripagherà con la sua stessa moneta.

Hans tried to hide his resentment as he served another bottle of champagne to the table.
“[Cheers](## "brindisi, cin cin") to the biggest film [opening](## "lancio, prima") of the year,” said Ron Robson, the new Hollywood star who was eating at Starlight, the chicest restaurant in Malibu.
His beautiful girlfriend and a group of friends [cheered](## "esultare") in response and clinked their glasses.
“Oh my gosh,” exclaimed a teenage girl at a nearby table. “That’s Ron Robson, he’s sooo [cute](## "carino").”
“Hey sweetheart,” said Ron in response, obviously loving the attention.
“Ugh, I can’t believe this guy,” said Hans to his sister, Renée. The two of them were working at Starlight for the summer. And while Renée loved seeing the celebrities that frequented the restaurant, Hans was becoming more and more [resentful](## "risentito") of them. 
“What is your problem?” said Renée. “I think it’s cool that we get to see famous people in real life.”
“Oh why? Because we’re not [worthy](## "degni, meritevoli") to be in the same room as them?”
“Nooo, that’s not what I meant. I just don’t understand why you hate them so much.”
“Because they’re famous for five seconds and think they [rule](## "governare") the world.” 
Now Ron was taking a selfie with the ecstatic teenager.
[Disgusted](## "disgustato"), Hans retreated to the kitchen with his sister. “Come on, let’s take our ten-minute break.”
They went to the staff room, where Hans immediately checked his phone. 
“Oh, yes!” he said. “I won $58 on that Lakers game.”

A Short Story: The Lone Wolf
La storia di Wendy non è per nulla convenzionale: rimasta orfana da bambina, è stata cresciuta da un branco di lupi. Da allora, qualcosa in lei continua a riportarla verso quel mondo.
Talitha Linehan , Molly Malcolm
He had [bet](## "scommettere") $10 that the basketball team the Los Angeles Lakers would beat the Boston Celtics, and won. It was one of hundreds of [bet](## "scommettere")s he’d made on prediction markets, where anyone could [bet](## "scommettere") ‘yes’ or ‘no’ on the [outcome](## "risultato") of practically anything: whether a specific person or team would win a game, a reality TV show, or a political election; whether the stock market would go up or down the next day; whether the temperature in LA would exceed 110°F that summer… 
Hans was an avid player. He had accumulated thousands of followers by making small [bet](## "scommettere")s on a variety of topics, and even [coming up with](## "inventare, uscirsene") things for his followers to [bet](## "scommettere") on. He was trying to develop the capacity to predict more [accurately](## "accuratamente") so he could quit his job and live off his online [income](## "reddito, entrate"). 
“What should I [bet](## "scommettere") on next?” he asked.
Before Renée could respond, their boss, Bronco, entered the staff room. “I need you back at work in two minutes,” he said. “That film star has ordered mimosa for everyone at the restaurant.” 
“Ugh, I should [pour](## "versare") a mimosa all over Ron Robson,” said Hans, after Bronco left. And then he had an idea, a fantastic idea. “What would he do if I [pour](## "versare")ed a mimosa all over him? Would he [play it cool](## "mantenere la calma") or [lose his cool](## "perdere le staffe")?”
“What are you talking about?” asked Renée.
But Hans was already typing the question into the prediction market app. “Would Ron Robson [lose his cool](## "perdere le staffe") if someone [pour](## "versare")ed a glass of mimosa on him in public?”
Moments later, his followers started [bet](## "scommettere")ting ‘yes’ or ‘no’ on the question.
Hans [slipped his phone into](## "infilarsi") his shirt pocket to livestream what happened next. When he served the mimosas to Ron’s table, he [spilled](## "rovesciare") one all over the film star’s [lap](## "grembo").
Ron jumped up from the table and shouted a series of [expletives](## "parolacce") at Hans. “Are you stupid?” he shouted at him. “These pants are Armani.”
“I’m so sorry, sir,” said Hans. “I’ll go get you a towel.”
He retreated to the kitchen, where Bronco shouted at him.
“I’m sorry,” said Hans, again. “It was an accident,” he lied.
“Well, we’re going to have to pay for his [suit](## "abito"), and that’s coming out of your [paycheck](## "stipendio").”
Hans didn’t care. He’d caused Ron Robson to [lose his cool](## "perdere le staffe") in public. And he had livestreamed it to his followers, and made those who’d [bet](## "scommettere") ‘yes’ into winners. That was an epic move.
“I can’t believe you did that,” said Renée. “This is your job and he’s a real person. It’s not a game.”
“Actually, it is a game,” he said. “Everything is a game now. And Ron Robson is a public figure. So, that makes him [fair game](## "bersaglio legittimo").”
When Hans checked his online profile that night, his number of followers had exploded. Over the next few weeks, [whenever](## "ogni volta che") he served a celebrity at Starlight, he [pranked](## "fare uno scherzo") them for his followers, who [bet](## "scommettere") on their reaction. 
Eventually, Bronco [figured out](## "capire, scoprire") what he was doing and [fired](## "licenziare") him. But by then, Hans had accumulated enough followers to live off his online [income](## "reddito, entrate"). His ‘[fair game](## "bersaglio legittimo") pranks’ had gone viral. And now instead of working at places like Starlight, he was frequenting them – and being recognised by his fans.
“Oh my gosh, you’re Hans, the [fair game](## "bersaglio legittimo") pranker,” said a beautiful woman to him, as he was having lunch with his sister — now in her first year at university — at Starlight. 
“Yes, I am,” he said, [wrapping his arm around](## "cingere con un braccio") the woman. “You want a selfie?”
“No, loser,” she said, [grabbing](## "afferrare") the bottle of champagne from the table and [pour](## "versare")ing it all over him. “Why don’t you get a real job and stop [picking on](## "prendersela con") people who work for a living?”
“What the… Are you [insane](## "pazza")?”
It was only then that he recognized the woman, as the beautiful girlfriend who’d been with Ron Robson when he’d [pour](## "versare")ed mimosa on his [lap](## "grembo"). He saw that she was recording him and wondered if she’d gamified his reaction.
“I’m here with my sister,” he shouted at her, and then said to Renée. “Can you believe she just did that?”
He expected Renée to be furious but she just [shrugged](## "scrollare le spalle"). “Well, now that you’re a public figure, I guess you’re [fair game](## "bersaglio legittimo") too.”
