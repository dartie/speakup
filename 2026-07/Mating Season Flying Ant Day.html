Mating Season: Flying Ant Day [B1]

A luglio, le formiche sviluppano le ali per il loro “volo nuziale”, un rituale di accoppiamento fondamentale per la sopravvivenza della specie.

It’s that time of year again in the UK, when ants sprout wings and emerge from their colonies to mate. This natural phenomenon is known as Flying Ant Day, though the title is actually a misnomer, since flying ants emerge over several weeks, when the weather is warm and humid. During this period, swarms of flying ants, known as ‘alates’, invade homes and gardens as they participate in what’s known as the ‘nuptial flight’.

VIRGIN QUEENS AND DRONES

Flying Ant Day normally takes place in July and August in the UK, and mostly involves a species called the black garden ant, or Lasius niger. So are all black garden ants flying ants? No. Once a year, however, many of their colonies produce reproductive females, called virgin queens, and males, called drones, which develop wings. Flying ants from different colonies then emerge at the same time, during optimal weather conditions — hence the phenomenon known as Flying Ant Day. This synchronised emergence maximises their chance of mating with ants from other colonies, thus reducing inbreeding. And it offers them protection from predators, because predators cannot consume all the ants at once. A cross section of an ant colony.

HUNDREDS OF THOUSANDS

Queens are larger than drones, and the two are often seen flying together. The queen normally mates with more than one drone. Incredibly, this single nuptial flight enables her to produce fertilised eggs for the rest of her life — often hundreds of thousands of eggs over a lifespan of up to twenty or even thirty years. Once she’s finished mating, she chews off her own wings and then starts a new colony underground. She does this because she will never fly again, and will instead use the nutrients from her wings to survive during the first critical weeks, until the first generation of ants — all female workers — are ready to forage for food. The only male ants ever produced in a colony are the drones. After they have served their reproductive purpose and the nuptial flight is over, they live for another day or two, and then die. Culture

BENEFICIAL IMPACT

Although Flying Ant Day is most famously observed in the UK, similar events happen all over the world. They often involve different species of ants and take place at different times of the year, depending on the local climate. Although many find swarms of flying ants a nuisance, they are harmless to humans and play an important ecological role. They provide a food source for birds and other predators, and the tunnelling and foraging activities of the queens’ colonies help improve soil quality. So Flying Ant Day is something for us all to celebrate!
