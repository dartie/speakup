Henrietta Lovell is a woman on a mission. She is trying to persuade tea lovers to abandon cheap tea bags, in favour of high-quality [tea leaves](## "foglie di tè"). Lovell is the founder of The Rare Tea Company and author of Infused: Adventures in Tea. The book seduces readers with the stories behind this [beloved beverage](## "amata bevanda"), describing the regions where it is grown, the communities of growers and the chefs who have [embraced](## "adottare") its many flavours. Through these stories, she not only shares her love of tea, but also builds a vivid picture of its origins.

## THE RARE TEA COMPANY
Known as The Rare Tea Lady, Lovell travels the world to discover new varieties. Through her company, she sells “the world’s best [loose-leaf tea](## "tè in foglie sfuse") — responsibly, sustainably and [ethically sourced](## "di provenienza etica"), direct from farmers and their tea gardens.” She met with Speak Up to talk about her passion for tea, which started in childhood, when she spent her holidays with her [great aunt](## "prozia") in Scotland.
**Henrietta Lovell (English accent):** She would have a [tea trolley](## "carrellino da tè") in the afternoon, like a silver trolley with beautiful [cups and saucers](## "tazze e piattini") and a silver [teapot](## "teiera") and cake. And she would make a ritual of tea in the afternoon in the [drawing room](## "salotto"). She’d been to India, and this tea would be a leaf tea that came from India, and she would tell the stories about these exotic places and she’d tell me that tea came from China... And I became [infatuated](## "affascinata") by the idea of the adventure and the place and the stories and this very [grown-up](## "da adulti"), very different kind of liquid that came in a beautiful cup with beautiful [accoutrements](## "accessori"). But that wasn’t my daily experience. It wasn’t until I was older working in China that I had an experience of tea again, that made me remember, “Oh, there is this other world out there.” And maybe these early experiences [boosted](## "aumentare") my excitement to explore.
Lovell’s memoir Infused: Adventures in Tea.

## OOLONG EPIPHANY
Around [the turn of the millennium](## "all’inizio del millennio"), Lovell had a successful career in corporate finance. During a business trip in China, she was served a high-quality oolong tea. Its complex flavours deeply affected her. The experience was so profound that she left her job and started The Rare Tea Company.
**Henrietta Lovell:** I read about all these extraordinary tea [terroirs](## "terroir (luoghi di produzione)"), like you might read about wine in Spain or France, and think about these beautiful places to visit where they are great makers. And I started to go and visit these people with no language skills, with no telephone. This is before the [mobile] phone. I had a little bit of paper with something written on it and a crazy [naivety](## "ingenuità"). And I visited places and fell in love with the place and the people and the makers, and I found that they still existed, these [ancient crafts](## "mestieri antichi"), and there was this world of really great craftsmen and makers making extraordinary elixirs that would [flood](## "inondare") your life with pleasure, like nothing in comparison with an industrial tea bag in a [mug](## "tazza grande"). And I thought, “I will make a company bringing these teas back to Britain and then eventually to the world.”

## TEA LIKE WINE
Lovell would like tea to be treated like [fine wine](## "vino pregiato"), with the same appreciation for the wide variety in taste, price, and quality. An [inexpensive](## "economico") wine is chosen for a normal [week-night dinner](## "cena in settimana"); a more select choice is made for a special occasion — so it should be for tea. Moreover, she would like tea to be known by its origins, just as wine is chosen from specific regions.
**Henrietta Lovell:** I would love to open the world to that possibility for tea as well, because there are makers behind it. It’s not just coming from a supermarket [shelf](## "scaffale") in a box. It’s coming from real communities, real people — fifteen million people in the world. And if we don’t [take notice](## "prestare attenzione") of them and [regard](## "apprezzare, rispettare") their craft and their skill, sometimes just the [middlemen](## "intermediari") take all the money and they end up living in poverty. This happens in chocolate, it happens in coffee, and it happens in tea because they’re very far away, marginalised communities, rural communities without power or access, and people exploit them, have exploited them traditionally.

## TEA FROM YORKSHIRE?
Consumers often do not know where their tea comes from — or who makes it. According to Lovell, this [lack](## "mancanza") of information is deliberate, to make us feel better about buying cheap tea bags. While most people believe that tea comes from China, Japan or India, one of the biggest tea-producing regions in the world is East Africa.
**Henrietta Lovell:** That’s a big surprise to people. In England, they might think it comes from Yorkshire before they think it comes from East Africa. And I think this has been hidden for a reason so that we don’t have to feel bad about poverty behind the cheap tea bag we drink on the [shelf](## "scaffale"). But when you know the effect of that cheap tea bag on the community that makes it, that they live in poverty so we can have this cheap bag, you start to think, “Is it really worth it? It’s cheap, yes, but is it worth the young woman in Malawi who’s [picking](## "raccogliere") tea who will maybe not [live to be out of](## "vivere oltre") her fifties? Is that worth it, that she won’t see her grandchildren, that she won’t have enough to feed her family necessarily, that she might live in poverty so that I can have a cheap [tea] bag that is not the most delicious thing in the whole world.” And if I paid a few [pence](## "penny, centesimi") more, I could have this extraordinary experience, much less than I would pay in the High Street for a coffee.
Lovell talks to tea makers in Nepal. The Rare Tea Company sells six different teas and herbal infusions from Nepal, sourced directly from the high Himalayan Jun Chiyabari Tea Garden and from small growers in eastern Nepal, showcasing the country’s distinctive high‑mountain terroir and traditional tea‑making practices.

## A JOY AND A PRIVILEGE
Despite competing with huge multinationals, Lovell succeeds in finding direct routes to market for small farms in places like Nepal or Malawi. Her work takes her all over the world in search of extraordinary tea varieties.
**Henrietta Lovell:** Next week, [I’m on my way](## "mettersi in cammino, partire") to Bhutan. That’s my first time to visit a new collective of women farmers in Bhutan. And then I’m visiting a new farm in Nepal, which is also a collective of farmers. And then I will travel to the Himalayas in India, to Meghalaya, which is another beautiful farm that I’ve known a long time. And it’s actually a matriarchal society where, in this area, the women own the land, and this is a woman tea farmer who owns her land, which is quite rare, and which is really wonderful. So that is a joy and a privilege to go and meet them, spend time with them, understand them, tell their stories. I feel the biggest responsibility is to tell their stories to people around the world so that they can share the enjoyment. And, you know, I think once something of someone, they are fascinating to you and you want to support them.

## THE VALUE OF TEA
While a higher price can help create a more [equitable](## "equa") [supply chain](## "catena di approvvigionamento"), it does not follow that the most expensive tea is always better. Cost versus quality ultimately depends on your own [palate](## "palato").
**Henrietta Lovell:** Some people really love strong English breakfast tea, and that’s not necessarily the most expensive, but you can [trade up](## "passare a una versione superiore") within that and find things that are very, very complex and beautiful. And then there are very, very expensive, say, matcha or Gyokuro from Japan, that is really, really expensive, but partly its cost is because land is expensive in Japan and [labour](## "manodopera") is expensive in Japan. So its high, high value is to do with value systems and economic systems within that country. And a tea from Malawi might be as exquisite — very different, but as exquisite — and be more [affordable](## "accessibile") because land and [labour](## "manodopera") are not so expensive. So if you know that it’s coming from a farmer and they’re getting the value of it, then you know that the value is really intrinsic to the tea rather than the [branding](## "immagine di marca") and the [fancy tin](## "scatola di latta elegante"). Your own flavour profile, whatever you like, is right. And nobody can tell you, this is the best tea because it’s more expensive. Your mouth, your [palate](## "palato"), your flavour is absolutely valid and right.

## THE RARE TEA LADY’S FAVOURITE
With so many varieties available nowadays, making a selection can be difficult. As a professional tea [connoisseur](## "intenditrice"), Henrietta Lovell is the perfect person to consult. Does she have a personal favourite? It depends on different factors, she says, including the time of day.
**Henrietta Lovell:** I like to drink bed tea in the morning. I drink a very beautiful white tea. I really like this, because it’s very soft. It’s pH neutral. It’s very [gentle](## "delicato"). I have to kind of look for the flavour, rather than it [slamming](## "colpire con forza") me in the head. And I find that very meditative in the morning. It’s very elegant, soft, [grassy](## "erbaceo"), sweet, little bits of [apricot](## "albicocca")… And I’m, like, lying there, enjoying the aromas, waking up slowly. But then during the day, when my [palate](## "palato") is awake and alert and I’ve been eating, I will have something more robust. I’m really, really enjoying a b[lack](## "mancanza") tea from a place called Meghalaya at the moment, in India, ‘cloud tea’. We call it cloud because the area in the local dialect is called the [Abode of Clouds](## "dimora delle nuvole"). And so this is something I’m really enjoying in the afternoon, it has real dark chocolate, caramel notes. But it changes, you know, and also, you [fall in and out of love](## "innamorarsi e disinnamorarsi") with different things. I always love them, I’m never out of love. I have a [vast array](## "ampia varietà") of teas to choose from and I love them all. I would never choose a tea that I didn’t love.
