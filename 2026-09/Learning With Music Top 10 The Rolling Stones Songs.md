One of the greatest and most controversial rock and roll bands of all time, The Rolling Stones were formed in London in 1962 when singer Mick Jagger and guitarist Keith Richards [bonded over](## "creare un legame attraverso") a shared love of music. Taking their name from a song by blues legend [Muddy Waters](## "acque fangose"), and adding guitarist Brian Jones, bassist Bill Wyman and drummer Charlie Watts to the [line-up](## "formazione"), they [took the world by storm](## "avere un successo travolgente"), rivalled in popularity only by their musical [foils](## "opposti") The Beatles.  
Over six decades later, the band have recently [released](## "pubblicare") their 25th studio album and have [hinted](## "lasciare intendere") they may tour in 2027. Not bad for a band whose singer declared, aged thirty-two, “I’d rather be dead than singing Satisfaction when I’m forty-five.”
The Rolling Stones’ lyrics are mostly low level and repetitive, making them ideal for improving your English.

## 1. I WANNA BE YOUR MAN (1963)
Following the moderate success of their debut single, a cover of American rock-and-roll pioneer Chuck Berry’s Come On, the band was looking for new material. They [bumped into](## "imbattersi in") the already hugely successful songwriting pair John Lennon and Paul McCartney, who offered them I Wanna Be Your Man (which later appeared on the Beatles’ second album.) With simple and repetitive lyrics, using [slang](## "gergo") for ‘want to’ and the imperative ‘tell’, it became The Rolling Stones’ first Top 20 UK hit, and was the first song to ever be performed on the BBC’s then-nascent iconic [chart programme](## "programma dedicato alle classifiche musicali") Top of the Pops.
I wanna be your lover, baby
I wanna be your man
Tell me that you love me, baby
Tell me you understand
Mick Jagger performs during the Rolling Stones’ Voodoo Lounge Tour in 1994.

## 2. (I CAN’T GET NO) SATISFACTION (1965)
Mick Jagger and Keith Richards were by now writing most of the band’s music, and the [lead single](## "singolo di lancio") for their third album “Out of Our Heads” [propelled](## "catapultare") The Rolling Stones to global superstardom. Its distinctive guitar riff was famously written by Richards in his sleep, and Jagger quickly [came up with](## "inventare") lyrics expressing his frustration with “America, its advertising syndrome, the constant [barrage](## "raffica, valanga").” It became the band’s first US Number One, and their fourth in the UK. The title is an example of the incorrect — although frequent in [slang](## "gergo") — use of the double negative.
When I’m driving in my car
And a man talks on the radio
He’s telling me more and more
About some useless information
Supposed to [fire](## "accendere") my imagination
Entertainment

## 3. PAINT IT BLACK (1966)
The mid-sixties were a time of musical experimentation and, inspired by the Beatles’ use of a sitar, guitarist Brian Jones incorporated improvisations and progressions on the Indian instrument into this song about [grief](## "dolore") after the loss of a loved one. Mick Jagger described it as “this kind of Turkish song” and its easy lyrics are largely in the present simple. It gave the band another Number One on both sides of the Atlantic and sixty years later is, by far, The Rolling Stones’ most streamed song on Spotify.
I see a red door
And I want it painted black
No colours anymore
I want them to turn black

## 4. JUMPIN’ JACK FLASH (1968)
The late 1960s were a turbulent time for The Rolling Stones. Considered the bad boys of rock-and-roll juxtaposed with the Beatles’ [wholesome appeal](## "immagine pulita"), in 1967 Mick Jagger and Keith Richards were arrested for drug possession in a police [raid](## "retata") at the guitarist’s country [estate](## "tenuta"). They drove to Morocco to escape the press, but Brian Jones’ girlfriend, actress Anita Pallenberg, left him for Richards on the trip. Jones was then arrested for drugs in a separate incident. The band channelled the [turmoil](## "scompiglio") into Jumpin’ Jack Flash, a return to the blues using the past simple.
I was born in a [cross[fire](## "accendere") hurricane](## "fuoco incrociato")
And I [howled](## "ululare") at the morning [drivin’ rain](## "pioggia battente")
But it’s all right now, in fact it’s a gas
But it’s all right, I’m Jumpin’ Jack Flash[It’s a gas](## "è uno spasso"), gas, gas

## 5. SYMPATHY FOR THE DEVIL (1969)
With guitarist Brian Jones becoming increasingly [unreliable](## "inaffidabile"), the band [dismissed](## "licenziare") him in June 1969. He was found dead in his swimming pool three weeks later. A huge free outdoor concert in London’s Hyde Park on 5 July, intended to introduce replacement Mick Taylor, became a memorial. It concluded with an eighteen-minute [rendition](## "interpretazione") of Sympathy for the Devil, an [opus](## "composizione") inspired by Brazilian folklore and rhythms. Narrated by the devil it looks at horrific events in history, ranging from the Bible to the JFK assassination. The lyrics are rich in vocabulary and were also inspired by French and Russian literature.  
Please allow me to introduce myself
I’m a man of wealth and taste
I’ve been around for a long, long year
Stole many a man’s soul and faith

## 6. GIMME SHELTER (1969)
[In late](## "a fine") 1969 The Rolling Stones embarked on a legendary tour of the US, described as “history’s first mythic rock and roll tour.” Tragedy [struck](## "colpire") on the final date in Altamont, California, where security was provided by the motorcycle gang Hells Angels. With the band [in full flow](## "nel suo miglior momento"), a man was fatally [stabbed](## "pugnalare") by one of the gang, immortalised in the 1970 documentary Gimme [Shelter](## "rifugio, riparo"). The tension-filled title track, from the album “Let it [Bleed](## "sanguinare")”, features soul singer Merry Clayton and opens with an example of the first conditional.
Oh, a storm is threatening
My very life today
If I don’t get some shelter
Oh yeah, I’m gonna [fade away](## "svanire")

## 7. ANGIE (1973)
At the peak of their creativity, The Rolling Stones [released](## "pubblicare") the now-classic albums “Sticky Fingers” and “Exile On Main Street” in the early 1970s — [the latter](## "quest’ultimo") was partly recorded in [debauched](## "dissoluto") tax-exile in the south of France. The band [decamped](## "trasferirsi") to Jamaica in late 1972 to record their eleventh album “Goats Head Soup”, according to Keith Richards because “it was one of the few places that would let us all in!” The first single was the acoustic ballad Angie, possibly named for Richards’ daughter, featuring references to Mick Jagger’s break up with singer Marianne Faithfull. The easy lyrics use information questions and the modal ‘can’t’.
Angie, Angie
When will those clouds all disappear?
Angie, Angie
Where will it lead us from here?
With no lovin’ in our souls
And no money in our coats
You can’t say we’re satisfied
Angie, Angie
You can’t say we never tried

## 8. MISS YOU (1978)
Long-term guitarist Mick Taylor left the band in 1974 and was replaced by Ronnie Wood. His first album as a full-time member was 1978’s “Some Girls”, recorded under the shadow of Keith Richards’ arrest for heroin possession in Canada. It was critically acclaimed after two less successful releases and became their bestselling album in the US. The opening track is a disco-infused jam, conceived with American keyboardist Billy Preston (who also featured on the Beatles’ Get Back.) A dance classic, with bassist Billy Wyman’s riff punctuating Jagger’s lyrics in the present perfect, this was The Rolling Stones’ eighth and final US Number One. 
I’ve been [holdin’ out](## "resistere") so long
I’ve been sleepin’ all alone
Lord, I miss you
I’ve been hanging on the phone
I’ve been sleeping all alone
I want to kiss you sometime

## 9. START ME UP (1981)
Keith Richards’ explosive guitar riff of [Start Me Up](## "mettere in moto") is now a staple opener of Rolling Stones concerts. The song’s 1981 release coincided with the launch of MTV and was one of the most-played videos in the early days of the music channel. First conceived as a reggae track six years earlier, the band reworked it to make it one of rock’s most recognisable songs and, in an early example of corporate collaboration, it was used in a 1995 Microsoft advertising campaign. The easy lyrics open with a perfect example of the first conditional.
If you start me up
If you start me up I’ll never stop

## 10. LIVING IN A GHOST TOWN (2020)
The band [drifted apart](## "allontanarsi") for brief periods in the next decade, but returned to break touring records in 1990, 1995, and 2006, while continuing to record together (although bassist Bill Wyman left in 1993.) Drummer and founding member Charlie Watts [passed away](## "morire") in 2021, and the final track the full band recorded together was [released](## "pubblicare") during the COVID-19 pandemic. Jagger originally wrote the song about a ghost after a plague but updated the lyrics to reflect the times. 
Life was so beautiful
Then we all got locked down
Feel a like ghost
Living in a ghost town, yeah
