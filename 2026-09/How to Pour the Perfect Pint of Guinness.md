[Pouring](## "versare") a perfect pint of Guinness follows a special ritual known as the ‘two-part pour’.
First, use a Guinness pint glass and hold it at a 45° angle under the beer [tap](## "rubinetto"). Pour the Guinness slowly as the liquid rises, then gradually [straighten](## "raddrizzare") the glass [upright](## "verticale") until it reaches the harp logo on the glass (about three-quarters full.)
Then place the glass on the bar and leave it to rest for 119.5 seconds. During this time, the famous [surge](## "riversarsi") and [settle](## "assestarsi") occurs: tiny [bubbles](## "bolle") move down the sides and rise through the middle, creating a dark body and a creamy white head.
This method matters because Guinness uses a [blend](## "mix") of nitrogen and CO₂ rather than CO₂ alone as most beers do, which produces smaller, finer [bubbles](## "bolle"). The pause allows these [bubbles](## "bolle") to [settle](## "assestarsi") properly, giving the drink its characteristic [thick](## "densa"), [velvety](## "vellutata") texture.

In the second part, hold the glass straight under the [tap](## "rubinetto") and carefully fill it to the top, pushing the [tap](## "rubinetto") handle away from you — never towards you, as with a standard [lager](## "birra chiara") [tap](## "rubinetto"). The [foam](## "schiuma") should form a smooth, slightly [domed head](## "testa bombata") sitting just above the [rim](## "bordo del bicchiere").
Finally, serve the pint with the harp logo on the glass facing the drinker.

# Glossary
* **Pouring** = versare
* **tap** = rubinetto
* **upright** = verticale
* **surge** = riversarsi
* **settle** = assestarsi
* **thick** = densa
* **velvety** = vellutata
* **foam** = schiuma
* **straighten** = raddrizzare
* **bubbles** = bolle
* **blend** = mix
* **lager** = birra chiara
* **domed head** = testa bombata
* **rim** = bordo del bicchiere
