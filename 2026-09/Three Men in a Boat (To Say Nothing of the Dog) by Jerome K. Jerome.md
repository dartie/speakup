# "Three Men in a Boat (To Say Nothing of the Dog)" by Jerome K. Jerome   [B2]

In questo divertente racconto di viaggio, l’autore, due amici e un cane risalgono il Tamigi in una barca a remi da Londra fino a Oxford. È considerato un classico dell’umorismo inglese.

Jerome K. Jerome’s [account](## "racconto, resoconto") of a two-week boating holiday on the River Thames is as [insightful](## "perspicace") and [delightful](## "piacevole") as it was when it was published in 1889. The book was originally intended to be a serious travel guide, but instead became a [timeless](## "intramontabile") comic reflection on the human condition. 

## OVERWORKED
At the start, J., the narrator, and two friends, George and Harris, discuss their bad health. After reading a medical text in the British Museum, J. diagnoses himself with almost every illness it contains. The three friends agree that “[overworking](## "lavorare troppo")” must be the problem, and decide to take a boat trip. First, though, they must [pack](## "preparare i bagagli"). J. is philosophical about it:
“Let your boat of life be light, [pack](## "preparare i bagagli")ed with only what you need — a homely home and simple pleasures, one or two friends worth the name, someone to love and someone to love you, a cat, a dog, and a pipe or two, enough to eat and enough to wear, and a little more than enough to drink; for thirst is a dangerous thing.” 
“Lascia che la tua barca della vita sia leggera, carica soltanto di quel che ti occorre: una casa accogliente e semplici piaceri, uno o due amici degni di tal nome, qualcuno da amare e qualcuno che ti ami, un gatto, un cane, una o due pipe, quanto basta da mangiare e quanto basta da indossare, e un po’ più di quello che è sufficiente da bere; poiché la sete è una cosa pericolosa.”

## ROMANTIC
In the morning, the men (and a dog, Montmorency) [head off](## "partire, mettersi in viaggio"). Happy to be out of the City of London, J. decides that people should embrace sensory pleasures rather than choose an austere life. He expresses this in a pretentious Romantic style: 
“A grim fraternity, passing grim lives in that sweet spot, that God had made so bright! Strange that Nature’s voices all around them — the soft singing of the waters, the whisperings of the river grass, the music of the rushing wind — should not have taught them a truer meaning of life than this.”
“Una tetra confraternita che viveva tetramente in quel luogo soave, reso così splendido da Dio! Strano che le voci della Natura tutto intorno a essi – il canto sommesso dell’acqua, i bisbigli delle erbe del fiume, la musica del vento frusciante – non avessero insegnato loro un più autentico significato della vita!”

## KINGSTON
J. imagines Kingston in its glory days, as a place where kings were crowned and  nobles and [courtiers](## "cortigiani") [wandered around](## "girovagare"). He [recalls](## "ricordare") a shop [lined](## "rivestire") in beautiful [oak wood](## "legno di quercia") that had been covered in [gaudy](## "vistosa, pacchiana") wallpaper. He reflects on the subjective value of things.
“ … what was sad in his case was that he, who didn’t care for carved oak, should have his drawing room panelled with it, while people who do care for it have to pay enormous prices to get it. It seems to be the rule of this world. Each person has what he doesn’t want, and other people have what he does want …”
“No, l’aspetto malinconico del caso in questione consisteva nel fatto che il proprietario del negozio, il quale si infischiava della quercia scolpita, aveva il salotto completamente rivestito da pannelli intagliati, mentre le persone che tengono a queste cose devono sborsare somme enormi per ottenerle. Ma, a quanto pare, questa è la legge che governa il mondo, ciascuno di noi ha quello che non desidera e gli altri hanno invece tutto quello che lui desidererebbe avere.”

## MAGNA CARTA
The men and their dog arrive at an island, said to be the location where King John signed the Magna Carta in 1215. J. imagines himself in the historical scene:
“Slowly against the swift current they work their ponderous way, till, with a low grumble, they grate against the bank of the little island […] And King John has stepped upon the shore, and we wait in the breathless silence till a great shout cleaves the air and the great cornerstone in England’s temple of liberty has, now we know, been firmly laid.” 
“Adagio, ma poderosamente, procedono contro la rapida corrente, finché, con uno stridore sommesso, raschiano contro la riva dell’isoletta [...] “E Re Giovanni è sbarcato, e noi aspettiamo in silenzio, con il fiato sospeso, finché un grande urlo lacera l’aria, poiché la grande pietra angolare del tempio della libertà inglese è stata, ora lo sappiamo, saldamente collocata.”
After lunch, the boat passes by Maidenhead, which J. dismisses as “too [snobby](## "snob") to be pleasant.” 

## OXFORD
In Reading, they see the floating [corpse](## "cadavere") of a woman in the river. They learn that she had committed suicide after a scandalous affair. 
Finally they reach their destination, Oxford, where it begins to rain non-stop. They try to sing songs to [raise their spirits](## "tirare su di morale"), but are soon all [overcome](## "essere sopraffatto, soccombere").
“Mother Earth looks at us with such dull, soulless eyes, when the sunlight has died away from out of her. […] she does not seem to know us or care for us. […] Harris sobbed like a little child, and the dog howled till I thought his heart or his jaw must surely break.”
“La madre Terra ci contempla con occhi così spenti e senz’anima [...] sembra che non ci conosca più e non si curi più di noi [...] Harris singhiozzò come un bimbetto e Montmorency ululò fino a farmi temere che potessero spezzarglisi il cuore o le mascelle.”
The men decide to [cut their trip short](## "accorciare, interrompere") and “for the good of their health” take the five o’clock train back to London, where they feel instantly at home.
There have been many stage and radio dramatisations of Three Men in a Boat. Film adaptations include a 1920 silent movie and a 1975 film directed by Stephen Frears.
