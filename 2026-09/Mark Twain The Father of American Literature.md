Mark Twain was born Samuel Langhorne Clemens on 30 November 1835. Considered the father of American literature, his [masterful](## "magistrale") use of vernacular speech, [biting](## "pungente") [wit](## "arguzia"), ferocious social satire, and deep understanding of human nature revolutionised the world of fiction in the US. His was the critical voice of a rapidly-changing nation in a period he himself called The [Gilded Age](## "Età dell’oro"). 

## MISSISSIPPI AS A SYMBOL
Born in Missouri in the village of Florida, Twain grew up in the [bustling](## "vivace") town of Hannibal on the Mississippi River. [Steamboats](## "battelli a vapore"), a slave-owning society, and the adventures of childhood along the [riverbanks](## "rive del fiume") would colour his imagination indelibly. Twain hated school and dreamt of becoming a [riverboat](## "battello fluviale") pilot. His first job, however, was as a printer’s apprentice and [typesetter](## "tipografo"), which “helped him [figure out](## "scoprire, capire") how words go together.” Finally, after a two-year training period, he became a Mississippi River pilot. The Mississippi was the central symbol of his literary work. The main artery of the slavery business: one million men, women and children were transported down the river for sale on steamboats from 1820 to 1860. 
Mark Twain in 1910.

## ACCLAIM AS A WRITER
When the Civil War [broke out](## "scoppiare") in 1861, Twain served briefly as a Confederate soldier. He then [headed](## "dirigersi") west in the 1861 [Gold Rush](## "corsa all’oro"), before working as a miner and finally a journalist. With writing, he found his calling. He adopted the [pen name](## "pseudonimo") Mark Twain, [riverboat](## "battello fluviale") [slang](## "gergo") meaning ‘two [fathoms](## "braccia (misura nautica)") of safe water’.

His career [breakthrough](## "svolta") came in 1865 [wit](## "arguzia")h a short story, The Celebrated Jumping [Frog](## "rana") of Calaveras County, about a man who loved to [bet](## "scommettere") on animals. It established his reputation as a master storyteller [wit](## "arguzia")h a [gift](## "talento") for regional accents. More fame came [wit](## "arguzia")h two travel books, The Innocents Abroad (1869) and [Roughing It](## "titolo italiano "In cerca di guai"") (1872). Twain used funny, irreverent observation to [mock](## "deridere") the [stuffiness](## "rigidità") of old European culture and the ignorance of American tourists. 


>A powerful agent is the right word. [whenever](## "ogni volta che") we [come upon](## "imbattersi") one of those intensely right words in a book or a newspaper the resulting effect is physical as well as spiritual, and electrically [prompt](## "immediato").




## THE GREAT AMERICAN NOVEL
Twain then published the two novels which would make him famous. In The Adventures of Tom Sawyer (1876), a [coming-of-age tale](## "racconto di formazione"), he captured the nostalgic, romantic, [light-hearted](## "spensierato") spirit, and imagination of his boyhood. Tom is a [mischievous](## "birichino") but good-hearted orphan who shares adventures [wit](## "arguzia")h his best friend, Huckleberry Finn. Its sequel, Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (1884) [cemented](## "consolidare") Twain’s place in literary history. A [runaway](## "fuggitivo") boy and an escaped slave travel down the Mississippi, looking for freedom, in a novel [tackling](## "affrontare") the [harsh](## "dura") reality of America’s [original sin](## "peccato originale"), slavery.  

## TRAGEDIES
Twain regularly invested in new technology. Disaster [struck](## "colpire") in 1894, when a failed investment in the Paige Compositor, a complex [typesetting machine](## "macchina per la composizione tipografica"), left him [bankrupt](## "bancarotta"). Suffering from depression and anxiety, he decided to [pay off](## "saldare") his debts [wit](## "arguzia")h a worldwide [lecture](## "conferenza") tour in 1895-96. Delighting audiences [wit](## "arguzia")h his quick [wit](## "arguzia") and eloquence, he became an international celebrity. He returned to the US [wit](## "arguzia")h his wealth and public reputation [restored](## "ristabilire").


>Always do right. This will gratify some people, and [astonish](## "stupire") the rest.



In his private life, however, Twain suffered a series of family tragedies. He and his wife Livy lost their son, Langdon, at just nineteen months, in 1872. Susy, his favourite daughter, died aged twenty-four in 1896 from spinal meningitis. His wife died in 1904, followed by their youngest daughter Jean, an epileptic, in 1909. Only Clara, his middle daughter, [outlived](## "sopravvivere a") him.

## AN IMMEASURABLE IMPACT
These terrible events, together [wit](## "arguzia")h disillusionment regarding human nature, imperialism, religion, and institutional [greed](## "avidità"), filled the writer [wit](## "arguzia")h [bitter anger](## "profonda rabbia"). His final works were dark and cynical. He died just four months after Jean.
Twain’s impact on American literature and culture is immeasurable. Ernest Hemingway famously said that “all modern American literature comes from Huckleberry Finn.” Twain was the first major American author to successfully use vernacular language. He democratised American literature by demonstrating that regional voices could describe and confront the country’s most serious moral [issues](## "questioni"). He also entertained millions, using his wonderful sense of humour as a weapon against injustice.

## HUCKLEBERRY FINN
Before this novel, American authors had written in formal English, copying European styles. Breaking the mould, Twain’s story is narrated through the voice, faithfully reproduced, of an uneducated [backwoods](## "di una zona rurale") boy.
Social [outcast](## "reietto") Huckleberry Finn is living [wit](## "arguzia")h the wealthy Widow Douglas, who is trying to civilise him, against his wishes. [Kidnapped](## "rapire") by his abusive, alcoholic father, Huckleberry manages to escape to an island on the Mississippi River, where he meets Jim, a [runaway](## "fuggitivo") slave. The [outcast](## "reietto")’s conscience is divided, as society says that helping a [runaway](## "fuggitivo") slave is a sin, but he wants to help. In a famous line, Huckleberry says, “All right, then, I’ll go to hell!”, as he decides to help Jim escape down the river to freedom. Our anti-hero grows into a hero, choosing individual morality and human loyalty over the hypocrisy of a corrupt society. Confronting America’s [original sin](## "peccato originale"), slavery and systemic racism, Twain forces Americans to confront themselves.
Cover from the 1885 original edition of Mark Twain’s Adventures of Huckleberry Finn [wit](## "arguzia")h illustrations by E. W. Kemble; Eddie Hodges played Huckleberry Finn in the 1960 series.
Huckleberry Finn is one of the most criticised, [banned](## "vietare") books in history. During the time of the Civil Rights movement in the US, in the 1950s and 1960s, [concerns were raised](## "suscitare preoccupazioni") about the book, especially its language. The racial [slur](## "insulto") ‘nigger’ appears 219 times. [Scholars](## "esperti") say Twain used it to portray the reality of the pre-Civil War American South, but others say the book is simply racist, [wit](## "arguzia")h the [slur](## "insulto") used even by the story’s hero.

## A FIGHTER AGAINST INJUSTICE
Mark Twain fought against slavery all his life. He was shocked when his own parents ordered the [whipping](## "fustigazione") of Jennie, the black slave who had [nursed](## "accudire") him and his [siblings](## "fratelli e sorelle"), for insubordination. The “woman was almost like one of the family”, Twain wrote. Jennie then demanded to be sold. 
Twain’s attitude towards American Indians changed from racist prejudice during his time in the West to later empathy and anti-imperialist anger as he recognised the terrible impact of European and American colonisation. Twain’s friend William Dean Howells described the writer as having “an indignant sense of right and wrong, a [scorn](## "disprezzo") of all affectation and [pretence](## "finzione"), an ardent hate of [meanness](## "meschinità") and injustice.”
The writer loved modern technology and inventions. He was one of the first Americans to have a telephone at home. The inventor Thomas Edison visited Twain at his Connecticut home in 1909 and made the only known film of the writer, dressed in his iconic white [suit](## "completo"), [wit](## "arguzia")h his daughters Clara and Jean. He adored his family, and the deaths of his wife and three of his children almost destroyed him. His wife Livy (Olivia), the daughter of a New York [coal](## "carbone") baron, was fundamental to his success, brilliantly editing his work.
The Mark Twain House and Museum in Hartford, Connecticut.
The author’s [outsized](## "enorme") [persona](## "personaggio")lity helped to make him famous. He made his [unruly](## "ribelli") hair an important part of his public [persona](## "personaggio"), laughing at it at times. He delighted in publicity and was a friend to presidents, artists, industrialists and European royalty. But he was also a friend to his neighbours, especially the local farmers.
Twain was born just after the appearance of Halley’s Comet in 1835. He died one day after the comet was at its [closest](## "più vicino") to the sun in 1910. Predicting this coincidence, Twain himself wrote: “The [Almighty](## "l’Onnipotente") has said, no doubt: ‘Now here are these two [unaccountable freaks](## "stranezze inspiegabili"); they came in together, they must go out together.’”
