During the forty days of [Lent](## "Quaresima") (Sundays are excluded,) many people across the UK will be [giving something up](## "rinunciare a qualcosa, smettere"): alcohol, cigarettes, [carbs](## "carboidrati")… Abstinence during [Lent](## "Quaresima") was originally a Catholic tradition, and England, Wales, and Scotland haven’t officially been Catholic countries since the 1500s. In recent years, however, there seems to be a renewed interest in doing a springtime detox.

## A NEW START
The word ‘[Lent](## "Quaresima")’ comes from the Old German word for springtime and has the same etymological [roots](## "radici") as the word ‘[lengthen](## "allungare")’. It is the time of year when the days are getting longer. In the Christian calendar, the day before [Lent](## "Quaresima") starts is called [Shrove Tuesday](## "Martedì Grasso (to shrive: confessare)"). It was the day when Catholics would confess their [sins](## "peccati") before [Lent](## "Quaresima") began. Rich foods were [forbidden](## "vietare") during [Lent](## "Quaresima"), so [households](## "famiglie, case") would [use up](## "consumare, usare completamente") eggs, butter and milk by making pancakes on this day. It’s similar to the way that in Catholic countries Mardi Gras and carnival are an opportunity for a final indulgence before the restrictions of [Lent](## "Quaresima"). In recent times, across the UK, Ireland, Australia, Canada, and New Zealand, many people eat pancakes on [Shrove Tuesday](## "Martedì Grasso (to shrive: confessare)"), which is now celebrated as the secular festival of Pancake Day.

## SELF-DISCIPLINE
Historians generally agree that observing [Lent](## "Quaresima") became a shared Christian practice in 325 [CE](## "d.C. (Common Era)"). [Early on](## "all’inizio"), [Lent](## "Quaresima") centred on [fasting](## "digiuno"), often in the form of the Black Fast. This extreme [fasting](## "digiuno") allowed only one small meal after [sundown](## "tramonto, calar del sole") and excluded meat, [dairy](## "latticini"), alcohol, and sexual relations. The [aim](## "obiettivo, scopo") was to practice self-discipline and [reflect](## "riflettere") on how Jesus spent forty days and nights in the desert before the crucifixion. But during the Protestant Reformation, which began in Germany in 1517 and [spread](## "diffondersi, espandersi") across Northern Europe, many of the Catholic Church’s practices, including [fasting](## "digiuno") for [Lent](## "Quaresima"), were criticised or even [banned](## "vietare"). Though some Protestants continued to observe [Lent](## "Quaresima"), it was in a more moderate form.

## A MODERN LENT
Today, [Lent](## "Quaresima") often survives less as a religious obligation than as a secular personal challenge. People might give up sweets, meat, alcohol, or carbohydrates. Others try digital [fasting](## "digiuno"), sometimes called a ‘digital detox’, giving up or at least reducing social media, screen time and phone use. Wellness culture has [embraced](## "adottare, accogliere") [Lent](## "Quaresima") as an opportunity for detox or reset, with apps and clinics promoting it as a time to lose weight or build healthier habits. [Lent](## "Quaresima"), whether religious or secular, is still a season when less is more.
