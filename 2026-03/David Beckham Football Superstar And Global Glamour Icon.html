David Beckham is one of the most famous footballers in history. He is the only player to win league titles in four different countries: England, Spain, France and the US. His fame both on and off the pitch means that he has transcended sport to become a British cultural icon and a global superstar.

A PASSION FOR FOOTBALL

Beckham was passionate about football from childhood. Every weekend he would be in the local park training alone or playing with his father for hours. He joined Manchester United as a youth trainee in 1991 at age fourteen and became part of the legendary Class of ‘92, which included other future stars such as Ryan Giggs, Gary Neville, and Paul Scholes. Beckham made his league debut as a midfielder four years later. On 17 August 1996, in the first game of the season, against Wimbledon, he scored an astonishing goal from his own half. This ‘golden goal’ made Beckham a household name. “It kicked open the door to the rest of my life”, he said.

I never do anything half-heartedly. I will continue  to work hard and play hard and do everything I can to be successful, whatever I do.

His curling strikes were his trademark. A perfect example was his goal in extra time against Greece in 2002, a twenty-seven-metre curling free kick, in the decisive qualifying game that sent England to the World Cup in Japan and South Korea. FOOTBALL ROYALTY

Beckham’s global fame came not just from his footballing skills. An astute businessman, his name has become a trademark, worth millions in clothes lines and fragrances. He is considered a style icon and is always on the world’s best-dressed list. Part of his celebrity is due to his marriage in 1999 to Victoria Adams, Posh Spice from The Spice Girls, who is now a renowned fashion designer. The couple have made hundreds of millions from the retail and entertainment industries. In 2003, for example, Beckham signed a $160-million lifetime contract with Adidas. His embrace of the world of brand partnerships has made him a celebrity superstar. As a sports journalist put it, “His brand is global, his appeal universal.” World

INTERNATIONAL CAREER

David Beckham also had a stellar international career. He made his England debut in September 1996, aged twenty-one. He won 115 caps representing England and captained the side fifty-nine times over a period of six years, scoring seventeen goals. He appeared in three World Cups, in 1998, 2002, and 2006. But playing international football gave him the worst experience of his life. In the 1998 World Cup, playing against Argentina, he was fouled by Diego Simeone and retaliated by kicking out. Simeone “fell like a ton of bricks” (Sports Illustrated) and Beckham got a red card. Simeone later admitted that he was trying to get Beckham sent off by over-reacting. Britain’s tabloid press viciously attacked Beckham (the Daily Mirror printed a dartboard with his face), and the player received death threats from the public and bullets in the post. Victoria Beckham later said that her husband became “clinically depressed” after receiving the red card. 

WIDELY RESPECTED 

Unexpectedly modest, despite his achievements, Beckham once described his career with the sentence: “I just want people to see me as a hardworking footballer.” In his autobiography, Manchester United manager Sir Alex Ferguson was open with his praise: “From the moment he first laid boot on ball, David Beckham displayed an unbreakable urgerio”) to make the best of himself and his talent.” Beckham’s  colleague Nicky Butt said he was “as mentally tough as anybody you’ll ever meet.”  Beckham suffers from obsessive compulsive disorder, and he has to have everything at home (such as food products or clothes) in straight lines or in pairs. From his playing years, he has kept a thousand pairs of boots in storage! Memories mean a lot to the footballer. Beckham has supported charities most of his life. He has worked with UNICEF for twenty years as a Goodwill Ambassador, promoting and protecting the rights of vulnerable children around the world. In 2015 he launched the 7 Fund with UNICEF to give disadvantaged children the opportunity to reach their full potential. He says: “My charity work gives me the most pleasure out of anything that I do.”
