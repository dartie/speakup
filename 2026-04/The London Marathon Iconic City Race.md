The London Marathon is one of the most famous running events in the world. Every spring, [big crowds](## "grandi folle") [flock to](## "accorrere in massa") the streets of London to [cheer on](## "fare il tifo") the runners. Millions more watch the marathon on television. For many people it is more than a race, it is a celebration of community, charity and personal [achievement](## "risultato").

## OLYMPIC FOUNDATION
The first London marathon was held in 1981. It was founded by former Olympic athletes Chris Brasher and John Disley. Inspired by the New York City Marathon, they wanted to create an event that would [bring people together](## "riunire") and show London [at its best](## "al suo meglio"). It was open to elite athletes and everyday runners, which helped to give it a friendly and inclusive feel. Over the years, the 26.2-mile race has grown into one of the six most prestigious marathons in the world, [alongside](## "insieme a") Boston, London, New York, Berlin, Chicago, and Tokyo. Last year’s event had over 55,000 participants. Because demand is so high, most runners have to [enter a public ballot](## "partecipare a un sorteggio"), which is often heavily oversubscribed. The 2026 ballot had over a million applicants!

## CHARITY LINKS
One of the London Marathon’s greatest cultural contributions is its [link](## "legame") to charity. It is widely known as the world’s biggest one-day [fundraising](## "raccolta fondi") event. Runners support thousands of different causes, from medical research to local community groups. Many participants run in memory of loved ones, others [aim to](## "avere come obiettivo") [raise awareness](## "sensibilizzare") of issues that matter to them, and this charitable spirit is a big reason why the race is so loved by the public.

## A CELEBRATION OF DIVERSITY
The race also includes wheelchair athletes and [visually-impaired](## "con disabilità visiva") runners, highlighting its inclusive nature. [On a lighter note](## "parlando di cose più leggere"), many runners wear [fancy dress](## "travestimenti"), with [costumes](## "costumi") ranging from superheroes to [household objects](## "oggetti domestici"). Over the years, the marathon has seen dinosaurs, [slices of toast](## "fette di pane tostato"), and even [landmarks](## "monumenti") taking part.

## SCENIC COURSE
The course itself is one of the most [scenic](## "pittoreschi") in the world. It starts in south-east London, near Greenwich, close to the famous Cutty Sark. Runners then head along the River Thames, passing historic areas and modern city views. One memorable moment is crossing Tower Bridge around the halfway mark. Then, the route goes through the tall buildings of Canary Wharf, before turning back towards central London. The final [stretch](## "tratto") passes the Houses of Parliament and Big Ben, with the finish line on The Mall, near Buckingham Palace. This mix of history and modern life makes the London Marathon truly special.
