Every time you use your smartphone or drive an electric vehicle, you benefit from technological advancements made possible by rare-earth elements (REEs, or ‘[rare earths](## "terre rare")’). Also called “the vitamins of modern industry,” a phrase attributed to Soviet scientist Aleksandr Fersman, [rare earths](## "terre rare") have become one of nature’s most valuable [resources](## "risorse"). This is because of [soaring](## "crescente") demand for advanced electronics, renewable energy technology, and military and defence systems. All of these [harness](## "sfruttare") the unique magnetic, optical, and catalytic properties of these critical [mine](## "estrarre")rals.

## SOLAR PANELS AND MISSILES
So what are [rare earths](## "terre rare")? They are seventeen metallic elements: fifteen lanthanides, a closely related series of metals [running from lanthanum to](## "che vanno da… a…") lutetium, known for their similar chemical behaviour, and two soft, [silvery metals](## "metalli argentati") called Scandium and Yttrium. They are often divided into light [rare earths](## "terre rare") and heavy [rare earths](## "terre rare"). However, they all have almost identical chemical and physical properties, and are typically found in the same [mine](## "estrarre")ral deposits. This makes them extremely difficult to separate in their [raw](## "allo stato grezzo"), natural form.
Rare earths are used in the development of powerful magnets and are essential for modern technologies including consumer electronics, such as laptops or [headsets](## "auricolari, cuffie"); green energy applications, including solar panels and wind turbines; medical devices such as X-ray equipment and lasers; and military machines, such as missiles and [fighter jets](## "aerei da combattimento").

## NOT THAT RARE
The surprising thing about [rare earths](## "terre rare") is that they are not, in fact, [scarce](## "scarse, poco diffuse"). They are found worldwide in the [Earth’s crust](## "crosta terrestre"). However, because of the difficulty in separating them from each other, mining and refining them is extremely costly and complex. These extractive processes cause significant environmental damage, including habitat destruction, [waste](## "rifiuti") generation and pollution.
The race for REEs have [heightened tensions](## "forti tensioni") between China and the US, provided Russia with an incentive for invading Ukraine and have impacted Latin American countries, such as Colombia (above) and Venezuela.  

## CHINESE DOMINATION
An accurate predictor of future trends, China began investing in complex technologies to [mine](## "estrarre"), separate and process [rare earths](## "terre rare") in the 1980s. That is why today the country is the global leader in [rare earths](## "terre rare"), mining up to 70 per cent and processing and refining about 90 per cent of the world’s [supply](## "fornitura"). Naturally, other countries are trying to compete, by developing their own processing capabilities. And while China continues to dominate the market, the US, Myanmar and Australia have become major producers in recent years.
The global market value for products containing [rare earths](## "terre rare") is well over $1 [trillion](## "mille miliardi"). And despite the financial and environmental costs involved in their production, the demand for [rare earths](## "terre rare") is expected to grow exponentially over the next few years as our dependence on technology, and AI in particular, becomes ever more acute.

## GEOPOLITICS
Rare earths have long been a geopolitical [concern](## "preoccupazione"). They are behind aggressive actions, such as the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and the US push to obtain Greenland. Both Ukraine and Greenland are home to significant rare earth deposits. US rhetoric regarding acquisition of [mine](## "estrarre")ral rights has also led to [heightened tensions](## "forti tensioni") with Venezuela. Interest in the country is [tied](## "legare") to its oil sector and also to its [broader](## "più ampia") [mine](## "estrarre")ral wealth. [Reported occurrences](## "giacimenti segnalati") of critical [mine](## "estrarre")rals include [rare earth-bearing sands](## "sabbie contenenti terre rare") in the Orinoco Mining Arc. Because [rare earths](## "terre rare") are critical for magnets, sensors, lasers and guidance systems, the country that controls separation and processing [wields](## "esercitare") considerable economic power. China’s state-subsidised investment in solvent extraction and [midstream](## "nella fase intermedia") refining (highly-polluting processes) has created a [bottleneck](## "punto critico, ostacolo"); as China processes the vast majority of the world’s REEs, it can restrict [supply](## "fornitura") to [leverage](## "sfruttare") its advantage.
