Marilyn Monroe: A Self-Made Star [B2]

La National Portrait Gallery di Londra celebra i cento anni di una delle più grandi icone di Hollywood con un’esibizione speciale che invita ad andare oltre i cliché.

Marilyn Monroe remains one of the most iconic figures of the 20th century, immortalised as the mythical blonde bombshell of the 1950s and 60s. With her hourglass figure and effortless charm, she became one of the most photographed people in history, a glamorous incarnation of Hollywood’s golden age. Yet behind this dazzling image, Marilyn was far more than a mere sex symbol: she was also a visionary who played an active role in crafting her own legend, laying the ground for future stars such as Taylor Swift or Madonna.

THE SUMMER OF MARILYN

Leading cultural institutions around the world will be marking the 100th anniversary of her birth this year, a celebration which has been nicknamed the ‘Summer of Marilyn’. In London, The National Portrait Gallery will open a landmark exhibition, Marilyn Monroe: A Portrait. It promises to go beyond the clichés, exploring Monroe’s life and work through a fresh lens, one that highlights her pioneering creative agency and her enduring influence on pop culture. One of the most iconic photos of Marilyn Monroe taken by photographer Milton H. Greene’s in 1954. She wears a transparent ballerina satin dress, apparently borrowed from Greene’s friend.

TRAILBLAZER

Born Norma Jean Mortensen on 1 June 1926, Marilyn Monroe was not just a muse — she co-created her own persona. Aged just twenty, when she was signing a contract as an actress with Twentieth Century Fox, studio executive Ben Lyon suggested she take on a new stage name. Lyon decided on Marilyn, because she reminded him of Marilyn Miller, a Broadway musical star, while Jean decided on Monroe, another surname on her mother’s side. Unlike many stars of her era, she also actively collaborated with photographers like Richard Avedon, Eve Arnold or Milton Greene. She didn’t just pose for the camera; she performed for it, directed sessions and claimed the right to veto any images she didn’t like.

CREATIVE CONTROL

Her partnership with Milton Greene was particularly groundbreaking. Together, they founded Marilyn Monroe Productions in 1955, a bold move that gave her creative control over her projects and challenged Hollywood’s studio system. This venture wasn’t just about independence, it was a declaration that Monroe was more than just a star; she was a producer and a pioneer in an industry that often treated women as disposable icons.  GROUNDBREAKING

Marilyn Monroe: A Portrait will bring together previously unseen photographs, intimate portraits and works by era-defining artists such as Andy Warhol and photographer Richard Avedon. The exhibition will trace Monroe’s journey from her early pin-up days to her final, poignant photoshoots, revealing how she evolved as both a subject and a creative force. Visitors can expect to see iconic portraits that defined her public persona, from the breathless glamour of Gentlemen Prefer Blondes, the 1953 movie that transformed her from a promising actress into a global superstar, to the raw vulnerability captured in her later years.
