London’s Young V&A is a museum of creativity, play and design for children and young people aged 0-14. Located in Bethnal Green, in London’s East End, it reopened in July 2023 after a major three-year transformation that reimagined the former Museum of Childhood as an interactive space designed with input from children themselves.
Named the best children’s museum in Europe by the Council of Europe, the Young V&A is on its third major exhibition since reopening. Entitled Inside Aardman: Wallace & Gromit and Friends, the show opened during the 50th anniversary of Aardman Animations and offers a behind-the-scenes journey into the studio’s world-famous stop-motion [craft](## "maestria"). Based in Bristol, Aardman is the creator of beloved films and series such as Wallace & Gromit, Chicken Run and Shaun the Sheep. Speak Up spoke to Tanaya Basu De Sarkar about how the exhibition translates Aardman’s behind-the-scenes [craft](## "maestria") into an experience children can understand and explore.
**Tanaya Basu De Sarkar:**The Young V&A is a really good place for us to be doing this exhibition about Aardman because we’re able to look at the creative skills that go into making a stop-motion movie so that’s what we’re really celebrating in this exhibition. Aardman’s films have the most incredible technical skill and [artistry](## "arte, talento") that go into making them, and so for our young audience we’re [breaking down](## "scomporre") the steps of how you make a stop-motion film, how you make an animation. And what we’ve learned during this is that stop-motion is actually one of the most accessible forms of film-making, and certainly of making an animation that a person can do. All you need is a camera, something to move and editing software. So I think that’s why it’s a really good exhibition for our young audience because it’s a really accessible creative skill.

## INSIDE THE EXHIBITION
The exhibition includes more than 150 objects from Aardman’s archives, including original [puppets](## "pupazzi"), early character [sketches](## "schizzi"), sets and hand-drawn storyboards from films such as The Wrong Trousers, Chicken Run, Robin Robin and the earliest adventures featuring the characters Wallace and Gromit.
**Tanaya Basu De Sarkar:** Some of the highlights of the exhibition include a pirate ship which is nearly four metres long which was used in the film The Pirates! In an Adventure with Scientists!, which is an incredible example of a set model used, a and it’s... yeah, it’s definitely one of the [showstopper](## "pezzi forti") pieces in the exhibition. One thing that fans might not know is that in stop-motion the mouths of the characters, they will [swap out](## "sostituire") the mouth  they speak or make a single sound. And so we’ve got some examples of a box of mouths used for the pirate captain or for the farmer and Shaun the Sheep [on display](## "in mostra") and I think that’s a perfect example of just how much work goes into animating a simple sentence.

## INSPIRING YOUNG CREATORS
Young visitors can try storyboarding, sculpting characters, [lighting](## "illuminare") miniature sets and creating their own stop-motion clips, discovering step by step how Aardman transforms simple materials into [compelling](## "coinvolgente") storytelling. A [key aim](## "obiettivo principale") of the exhibition is to help young visitors feel confident enough to try stop-motion animation themselves. This confidence comes from understanding Aardman’s beginnings, but also from seeing what other children have already created and from having the chance to experiment first-hand.
