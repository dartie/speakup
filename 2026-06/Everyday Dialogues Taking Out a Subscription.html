Everyday Dialogues: Taking Out a Subscription [A2]

Questo breve dialogo telefonico è perfetto per ripassare espressioni utili legate alle richieste di informazioni, alle offerte e al linguaggio formale.

Luca: Hi, I’m calling to enquire about the subscription to Speak Up. Laura: Good morning! Thanks for your interest. Are you familiar with the magazine? Luca: Yes, I’ve seen it on the newsstands and my language school uses it for listening exercises. Laura: Okay, so, you know that the articles have audio, exercises and a glossary. Luca: Yes, it’s great that you can listen and read at the same time. Laura: That’s good to hear. Are you interested in the digital or print edition? Luca: I guess I like reading a physical magazine. Laura: Okay, well, a print subscription also includes access to the digital edition via the web or our app, where you can access the magazine’s archive. Luca: What’s that? Laura: It means you can read past issues going all the way back to 2022, plus lots of exclusive content, such as grammar lessons. Luca: That sounds great. How much is that? Laura: Right now it’s €38,97 per trimester, but if you subscribe via the web, you get a 62 per cent discount. That works out at €15 for three issues. Luca: Oh, I’ll do that! Please give me the link. NOW LET’S REVIEW THE VOCABULARY!

“I’m calling to enquire about…” is a formal, polite phrase used to ask for information. When you are familiar with something, you know about it. A newsstand is an outdoor temporary structure used as a shop for selling newspapers and magazines. “That’s good to hear” is a commonly-used response to good news. A print edition is a physical, paper-based copy of a publication; a digital edition is an electronic version, often in an interactive online format, with multimedia features. Starting a sentence with “I guess” (or “I suppose” or “probably”) indicates uncertainty or indifference. Here, the speaker is not fully committed to the answer and could be persuaded otherwise. An issue refers to a single, specific release of a magazine or newspaper, published on a particular date, i.e., the June issue of Speak Up.  “To work out” in this context means to calculate an amount.

Glossary

  • all the way back to = fino a
  • outdoor = outdoor
  • features = caratteristiche
  • otherwise = altrimenti
  • i.e. = cioè (‘id est’)
  • amount = quantità
  • polite = educata
  • uncertainty = incertezza
  • fully committed = pienamente sicuro
