# A Short Story: Scream   [C1]

Una scorciatoia attraverso una zona industriale deserta si trasforma in un’esperienza carica di tensione. Nel buio, tra rumori inquietanti e l’immaginazione che corre veloce, un semplice rientro a casa si trasforma in un incontro del tutto inatteso.

When the last bus dropped me at the end of Bristol Road I had two choices: walk along the brightly-lit main road, which curved round in a 1.5-km [horseshoe](## "a ferro di cavallo"), or take the 500m [shortcut](## "scorciatoia") through the industrial zone. It was past midnight and I was carrying some heavy shopping. I took the [shortcut](## "scorciatoia").
The industrial zone comprised a couple of abandoned factories, a few warehouses still in use and the ugly beginnings of a logistics centre for container lorries. Part of the local forest had been [chopped down](## "abbattere") for this last construction, despite many protests, and the earth was [scarred](## "lasciare cicatrici, segnare") with the tracks of bulldozers. It was very dark, the street lights were [few and far between](## "poche e molto distanziate").
I knew that to keep safe from any [muggers](## "aggressori") or [rapists](## "stupratori"), I should walk [purposefully](## "con determinazione") as though I knew where I was going, so I pushed back my shoulders and accelerated. When I reached the first warehouse, a security light [blinked on](## "accendersi") but the abandoned factories stood in complete darkness. I fixed my [gaze](## "sguardo fisso") on the cone of light under a lamppost a hundred metres ahead of me and marched on. When I reached the light, the plastic handles of my shopping bags were cutting into my hands and I had to rest.
It was then that I heard the first sound. Somewhere between a [groan](## "gemito") and a [sob](## "singhiozzo"), it was full of fear and pain. I froze. A minute went by. All I could hear was the distant buzz of traffic on the Bristol Road. I pushed the shopping bag handles up to my elbows, held my phone in front of me with the torchlight on and [set off at a trot](## "partire a passo svelto").
A [pile of logs](## "pila di tronchi") was stacked on the edge of the construction site. Beyond that there were the grey cement skeletons of the new buildings and, behind them, the darker shadows of what was left of the forest. A [wire fence](## "recinzione metallica") [skirted](## "costeggiare") the perimeter of the site and in one place it was [bent down](## "piegare verso il basso") to the ground. Vandals? [Kids on a dare](## "ragazzini che fanno una sfida")? I still had a hundred metres to go, but my muscles were on fire and I had to put the bags down for a moment. There came a sudden, [bone-chilling](## "raggelante") scream. Terrified, I shone my torch into the darkness, but could see nothing. It came again: the sound of a woman in pain and fear. Then there were other noises: [snapping](## "schioccare, spezzare") [twigs](## "rametti"), footsteps.

“Hello?” My voice sounded thin and [frail](## "debole"). I wanted to run away, but my legs wouldn’t move. The next cry was [high-pitched](## "acuto"). Was someone harming a young child?
“Come on!” I said to myself. “Brave people run towards danger, and one day that could be you!”
I dropped my shopping bags, and climbed [clumsily](## "goffamente") over the broken fence. The scream had come from behind a naked [concrete](## "cemento") pillar that stood out against the dark forest. I [inched towards](## "avanzare lentamente") it, [clutching](## "stringere") my phone like a weapon. There came another cry, then [heavy panting](## "respiri affannosi"), running footsteps. I threw myself to the ground as a [hooded figure](## "figura incappucciata") [burst out](## "scattare fuori") from the trees behind the construction, [thundered past](## "sfrecciare oltre") me and [leapt](## "balzare") noisily over the fence. I lay frozen in the mud until another weak cry brought me to my feet. 
The girl was still alive.
“Hello? Are you okay?”
Nothing.
“Are you hurt?”
Silence.
Was she unconscious? Dead? Shaking, I [peered](## "sbirciare") around the [concrete](## "cemento") pillar, swinging my phone around in the darkness until at last its [beam](## "fascio di luce") was answered by two shining lights in the dark. 
Eyes.
Slowly a fox walked into the light, opened her jaws and emitted the [blood-curdling](## "da far gelare il sangue") scream I had heard before. Then she turned and disappeared between the trees.
A fox! Relief [flooded over](## "travolgere") me like a heavy [blanket](## "coperta") and I almost sat down in the mud.
I found the man leaning against a wall near the bent fence. He was massaging his ankle. One of my shopping bags was on its side and apples had spilled over the pavement. He’d obviously [tripped over](## "inciampare") it.
He [jerked upright](## "rizzarsi di colpo") as I approached. “Oh my God, are you okay?”
I [nodded](## "annuire"). “You?”
“Yeah, I fell over climbing the fence.”
“What were you doing?”
He [smirked](## "sorridere in modo compiaciuto"). “A bit of graffiti on those [concrete](## "cemento") pillars. You know, protesting against the logistics centre. Then I heard you screaming.”
“And you ran away?”
He [straightened](## "raddrizzare") his shoulders. “I was running to get help. My phone was dead.”
“It’s all right. It wasn’t me screaming.”
“No?” He looked nervously over my shoulder. “Oh no! Is she still out there?”
“It was a fox.”
He [burst out](## "scattare fuori") laughing. “A fox! No way! I thought someone was being murdered.”
I looked at him and then at my shopping all over the road.
“Sorry about that. I [tripped over](## "inciampare") the bags in the dark.” I couldn’t see his face very well but I knew he was [blushing](## "arrossire").
Well,” I said, smiling, “give me a hand with this stuff, and I’ll walk you to the end of the road.”
