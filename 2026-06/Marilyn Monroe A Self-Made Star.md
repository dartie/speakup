Marilyn Monroe remains one of the most iconic figures of the 20th century, immortalised as the mythical [blonde bombshell](## "bionda esplosiva") of the 1950s and 60s. With her [hourglass figure](## "fisico a clessidra") and [effortless charm](## "fascino naturale (lett. senza sforzo)"), she became one of the most photographed people in history, a glamorous incarnation of Hollywood’s golden age. Yet behind this [dazzling](## "mozzafiato") image, Marilyn was far more than a mere sex symbol: she was also a visionary who played an active role in [crafting](## "forgiare") her own legend, [laying the ground](## "gettare le basi") for future stars such as Taylor Swift or Madonna.

## THE SUMMER OF MARILYN
Leading cultural institutions around the world will be marking the 100th anniversary of her birth this year, a celebration which has been [nicknamed](## "soprannominare") the ‘Summer of Marilyn’. In London, The National Portrait Gallery will open a [landmark](## "emblematica") exhibition, Marilyn Monroe: A Portrait. It promises to go beyond the clichés, exploring Monroe’s life and work through a fresh lens, one that highlights her pioneering [creative agency](## "autonomia creativa") and her [enduring](## "duratura") influence on pop culture.
One of the most iconic photos of Marilyn Monroe taken by photographer Milton H. Greene’s in 1954. She wears a transparent ballerina satin dress, apparently borrowed from Greene’s friend.

## TRAILBLAZER
Born Norma Jean Mortensen on 1 June 1926, Marilyn Monroe was not just a muse — she co-created her own persona. Aged just twenty, when she was signing a contract as an actress with Twentieth Century Fox, studio executive Ben Lyon suggested she take on a new [stage name](## "nome d’arte"). Lyon decided on Marilyn, because she reminded him of Marilyn Miller, a Broadway musical star, while Jean decided on Monroe, another surname on her mother’s side. Unlike many stars of her era, she also actively collaborated with photographers like Richard Avedon, Eve Arnold or Milton Greene. She didn’t just pose for the camera; she performed for it, directed sessions and [claimed](## "rivendicare") the right to veto any images she didn’t like.

## CREATIVE CONTROL
Her partnership with Milton Greene was particularly [groundbreaking](## "rivoluzionaria"). Together, they founded Marilyn Monroe Productions in 1955, a [bold move](## "mossa audace") that gave her creative control over her projects and challenged Hollywood’s studio system. This [venture](## "iniziativa, impresa") wasn’t just about independence, it was a declaration that Monroe was more than just a star; she was a producer and a pioneer in an industry that often treated women as [disposable](## "usa e getta") icons.

##  GROUNDBREAKING
Marilyn Monroe: A Portrait will bring together previously unseen photographs, intimate portraits and works by era-defining artists such as Andy Warhol and photographer Richard Avedon. The exhibition will trace Monroe’s journey from her early pin-up days to her final, [poignant](## "toccanti, intensi") [photoshoots](## "servizi fotografici"), revealing how she evolved as both a subject and a creative force. Visitors can expect to see iconic portraits that defined her public persona, from the [breathless](## "mozzafiato") glamour of Gentlemen Prefer Blondes, the 1953 movie that transformed her from a promising actress into a global superstar, to the [raw](## "cruda, senza filtri") vulnerability captured in her later years.
