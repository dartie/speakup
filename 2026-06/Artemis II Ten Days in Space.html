Artemis II: Ten Days in Space [B2]

Gli astronauti della missione Artemis II hanno intrapreso un viaggio straordinario di quasi 10 giorni nello spazio profondo. Una missione che ha segnato una nuova era dell’esplorazione e riacceso il sogno di tornare sulla Luna.

In Greek mythology, Artemis is the twin sister of Apollo. Artemis II sent four astronauts on a historic ten-day, 695,081-mile journey around the Moon. Their mission was to test their spacecraft’s life-support and navigational systems, make geological observations of the Moon’s surface, and conduct experiments on how deep space affects astronauts’ bodies. On Day 1, at 22:35 (ET) on 1 April, the four astronauts blasted off from NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida inside the 16.5-foot-wide [5m] spacecraft Orion. The spacecraft has two main modules: the service module, which includes power, propulsion, and life support; and the crew module, a pressurised capsule where the astronauts lived and that they named Integrity. In the first days, while travelling at up to almost 25,000 m/h [40,230 km/h], they encountered some computer and communication problems, and a malfunctioning toilet. On Day 2, they left Earth’s orbit for the Moon. On Days 3 and 4, they conducted some tests and demonstrations, and communicated with their families. And on Day 5, they reached the Moon’s sphere of influence, where the Moon’s gravitational force is stronger than Earth’s. Illustration showing the planned trajectory of the Artemis II mission. Day 6 was when the astronauts became the first humans in fifty years to see the far side of the Moon. At 13:56 ET, they made history by reaching a distance of over 252,756 miles [406,771 kilometres] from Earth, the furthest humans had ever travelled from their home planet. As they orbited the Moon, they photographed its far side, which has a thicker crust, more craters, and fewer maria (dark volcanic plains) than its near side. As they continued around the far side, they lost contact with Earth for about forty minutes because the Moon blocked radio signals. “To all of you down there on Earth, and around Earth, we love you, from the Moon,” Glover said. “We’ll see you on the other side,” responded Mission Control. Talitha Linehan , Alex Warner The astronauts spent the final days of their mission doing more tests and preparing to return to Earth through a free-return trajectory. This utilized the Moon’s gravity to modify the trajectory of the spacecraft without requiring a significant return engine burn, so saving fuel. The astronauts splashed down in the Pacific Ocean, off the coast of San Diego, at 20:07 ET on 10 April. They were taken to a recovery ship, where they underwent medical checks, before travelling to Houston to reunite with their families.
