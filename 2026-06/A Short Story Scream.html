A Short Story: Scream [C1]

Una scorciatoia attraverso una zona industriale deserta si trasforma in un’esperienza carica di tensione. Nel buio, tra rumori inquietanti e l’immaginazione che corre veloce, un semplice rientro a casa si trasforma in un incontro del tutto inatteso.

When the last bus dropped me at the end of Bristol Road I had two choices: walk along the brightly-lit main road, which curved round in a 1.5-km horseshoe, or take the 500m shortcut through the industrial zone. It was past midnight and I was carrying some heavy shopping. I took the shortcut. The industrial zone comprised a couple of abandoned factories, a few warehouses still in use and the ugly beginnings of a logistics centre for container lorries. Part of the local forest had been chopped down for this last construction, despite many protests, and the earth was scarred with the tracks of bulldozers. It was very dark, the street lights were few and far between. I knew that to keep safe from any muggers or rapists, I should walk purposefully as though I knew where I was going, so I pushed back my shoulders and accelerated. When I reached the first warehouse, a security light blinked on but the abandoned factories stood in complete darkness. I fixed my gaze on the cone of light under a lamppost a hundred metres ahead of me and marched on. When I reached the light, the plastic handles of my shopping bags were cutting into my hands and I had to rest. It was then that I heard the first sound. Somewhere between a groan and a sob, it was full of fear and pain. I froze. A minute went by. All I could hear was the distant buzz of traffic on the Bristol Road. I pushed the shopping bag handles up to my elbows, held my phone in front of me with the torchlight on and set off at a trot. A pile of logs was stacked on the edge of the construction site. Beyond that there were the grey cement skeletons of the new buildings and, behind them, the darker shadows of what was left of the forest. A wire fence skirted the perimeter of the site and in one place it was bent down to the ground. Vandals? Kids on a dare? I still had a hundred metres to go, but my muscles were on fire and I had to put the bags down for a moment. There came a sudden, bone-chilling scream. Terrified, I shone my torch into the darkness, but could see nothing. It came again: the sound of a woman in pain and fear. Then there were other noises: snapping twigs, footsteps. Fiction

Non trovando l’appoggio della moglie, cercherà compagnia in una tenera famiglia di procioni. Sarah Davison “Hello?” My voice sounded thin and frail. I wanted to run away, but my legs wouldn’t move. The next cry was high-pitched. Was someone harming a young child? “Come on!” I said to myself. “Brave people run towards danger, and one day that could be you!” I dropped my shopping bags, and climbed clumsily over the broken fence. The scream had come from behind a naked concrete pillar that stood out against the dark forest. I inched towards it, clutching my phone like a weapon. There came another cry, then heavy panting, running footsteps. I threw myself to the ground as a hooded figure burst out from the trees behind the construction, thundered past me and leapt noisily over the fence. I lay frozen in the mud until another weak cry brought me to my feet.  The girl was still alive. “Hello? Are you okay?” Nothing. “Are you hurt?” Silence. Was she unconscious? Dead? Shaking, I peered around the concrete pillar, swinging my phone around in the darkness until at last its beam was answered by two shining lights in the dark.  Eyes. Slowly a fox walked into the light, opened her jaws and emitted the blood-curdling scream I had heard before. Then she turned and disappeared between the trees. A fox! Relief flooded over me like a heavy blanket and I almost sat down in the mud. I found the man leaning against a wall near the bent fence. He was massaging his ankle. One of my shopping bags was on its side and apples had spilled over the pavement. He’d obviously tripped over it. He jerked upright as I approached. “Oh my God, are you okay?” I nodded. “You?” “Yeah, I fell over climbing the fence.” “What were you doing?” He smirked. “A bit of graffiti on those concrete pillars. You know, protesting against the logistics centre. Then I heard you screaming.” “And you ran away?” He straightened his shoulders. “I was running to get help. My phone was dead.” “It’s all right. It wasn’t me screaming.” “No?” He looked nervously over my shoulder. “Oh no! Is she still out there?” “It was a fox.” He burst out laughing. “A fox! No way! I thought someone was being murdered.” I looked at him and then at my shopping all over the road. “Sorry about that. I tripped over the bags in the dark.” I couldn’t see his face very well but I knew he was blushing. Well,” I said, smiling, “give me a hand with this stuff, and I’ll walk you to the end of the road.”
