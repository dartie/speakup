"All will be [disclosed](## "rivelare")." This is the tagline of the new sci-fi thriller Disclosure Day. The film follows a group of scientists, military officers and civilian witnesses who [become entangled](## "rimanere coinvolto") in a high-level [cover-up](## "insabbiamento") after a series of inexplicable aerial events take place across the globe. It depicts the [mounting public pressure](## "pressione crescente") to reveal the truth, and the emotional, societal, and existential panic caused by the revelation that aliens really do exist and are among us here on Earth. Directed by Steven Spielberg, the film stars Emily Blunt, Josh O’Connor, and Colin Firth.

## SOMETHING’S GOING ON
In numerous interviews over the years, Spielberg has discussed the deep curiosity he has held about the cosmos since early childhood. Indeed, some of his early films, such as Close Encounters of the Third Kind and E. T. the ExtraTerrestrial, established him as a film-maker fascinated by the possibility of intelligent life beyond Earth. In a promotional interview for his new movie, Spielberg, now seventy-nine, said that while he has never had any direct UFO- or alien-related experience, recent government disclosures and relevant public reports have [reignited](## "riaccendere") his curiosity about whether humanity is truly alone in the universe.
Filmmaker Steven Spielberg has long been fascinated by the mysteries of the cosmos. The 1982 movie for E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial is an iconic example.
**Steven Spielberg (American accent):** I don’t know if I’m a believer in the sense that I got to see something like that to believe it. I can [make up stuff](## "inventarsi delle cose") and make movies about things that I’ve never seen or experienced, and that’s fine, but in terms of really believing something, I think I’d have to have my own close encounter. I’ve never seen anything that I couldn’t explain, but I think what has been coming out recently is fascinating, just absolutely fascinating. And I think the secrecy that is [shrouding](## "avvolgere") all of these [sightings](## "avvistamenti") and the [lack](## "mancanza") of transparency until Freedom of Information Act [compels](## "costringere") certain materials to be released publicly, I think that there is something going on that simply needs extraordinary [due diligence](## "necessaria diligenza").

## MATHEMATICALLY IMPOSSIBLE
Spielberg’s fascination is [rooted in](## "radicata in") an innate curiosity. For him, the UFO question is both a scientific and philosophical one, shaped by human imagination and the limits of our knowledge about the laws of space and time.
**Steven Spielberg:** I think it’s mathematically impossible that we are the only intelligent species in the cosmos. I think that’s totally impossible. At the same time, it almost seems impossible that anybody would visit us from four hundred million light years from here ­— except in the movies — unless it figures out some way of jumping the shark, so to speak, and getting here through wormholes. So I’m not an astrophysicist, but I just know as a person that makes movies and uses his imagination, and also as a person that is insatiably curious about UFOs or [UAPs](## "fenomeni aerei non identificati ('unexplaind aereal phenomena')"), that there’s something going on that’s not being [disclosed](## "rivelare") to us.

## CURIOSITY NOT AGGRESSION
Spielberg argues that there is room for alternative scientific theories to explain [UAPs](## "fenomeni aerei non identificati ('unexplaind aereal phenomena')"), including ideas related to time travel and future human evolution. He also [addresses](## "affrontare") a broader ethical [question often raised](## "sollevare una questione") by astrobiologists: if advanced civilisations were capable of reaching Earth, what motives would they have for such an extraordinary journey?
**Steven Spielberg:** What if they’re not from an advanced civilisation three hundred million light years from here? But what if it’s us, five hundred thousand years into the future that is coming back to document the second-half of the 20th century and into the first 20th century because they’re anthropologists, and they know something that we don’t quite know yet that has occurred. And they’re trying to [track](## "tracciare, monitorare") the last hundred years of our history. I believe that if any extraterrestrial civilisation has journeyed all the way here, it’s because of curiosity and science and it’s not about aggression.
