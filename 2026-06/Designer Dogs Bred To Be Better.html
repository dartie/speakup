Designer Dogs: Bred To Be Better [B1]

Desideri un cane con l’intelligenza di un barboncino e il carattere socievole di un labrador? Nessun problema! Negli ultimi anni, i cani ibridi hanno visto crescere notevolmente la loro popolarità… e il loro prezzo!

You’ve heard of designer clothes, shoes or bags. But how about designer dogs? Also known as ‘dog crossbreeds’, designer dogs are not like typical mutts, which have unknown mixed ancestry. (That’s okay, we love them anyway!) They are intentionally crossbred from purebred parents, with the objective of creating a puppy that inherits the best traits of both parents. These custom-made designer dogs are often more expensive than purebred dogs — sometimes selling for $5,000 or more! — and have become more popular than ever in recent years.

FROM WOLVES TO DOGS

Dog breeding is not a recent activity. It began over 15,000 years ago, with the domestication of wolves. For thousands of years, we bred dogs for purposes such as herding and hunting. It wasn’t until the 1800s that we began breeding dogs for physical aesthetics and uniformity, creating the standardised purebred dogs that we know today. Then, in the late 1980s, people began intentionally crossbreeding purebred dogs to create so-called designer dogs. A pomsky (Siberian husky and Pomeranian).

SPECIFIC TRAITS

Designer dogs are often bred to have a specific trait, such as appearance, intelligence, or temperament. They might be bred to shed less fur than other breeds, making them ideal pets for people with dog allergies. Or they might be bred to reduce their predisposition to genetic disorders. This is based on the assumption that crossbreeding reduces the probability of inheriting breed-specific health conditions. Popular designer dogs include the labradoodle, a cross between a Labrador retriever and a poodle; the goldendoodle (golden retriever and poodle); the cockapoo (cocker spaniel and poodle); the cavapoo (cavalier King Charles spaniel and poodle); and the pomsky (Pomeranian and Siberian husky).

UNPREDICTABLE 

Creating a designer dog is extremely difficult because it’s impossible to predict the result. There is no guarantee that the dog will inherit the desired traits from either or both parents, and have the intended appearance, intelligence, temperament, genetic health, and so on. Instead of inheriting the ‘best’ of both parents, the puppy can inherit the ‘worst’ of both parents. Also, their popularity and high price have contributed to the proliferation of industrial-scale puppy mills, which prioritise high-volume production over the health and happiness of the puppies. Perhaps we should stick to our beloved mutts instead. Culture
