Romantasy: The Rise of a Hybrid Genre [B2]

Le storie d’amore arricchite da draghi, magia e pericoli sono la principale tendenza editoriale del momento, grazie anche a numerose comunità online come quella di BookTok.

In a post-pandemic world of geopolitical chaos, who doesn’t need a dose of feel-good fiction where good overcomes evil, villains are defeated and noble warriors defy the odds, to win the hearts of their beloved? Doomscrolling the news is driving readers to escape to alternative realities. With a little help from online communities like BookTok, romantasy is becoming one of today’s most popular literary genres. These novels blend magic, folklore and exciting battle scenes, with a romantic love story at their core. Romantasy has become a global phenomenon, gradually dominating the international bestseller charts.

ROMANCE RULES

The genre evolved from fantasy. From J. R. R. Tolkien’s The Lord of the Rings trilogy to George R. R. Martin’s Game of Thrones series, fantasy fiction has always had a loyal following. These tales take place in imaginary worlds, often defined by magical creatures, supernatural elements and an epic quest. Authors typically draw inspiration from folklore, mythology and medieval history, but romance is optional. Remove Aragorn and Arwen’s love affair from Tolkien’s Middle-earth saga, for example, and the story still works. In romantasy fiction, however, the whole plot hinges on the romance. The clue is in the name: it is a combination of ‘romance’ and ‘fantasy’. Some stories happen in classic high-fantasy settings, with kingdoms and dragons. Others mix fantasy with modern settings or darker themes. Common subgenres include epic romantasy, paranormal romance, dark romantasy, and young adult romantasy. From Blood and Ash (2020) by Jennifer Lynn Armentrout and Fourth Wing (2023) by Rebecca Yarros. Both books fit into the romantasy genre and are described by readers as both “engrossing” and “spicy”.

BESTSELLING AUTHORS

One of the most influential romantasy authors is Sarah J. Maas. Her series A Court of Thorns and Roses is probably the most popular saga today. She shares the crown with Rebecca Yarros, author of the bestselling novel Fourth Wing. Many readers also love the works of Jennifer L. Armentrout and Holly Black. The genre’s success has naturally caught the interest of film and television studios. A host of screen adaptations are in development, including Armentrout’s From Blood and Ash and Callie Hart’s Quicksilver. Culture

MORE THAN A TREND

Fans of fantasy used to be typically male, but romantasy has a more universal appeal. Its main audience is young adult and adult readers, especially women in their teens, twenties and thirties. The genre is expanding, too, through social media recommendations and online book communities. With a loyal fanbase and time-honoured themes, it looks like more than a passing trend. As long as readers enjoy stories that combine magical adventures with powerful love stories, romantasy is likely to live happily ever after.
