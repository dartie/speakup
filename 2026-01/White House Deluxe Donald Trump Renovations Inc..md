The White House changes with each administration. The decoration and use of the rooms can be [rearranged](## "riorganizzare") by each president and first family. While there are few details on former president Joe Biden’s changes, his residency reportedly started with a $127,000 “inaugural cleaning,” as part of Covid-19 health and safety measures.
President Trump’s changes, on the other hand, [cannot fail to be noticed](## "è impossibile non accorgersene"). The White House is turning gold. The Oval office is [gilded](## "dorato") on every surface, with gold trophies on the [mantlepiece](## "mensola del camino"), [gold trim](## "rifiniture dorate") along the ceiling and [gold-framed portraits](## "ritratti con cornici dorate") on the walls. Some liken it to Versailles; others call it “[gaudy](## "pacchiano").” What is certain is that the aesthetics resemble those of Trump’s Florida estate, Mar-a-Lago.

## GOLDEN MAKEOVER
Similarly, the Cabinet Room has been subject to a golden [makeover](## "ristrutturazione, riforma"). The walls are decorated with [twenty-four-carat](## "di 24 carati") gold [decals](## "motivi ornamentali"). The windows are [draped](## "drappeggiare") with gold curtains. Chandeliers hang from the ceiling and more flags have been added. The bathroom of the Lincoln Bedroom is also [decked out](## "adornare") in gold [against a marble backdrop](## "su uno sfondo di marmo").
The famous Rose Garden in the West Wing has been [paved over](## "pavimentare"). Now featuring [lawn furniture](## "mobili da giardino") and [striped parasols](## "ombrelloni a righe"), it looks a lot like Mar-a-Lago’s patio. Trump hosted his first gathering with Republican [lawmakers](## "legislatori") there on 5 September. It has since been given the nickname The Rose Garden Club.

## DEMOLITION
Over in the East Wing, the Jacqueline Kennedy Garden has been demolished to make room for Trump’s most controversial addition: the White House [ballroom](## "sala da ballo"). In fact, most of the East Wing has been [torn down](## "demolire"), [despite](## "nonostante") initial assurances to the contrary. The $300 million construction is financed by private donors, including several Big Tech companies, [raising concerns](## "suscitare preoccupazioni") about ethics and historic legacy. The fact that the project started during a time of government shutdown and food insecurity has [drawn criticism](## "sollevare critiche") about the administration’s priorities. A White House [spokesperson](## "portavoce") told The Independent newspaper: “These long-needed upgrades will benefit generations of future presidents and American visitors to the People’s House.”

## 999 GUESTS
The White House has not yet confirmed whether the plans will include permanent offices for the first lady and her staff, currently working in the main building. Unlike her predecessors, Melania Trump does not live at the White House full-time. 
The [ballroom](## "sala da ballo") is supposed to resolve the [issue](## "problema") of [cramped space](## "spazio ristretto") for state functions. Where previously tents were erected on the lawn for big events, the [ballroom](## "sala da ballo") will accommodate 999 people. The project is scheduled for completion before the end of President Trump’s [term](## "mandato") in 2029. It is an ambitious deadline, but one thing is certain: it will feature a lot of gold.
