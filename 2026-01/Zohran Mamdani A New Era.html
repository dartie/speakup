Zohran Mamdani: A New Era [C1]

Analizziamo i passaggi più significativi del discorso di ringraziamento di Zohran Mamdani, il neoeletto sindaco di New York City che ha dato una scossa alla politica statunitense.

Zohran Mamdani’s historic election as the mayor of New York City has shaken the foundations of the US political establishment at a critical time. And not just because he became the first Indian or the first Muslim, and the first self-declared democratic socialist, to lead the city at the forefront of cultural and economic transformations. His political style and rhetoric — full of idealism and utopian promises — echo the voices of past leaders like Barack Obama or even Martin Luther King Jr. Mamdani’s speech on the night of 4 November has been hailed as one of the most inspiring in recent memory. Time magazine called it “a moment of generational shift, delivered with the cadence of a movement leader rather than a politician,” while Politico defined his words as “a defiant, poetic rebuke of political convention.”

CITY OF IMMIGRANTS

Throughout his campaign, Mamdani often spoke about his immigrant background and visited immigrant neighbourhoods across New York City. In his victory speech, he began by addressing his base, comprised of the more than four million working-class New Yorkers who keep the city running. Zohran Mamdani (American accent): For as long as we can remember, the working people of New York have been told by the wealthy and the well-connected that power does not belong in their hands. Fingers bruised from lifting boxes on the warehouse floor, palms calloused from delivery bike handlebars, knuckles scarred with kitchen burns: these are not hands that have been allowed to hold power. And yet, over the last twelve months, you have dared to reach for something greater. Tonight, against all odds, we have grasped it. The future is in our hands. My friends, we have toppled a political dynasty.

LITTLE EGYPT

He then turned to thank the many communities that had embraced his message — making special mention of Steinway Street, an area in the Queens neighbourhood of Astoria known as Little Egypt for its vibrant Arabic-speaking and Middle Eastern immigrant community. Zohran Mamdani: Before I say anything else, I must say this: thank you. Thank you to the next generation of New Yorkers who refuse to accept that the promise of a better future was a relic of the past. You showed that when politics speaks to you without condescension, we can usher in a new era of leadership. We will fight for you, because we are you. Or, as we say on Steinway, “Ana minkum wa ilaykum.” Thank you to those so often forgotten by the politics of our city, who made this movement their own. I speak of Yemeni bodega owners and Mexican abuelas. Senegalese taxi drivers and Uzbek nurses. Trinidadian line cooks and Ethiopian aunties.

A BOLD VISION

Later in the speech, Mamdani referenced Jawaharlal Nehru, a central figure in India’s struggle for independence from British colonial rule and the country’s first prime minister. Quoting from Nehru’s famous “Tryst with Destiny” speech, delivered on the eve of India’s independence in 1947, Mamdani marked the symbolic end of an era in New York politics. Zohran Mamdani: Standing before you, I think of the words of Jawaharlal Nehru: “A moment comes, but rarely in history, when we step out from the old to the new, when an age ends, and when the soul of a nation, long suppressed, finds utterance.” This will be an age where New Yorkers expect from their leaders a bold vision of what we will achieve. Central to that vision will be the most ambitious agenda to tackle the cost-of-living crisis : an agenda that will freeze the rents for more than two million rent-stabilised tenants, make buses fast and free, and deliver universal childcare across our city.

NEW YORKERS

He also made a pointed reference to Donald Trump, who, like Mamdani, is a product of New York; but whose legacy Mamdani vowed to dismantle. Zohran Mamdani: In this moment of political darkness, New York will be the light. As has so often occurred, the billionaire class, they want the people to fight amongst ourselves so that we remain distracted from the work of remaking a long-broken system. Together, we will usher in a generation of change. And if we embrace this brave new course, rather than fleeing from it, we can respond to oligarchy and authoritarianism with the strength it fears, not the appeasement it craves. After all, if anyone can show a nation betrayed by Donald Trump how to defeat him, it is the city that gave rise to him. This is not only how we stop Trump; it’s how we stop the next one. We will put an end to the culture of corruption that has allowed billionaires like Trump to evade taxation and exploit tax breaks. We will stand alongside unions and expand labour protections because we know, just as Donald Trump does, that when working people have ironclad rights, the bosses who seek to extort them become very small indeed.

THE FINALE

Mamdani ended his speech with a nod to Mario Cuomo, the long-standing former governor of New York, and father of Mamdani’s defeated opponent, Andrew Cuomo. Zohran Mamdani: A great New Yorker once said that while you campaign in poetry, you govern in prose. If that must be true, let the prose we write still rhyme, and let us build a shining city for all. I am Muslim. I am a democratic socialist. And most damning of all, I refuse to apologise for any of this!
