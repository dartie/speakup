White House Deluxe: Donald Trump Renovations Inc. [B2]

Casa Bianca o Casa Dorata? Le riforme apportate da Trump alla residenza storica dei presidenti degli Stati Uniti sono lontane dall’essere discrete.

The White House changes with each administration. The decoration and use of the rooms can be rearranged by each president and first family. While there are few details on former president Joe Biden’s changes, his residency reportedly started with a $127,000 “inaugural cleaning,” as part of Covid-19 health and safety measures. President Trump’s changes, on the other hand, cannot fail to be noticed. The White House is turning gold. The Oval office is gilded on every surface, with gold trophies on the mantlepiece, gold trim along the ceiling and gold-framed portraits on the walls. Some liken it to Versailles; others call it “gaudy.” What is certain is that the aesthetics resemble those of Trump’s Florida estate, Mar-a-Lago.

GOLDEN MAKEOVER

Similarly, the Cabinet Room has been subject to a golden makeover. The walls are decorated with twenty-four-carat gold decals. The windows are draped with gold curtains. Chandeliers hang from the ceiling and more flags have been added. The bathroom of the Lincoln Bedroom is also decked out in gold against a marble backdrop. The famous Rose Garden in the West Wing has been paved over. Now featuring lawn furniture and striped parasols, it looks a lot like Mar-a-Lago’s patio. Trump hosted his first gathering with Republican lawmakers there on 5 September. It has since been given the nickname The Rose Garden Club.

DEMOLITION

Over in the East Wing, the Jacqueline Kennedy Garden has been demolished to make room for Trump’s most controversial addition: the White House ballroom. In fact, most of the East Wing has been torn down, despite initial assurances to the contrary. The $300 million construction is financed by private donors, including several Big Tech companies, raising concerns about ethics and historic legacy. The fact that the project started during a time of government shutdown and food insecurity has drawn criticism about the administration’s priorities. A White House spokesperson told The Independent newspaper: “These long-needed upgrades will benefit generations of future presidents and American visitors to the People’s House.”

999 GUESTS

The White House has not yet confirmed whether the plans will include permanent offices for the first lady and her staff, currently working in the main building. Unlike her predecessors, Melania Trump does not live at the White House full-time.  The ballroom is supposed to resolve the issue of cramped space for state functions. Where previously tents were erected on the lawn for big events, the ballroom will accommodate 999 people. The project is scheduled for completion before the end of President Trump’s term in 2029. It is an ambitious deadline, but one thing is certain: it will feature a lot of gold.
