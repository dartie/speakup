Among the most influential musicians ever, English singer David Bowie was a musical [magpie](## "gazza"), taking inspiration from a [wide range](## "ampia gamma") of genres. His eclectic career [spanned](## "abbracciare, coprire") five decades and incorporated everything from folk, glam rock, R&B, disco and pop to drum’n’bass and jazz. He was an avid reader and explored many recurring themes in his lyrics, creating multiple [personas](## "personaggi"). Outer Space was an early motif with his first hit Space Oddity and his alter ego Ziggy Stardust, and later again in 1980’s [Ashes to Ashes](## "polvere alla polvere"). Bowie’s half-brother Terry suffered from schizophrenia and therefore many of Bowie’s songs deal with isolation and alienation.

## 1. SPACE ODDITY (1969)
Bowie’s first chart success coincided with the 1969 [Moon landings](## "sbarchi sulla Luna"). A psychedelic folk tale of an [ill-fated](## "sfortunato") astronaut, it begins with a countdown while ground control [issues](## "emettere, impartire") instructions through imperatives, with Major Tom later reporting back in the present continuous.
Ground Control to Major TomGround Control to Major TomTake your protein pills and put your helmet onGround Control to Major Tom (ten, nine, eight, seven, six)Commencing countdown, engines on (five, four, three, two)Check ignition and may God’s love be with you (one, [lift off](## "decollare"))

## 2. THE MAN WHO SOLD THE WORLD (1970)
Ignored on its initial [release](## "uscita"), this subsequently became one of Bowie’s best-known tracks after it was covered by Nirvana in 1993. Defying interpretation the lyrics refer to a cryptic encounter, using relative clauses and the past simple.
We passed upon the stairWe spoke of was and whenAlthough I wasn’t thereHe said I was his friendWhich came as some surpriseI spoke into his eyes, “I thought you died aloneA long long time ago”
 

## 3. CHANGES (1971)
[Stung](## "pungere, ferire") by the commercial failure of the [follow-up](## "seguito") to Space Oddity, the opening track of Bowie’s next album “Hunky Dory” was a [prescient](## "lungimirante") reflection on how he should change direction. It uses the past continuous, informal phrases and intensifiers.
Still don’t know what I was waitin’ forAnd my time was runnin’ wildA million [dead end streets](## "vicoli ciechi, strade senza uscita") andEvery time I thought [I’d got it made](## "farcela, avere successo")It seemed the taste was not so sweet

## 4. LIFE ON MARS? (1971)
Using the [chords](## "accordi") of Frank Sinatra’s My Way, for which Bowie had unsuccessfully [submitted](## "presentare") lyrics, this is about a girl’s disillusionment with life and its [depiction](## "rappresentazione") in art. It is mostly in the present simple, with some present continuous and present perfect, and many descriptive adjectives.  
It’s a [God-awful](## "orribile") small affairTo the girl with the [mousy hair](## "capelli castano spento")But her mummy is [yelling](## "urlare"), “No” And her daddy has told her to go

## 5. STARMAN (1972)
Bowie wrote this song with the express objective of producing a hit, basing the melody of the [chorus](## "ritornello") on Over The Rainbow from the 1939 film The [Wizard](## "mago") of Oz. It worked, and his glam rock appearance on BBC’s Top of the Pops changed pop music forever. The song, about an extra-terrestrial [broadcasting](## "trasmettere") messages of hope over the radio, contrasts the past simple and the past continuous.  
Didn’t know what time it was, the lights were lowI [leaned back](## "appoggiarsi all’indietro") on my radioSome cat was layin’ downsome rock ‘n’ roll“[Lotta](## "contrazione di 'lot(s) of', un sacco di") soul,” he said

## 6. ZIGGY STARDUST (1972)
Bowie’s androgynous alien alter-ego Ziggy Stardust [propelled](## "spingere") him to [superstardom](## "super celebrità"). This [eponymous](## "omonimo") tribute recounts the rock god’s fictional rise and fall, using past simple narrative and possessive for the band. 
Now Ziggy played guitar[Jamming](## "suonare improvvisando") good with Weird and GillyAnd The Spiders from MarsHe played it left handBut made it too farBecame the special manThen we were Ziggy’s band

## 7. FAME (1975)
The 1975 album “Young Americans” is a homage to Philadelphia soul, an American East coast precursor of disco. Co-written and recorded with John Lennon, the single Fame deals with the [trials and tribulations](## "sfide e difficoltà") of success and gave Bowie his first US number one.
Fame (fame) makes a man [take things over](## "prendere il controllo")Fame (fame) lets him lose hard to [swallow](## "ingoiare")Fame (fame) puts you there where things are [hollow](## "vuote")Fame (fame)

## 8. HEROES (1977)
Increasingly isolated while touring and working on a parallel acting career, Bowie sought refuge in drug abuse for several months. Then, in 1976, he moved to Berlin — a still-divided city with guards patrolling the Berlin Wall — to recover. The city’s vibrant Krautrock scene inspired a trilogy of critically-acclaimed albums with ambient producer Brian Eno, ranging from [brooding](## "cupi, malinconici") electronic instrumentals to art rock and new wave. While recording at the studio, he looked through the window to see his producer Tony Visconti (who was married) kissing a girl, and was inspired to write one of his best-known tracks. Looking at possibilities during [strife](## "conflitto, lite"), it uses the future ‘will’ and modal ‘can’. 
I, I will be kingAnd you, you will be queenThough nothing will drive them awayWe can beat them, just for one dayWe can be heroes, just for one day

## 9. LET’S DANCE (1983)
The early 1980s was David Bowie’s most commercially-successful period, with four number ones in the UK: Ashes To Ashes (1980) revisits Major Tom, last seen floating in Space Oddity (1969); the Queen collaboration Under Pressure (1981) is an improvised studio jam about the difficulties of modern life; Dancing in the Street (1985) is a high-energy duet with Mick Jagger; while Let’s Dance (1983), biggest-selling single ever with 10.7 million copies worldwide, is a funky story of a couple dancing under the moonlight. The [bleak](## "cupi, tristi") lyrics use the first conditional and the modals ‘would’ and ‘should’. 
If you say run, I’ll run with youAnd if you say hide, we’ll hideBecause my love for you would break my heart in twoIf you should fall, into my arms and tremble like a flower

## 10. LAZARUS (2016)
In 1995, Bowie [rejoined](## "riunirsi") Brian Eno for the experimental album “Outside”, recounting the fictional investigation of the murder of a fourteen-year-old girl. He followed this with a [foray](## "incursione") into the nascent drum’n’bass scene. In 2013, after a decade-long hiatus, and believed to have retired, he surprised the music world on his 66th birthday with the [release](## "uscita") of the single Where Are We Now?, a reflective walk through Berlin. His 2016 [swansong](## "canto del cigno"), “Blackstar”, another surprise [release](## "uscita"), was heavily jazz-infused with lyrics inspired by his impending death, notably on the single Lazarus, which serves as a [haunting](## "inquietante") [farewell](## "addio") and alludes to the biblical figure [raised from the dead](## "resuscitare"). He died two days after its [release](## "uscita"). 
Look up here, I’m in heavenI’ve got [scars](## "cicatrici") that can’t be seenI’ve got drama, can’t be stolenEverybody knows me now
