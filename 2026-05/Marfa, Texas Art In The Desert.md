At the heart of West Texas’ Chihuahuan Desert [lies](## "trovarsi") an [unlikely](## "inaspettata") oasis of contemporary art: the town of Marfa. Once a [sleepy](## "assonnato") [outpost](## "avamposto") [split in two](## "dividere in due") by a railway line and populated largely by ranchers and cowboys, Marfa might have remained one of many forgotten desert towns. Instead, in the early 1970s, it [underwent](## "attraversare") a remarkable transformation, becoming an international symbol of artistic renewal when the minimalist sculptor Donald Judd [fell under its spell](## "cadere sotto il suo incantesimo").

## DESERT LANDSCAPES
[At the height of](## "all’apice di") his fame, Judd made the radical decision to leave New York. He saw in Marfa’s desert landscapes an ideal setting for his monumental, permanent installations. At the time, Marfa resembled the ghost towns so common in the American Southwest, consisting of only a [handful](## "manciata") of ranches and adobe [house](## "ospitare")s, many without [running water](## "acqua corrente") or electricity. [Undeterred](## "imperterrito"), Judd [purchased](## "acquistare") several buildings after moving there with his family, including former military ware[house](## "ospitare")s that he converted into spaces to [house](## "ospitare") his personal collection. This collection included not only his own work, but also pieces by close friends and collaborators — the very cream of Minimalism.
100 Untitled Works in Mill Aluminum (1982-1986), a permanent installation at The Chinati Foundation in Marfa, Texas. 

## RAW BEAUTY
Artists and collectors followed Judd to Marfa, d[raw](## "grezza")n by both his vision and the town’s [raw](## "grezza"), elemental beauty. The town soon gained an almost mythical reputation worldwide.
Nearly fifty years later, Marfa continues to [exert](## "esercitare") the same magnetic [pull](## "attrazione"), particularly on New Yorkers seeking an escape from city life. Many arrive for long weekends, only to find themselves [lingering](## "trattenersi") longer than planned. The town is known for its [bewitching](## "incantevole") quality: [sparse](## "rada") yet vibrating with creative energy.

## EMPTINESS AND ART
Although fewer than two thousand people live there permanently, Marfa [teems with](## "pullulare di") art galleries, studios, and [thoughtfully](## "con cura") curated restaurants, and even [boasts](## "vantare") its own radio station. Among the [must-see spots](## "luoghi imperdibili") are Ballroom Marfa, a contemporary art centre that [doubles as](## "fungere anche da") a concert hall and movie theatre, and the Wrong Store, a cooperative space that exhibits and sells works by local artists, [embodying](## "incarnare") the town’s collaborative spirit.
Every year in May, Marfa also hosts a festival, the Invitational, a weekend-long festival dedicated to contemporary art. The festival combines an art fair with activities such as conversations with artists. Founded in 2019 by artist and curator Michael Phelan and his wife, art advisor Melissa Bent — both residents of Marfa —, the Invitational has quickly become a key moment in the town’s cultural calendar, reinforcing Marfa’s status as a small town with an [outsize](## "smisurata") influence on the global art world.
