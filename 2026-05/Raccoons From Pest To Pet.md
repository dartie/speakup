They have been [rummaging](## "frugare") through our [trash cans](## "bidoni della spazzatura") for some time now, but soon we may be inviting them into our homes, feeding them, and treating them like family. A recent study published in the journal Frontiers in Zoology shows that [raccoons](## "procioni") are [undergoing](## "attraversare") some physiological and [behavioural](## "comportamentali") changes that eventually may lead to them becoming our pets.

## TRASH PANDAS
Also affectionately known as ‘trash pandas’, [raccoons](## "procioni") are highly intelligent, medium-sized [mammals](## "mammiferi") with a black bandit mask around their eyes and [bushy](## "folte"), [ringed tails](## "code ad anelli"). With extremely [dexterous](## "abili") [paws](## "zampe") that they use to open [jars](## "barattoli"), doors, and [latches](## "chiavistelli"), [raccoons](## "procioni") have become experts over the years at finding food in urban environments. They are also very smart; researchers have found that [raccoons](## "procioni") can solve [tricky puzzles](## "enigmi complicati") and remember the answers for years.
Two [raccoons](## "procioni") in a trash bin.

## LEFTOVER LOVERS
The study carried out at the University of Arkansas at Little Rock analysed nearly twenty thousand photos of [raccoons](## "procioni") from across the continental United States and found a clear reduction in [snout](## "muso") length in urban [raccoons](## "procioni") compared to their rural [relatives](## "parenti"). According to zoologists, shorter, stronger [snout](## "muso")s are better [suite](## "insieme, serie")d for [foraging](## "cercare cibo") within human habitats, searching through [trash cans](## "bidoni della spazzatura"), breaking open packages and [crunching](## "sgranocchiare") [leftovers](## "avanzi").
Raccoons have also changed their behaviour in order to better navigate and [thrive](## "prosperare") in human environments. Individuals that are calmer and less [fearful](## "timorosi") of people are better at getting to [trash cans](## "bidoni della spazzatura") and other easy sources of food than aggressive ones, since they represent [less of a threat](## "una minaccia minore") to humans. Consequently, they have [dampened](## "ridurre") their innate [flight](## "fuga") response.

## DOMESTICATION SYNDROME
The reduced [snout](## "muso") and the friendlier [demeanour](## "comportamento") belong to a [suite](## "insieme, serie") of physical and [behavioural](## "comportamentali") [traits](## "tratti") present in domesticated [mammals](## "mammiferi") that differ from their wild ancestors, and that Charles Darwin identified as “domestication syndrome”. This set of features — which also includes [floppy ears](## "orecchie cadenti"), [coat](## "pelliccia") colour changes, and smaller brains and teeth — tends to emerge as animals become more gentle, tolerant, and less aggressive toward humans, and would be part of a wild animal’s transition to [tameness](## "mansuetudine") and eventually domestication.

## LONDON FOXES
These morphological and [behavioural](## "comportamentali") processes have also been observed in the UK among foxes. Researchers at the University of Glasgow analysed a collection of about 1,500 fox [skulls](## "crani"), comparing those belonging to individuals from London to those from the surrounding countryside. They found that, in addition to shorter [snout](## "muso")s, urban foxes had a smaller brain capacity, the reason being that they do not need the mental agility required to catch [live prey](## "prede vive").

## A NEW RELATIONSHIP
[In this light](## "visto così"), the set of [traits](## "tratti") described by Darwin challenges the popular concept of domestication as a [human-driven](## "indotto dagli esseri umani") process of artificial selection. Rather, foxes and [raccoons](## "procioni") may be inflicting these changes on themselves to better adapt to an inescapable life around humans, perhaps as dogs and cats did thousands of years before them. Domestication could then start without direct human involvement. In the case of [raccoons](## "procioni"), the abundance of trash may be the decisive factor that someday leads to a new, closer relationship between these omnivorous and nocturnal [mammals](## "mammiferi") and us.
The urban fox is more commonly seen in UK and European cities; here one passes 10 Downing Street in London in broad daylight. 

## GLOBAL EXPANSION
Although native to North America, these adaptable ‘backyard bandits’, as they are also known, have successfully expanded into urban areas worldwide, from Germany to Japan. Now, the evolutionary selection pressure exerted by human expansion and the destruction of wild habitats may be turning them from [pests](## "piaga") into pets.
