With [political turmoil](## "instabilità politica") around the world and scientists [warning](## "avvertire") about the inevitable and devastating effects of climate change, few of us doubt that this century [will](## "volontà") see some kind of major transition. Where that transition [will](## "volontà") take us is impossible to predict, but hope and inspiration can be found in the work of writer and integrator Jeremy Lent.

## LIFE CHANGES
Born and brought up in the UK, after a first [degree](## "laurea") at Cambridge University, Jeremy moved to the United States to continue his studies before [going into business](## "dedicarsi al mondo degli affari"). Although successful, in 2000 he was forced to sell his company in order to stay at home and look after his wife, who was terminally ill and sadly [passed away](## "morire"). Jeremy describes how, after this series of deep personal shocks, he decided that, whatever he did with the rest of his life, he wanted it to be [meaningful](## "significativa"). Since then, he has dedicated himself to an academic, intellectual and personal search for meaning.
The [Patterning](## "modellare, seguire degli schemi") Instinct (2017) and The Web of Meaning (2021) by Jeremy Lent.

## TWO BOOKS
The result has been two books: The [Patterning](## "modellare, seguire degli schemi") Instinct (2017) and The Web of Meaning (2021). These books have led climate activist George Monbiot, as well as many others, to describe Jeremy Lent as “one of the greatest thinkers of our age.”
The [Patterning](## "modellare, seguire degli schemi") Instinct is a tour de force study of the history of humanity in [terms](## "definire, chiamare") of culture, biology and psychology. It considers every possible influence — from the birth of language, religion and agriculture to art, commerce, science and technology — in order to better understand exactly how we arrived where we are today.

## UNCLE BOB
Lent describes his second book, The Web of Meaning, as a long answer to an archetypal Uncle Bob — a [relative](## "parente") or [acquaintance](## "conoscente") we all have, whom he characterises as follows:
**Jeremy Lent (English accent):** If you can imagine if you’re in a [tea party](## "festicciola, riunione informale"), like a family party, and people are talking and somebody’s saying, “You know, the world can be different. We can make a better world. There’s all these things we can do and should do.” And then Uncle Bob [comes along](## "presentarsi, arrivare") into the conversation. And Uncle Bob is this person; we all know Uncle Bob, there’s always an Uncle Bob in our own families. And we all [have Uncle Bob within us](## "avere dentro di noi") a little bit, too. But Uncle Bob [comes along](## "presentarsi, arrivare") and says, “Let me tell you about it. When you’ve been around the world like I have, you kind of know that actually, all human beings are [selfish](## "egoisti"), and it’s a [rat race](## "competizione spietata") out there. And nature is just basically a matter of all these [selfish](## "egoisti") genes fighting against each other, and humans [turned out](## "rivelarsi") to be successful because we’re better at being competitive than the others. And the reason capitalism works is because it takes all that competitiveness and [harnesses](## "incanalare") it to make everybody more efficient. And that’s just the way it is, and we’ve got a few problems, sure, but this is the best way to solve things... is just by allowing our [selfish](## "egoisti")ness and capitalism to do its thing.” And everyone kind of [shuts down](## "bloccarsi"), and that’s the end of the conversation, right? Because what do you say to that?  

## THE HEDONIC TREADMILL
According to Jeremy, the whole world seems to agree with Uncle Bob. In fact, we’re taught right from our school years that the most important values in life are prestige, financial success, and status. School has been designed with the ultimate goal of turning people into the most efficient little [cogs](## "ingranaggi") in a destructive machine that makes money for a wealthy elite, instead of helping people to achieve a sense of real [fulfilment](## "realizzazione personale") in their lives.
**Jeremy Lent:** The problem is, with that way of living, sometimes called the ‘[hedonic treadmill](## "tapis roulant edonico")’. Obviously, we all know what a treadmill is, and the hedonic part means it’s always wanting us to feel that next bit of momentary pleasure. But the next time you feel it, you always then want the next one and the next one, so you’re never satisfied. And again, I think the opportunity is, each person has to come to their own moment of recognising that something’s wrong, recognising that this is actually not giving them deeper pleasure.

## THE HEDONISTIC TREADMILL
The treadmill or treadwheel was introduced into prisons in 1818 by the British engineer Sir William Cubitt (1785-1861). It was a a long wooden cylinder with [metal framing](## "struttura metallica") and with metal [steps](## "scalini") on the exterior. The idea was to occupy the [inmates](## "detenuti") of the prison in a useful way: if a prisoner put his weight onto one step, it moved [downwards](## "verso il basso"), turning the cylinder, so the prisoner would be forced to move onto the step above in a kind of infinite [staircase](## "scala"). The energy produced by the turning cylinder could be used, for example, to [grind](## "macinare") grain into [flour](## "farina"). It was extremely hard work for the prisoners, and to this day we often say we have to ‘go back to the treadmill’ if we have to return to a job we don’t like after the weekend or a holiday.

## COLLAPSE OR TECHNO SPLIT
Jeremy believes that if we continue [on our current path](## "su questa strada"), [striving](## "impegnarsi") for greater and greater success with more and more consumption and production, and measuring our growth with false measurements such as [GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)"), there [will](## "volontà") be three possible [outcomes](## "esiti"). The first scenario is the terrible possibility of the total collapse of our civilisation. The second is what he [terms](## "definire, chiamare") the ‘techno [split](## "divisione, frattura")’:
**Jeremy Lent:** There’s another scenario — I call that ‘techno [split](## "divisione, frattura")’ — which I think sometimes the elites secretly are expecting, which is similar to that first scenario, but it’s almost like a bifurcation of the human species where most people in the world do suffer some sense of collapse, a collapse of just any kind of normal way of living life, any kind of decent life. But this other small elite section, the ones that have their private jets and live in these kind of fortress communities right now, and basically keep themselves separated from everything else, they [end up](## "finire per") maintaining that sense of separation, and [end up](## "finire per") even genetically [figuring out](## "capire") how to become superhumans, and have some sort of techno utopia where they even [enhance](## "migliorare, potenziare") their own cognition and become part of some different kind of species entirely.

## A THIRD WAY
The third scenario is one of true [flour](## "farina")ishing, which Jeremy and many others are calling ‘an ecological civilisation’. This is a scenario where we learn to live in balance and our economy, our technology, and in fact our whole civilisation are designed not to just consume, but to actually create the conditions for all human beings to [flour](## "farina")ish on a regenerated earth. But is it possible?
**Jeremy Lent:** I believe it is, but the only way we’re going to get to that place is if we transform the very [foundations](## "fondamenta, basi") of our society right now. If we transform this growth-based capitalist system of extraction and exploitation to one that actually doesn’t even have to look that different. We’ll still have cities, we’ll still have marketplaces, we’ll still have technology and industry, and all the things that we actually really want and care about. But we’ll be able to live in this way where we all get to share in the abundance, where we actually have abundance that’s more publicly there for people to share rather than privately-owned behind big [fences](## "recinzioni"). And this is possible, but it involves looking at some of these deep [underlying](## "sottostanti") structural [flaws](## "difetti") of our current civilization and having the political [will](## "volontà") to change them.

## REACHING OUT TO YOUNG PEOPLE
With pressure from school, parents and [peers](## "pari, colleghi") to be successful in life, not to mention the influence of advertising and social media, it can be hard for young people to escape from the [hedonic treadmill](## "tapis roulant edonico"). But Jeremy has some [advice](## "consiglio"):
**Jeremy Lent:** The most important thing we can do for our children is to offer them the love that, as all human beings [do], they really want. To give them that secure foundation from which they themselves can learn to be curious, and learn to love themselves, and ultimately want themselves to feel fulfilled. As our children get older, I think one of the most important things we can do is really get them to understand they don’t have to get onto that [hedonic treadmill](## "tapis roulant edonico"). That they can be happy by really looking into themselves and [truly](## "veramente") fulfil their sense of who they are as people, and whatever that might be, however low status that might be in our dominant culture, to actually go ahead and [pursue](## "perseguire") that, and know that they can be loved by doing that. And I think if they can approach life in that way, then they [will](## "volontà") naturally [end up](## "finire per") really contributing to that movement towards an ecological civilisation, a [life-affirming](## "vitale") civilisation.

## WHAT TO DO NEXT
Anyone hoping for a positive outcome may feel [overwhelmed](## "sopraffatto") by the task ahead and a sense of terrible impotence, but The Web of Meaning is full of positive possibilities and Jeremy has some practical [advice](## "consiglio"):
**Jeremy Lent:** The thing that I would invite people to do is to realise that the future that’s [unfolding](## "dispiegarsi") is not a spectator sport. It’s not like we’re going into the stadium, supporting our team to win against the other. It’s actually something that we [are all engaged in](## "essere coinvolto in") ourselves. So every one of us together, by the decisions we make, the acts that we do, are actually co-creating the future ourselves. And once we begin to realise that, and we get to see that we don’t feel so [powerless](## "impotenti"), we actually can do something. None of us alone are going to change things in a way that we can see [measurably](## "in modo misurabile"). But as part of something bigger, we’re all going to [make a difference](## "fare la differenza"). And that brings a sense of both freedom, that none of us is going to be the one to be the saviour to change it all, but also a sense of responsibility.

## WHAT IS [GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)") GOOD FOR?
[Gross Domestic Product](## "Prodotto Interno Lordo") ([GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)")) is the primary, standard, and most common economic indicator used to measure the total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country. It was developed in the 1930s by economist Simon Kuznets, who immediately warned that it was “a potentially dangerous oversimplification that could be [misleading](## "fuorviante")” and “vulnerable to abuse.” 
The central problem with [GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)") as a measure of a country’s economic performance is that it does not distinguish between activities that promote [welfare](## "benessere") and those that diminish it. Under [GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)") accounting, any increase in economic activity — whether beneficial or [harmful](## "dannosa") — adds to the total. An [oil spill](## "sversamento di petrolio"), for example, raises [GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)") because of the cost of cleaning it up. In the description of one team of analysts: “By the curious standard of the [GDP](## "PIL (gross domestic product)"), the nation’s economic hero is a terminal cancer patient who is going through a costly divorce. The happiest event is an [earthquake](## "terremoto") or a hurricane.”
