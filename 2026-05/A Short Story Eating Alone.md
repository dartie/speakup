# A Short Story: Eating Alone   [C1]

Per il protagonista di questo racconto, il cibo non è solo nutrimento: è un piacere da condividere. Non trovando l'appoggio della moglie, cercherà compagnia in una tenera famiglia di procioni.

“There you are again,” said Greta. “What are you staring at the fridge for? This is not the Netflix catalogue for you to browse [for hours on end](## "per ore e ore"). Just pick something!” 
As usual, his wife was right. It might well have been the third or fourth time he had opened the fridge since getting home from work that afternoon.
The fridge was already beeping in warning, but he was unable to make up his mind. He was trying to give shape to an indefinite [craving](## "voglia"), assign a texture and a taste to whatever his brain was [nudging](## "spingere") him to want. Sweet or [savoury](## "salato")? At that time of the night (and at his age), the [sensible choice](## "scelta sensata") would have been a light snack, a salad, or a piece of fruit. But his anxiety pushed him towards something hyper-caloric and filling. More than [nourishment](## "nutrimento"), a night meal ought to be entertaining; crunchy, noisy, something he could eat with his hands, or [devour by the spoonful](## "divorare a cucchiaiate") from a large bowl on his [lap](## "grembo").
“Remember, I’m saving the yoghurt with muesli and [berries](## "frutti di bosco") for tomorrow, when I [break my fast](## "rompere il digiuno").”
Ever the judicious one, Greta had taken up intermittent fasting shortly after the twins left for college at the end of the summer. Almost nine months on, and she said she hadn’t felt so energised since before having the kids. He, meanwhile, remained devoutly committed to permanent breakfasting. And it showed in his [waistline](## "girovita").
He opted for the elaborate and prepared his favourite sandwich: pastrami and cheese with [pickle butter](## "burro aromatizzato ai cetriolini") on [wholegrain bread](## "pane integrale"). It had taken him years to perfect the recipe. “Culinary‑award material,” he thought as he grabbed a couple of beers from the fridge and headed out into the cool spring evening on his front porch.
It was peaceful on the quiet [cul‑de‑sac](## "vicolo cieco"), but he felt a [prick](## "fitta") of sadness in his chest. He disliked eating alone. He missed sharing meals with his wife and children. Food is not just [sustenance](## "sostentamento"), he thought. Food is meant to be shared. Otherwise, it’s just fuel.

Biting into his sandwich — wondering, not for the first time, what the Pulitzer for sandwiches might be called — and [sipping](## "sorseggiare") a not‑so‑cold beer in a [folding chair](## "sedia pieghevole"), he heard one of those nocturnal noises that could have been anything. Putting on his glasses, he saw shadows moving next to the [bins](## "bidoni della spazzatura").
“[Raccoons](## "procioni")!” he whispered to himself. It was a family of four, a parent and three [young](## "cuccioli"), [rummaging](## "frugare") through the [bins](## "bidoni della spazzatura") and sharing their discoveries between them: last night’s takeaway chicken‑wing bones, an apple core, an empty yoghurt container, some [overripe](## "troppo maturi") [berries](## "frutti di bosco").
Watching them eat brought him pure joy. When the parent [locked eyes](## "incrociare gli sguardi") with him and the three [offspring](## "prole") noticed, all five of them stared at one another for what seemed like a very long time. They [munched](## "divorare") in peace and he [chewed](## "masticare") [in awe](## "meravigliato"), feeling what could only be described as happiness.
The following night, he waited for them to show up again. And they did. He [slurped](## "risucchiare rumorosamente") instant noodles while they [nibbled](## "rosicchiare") [stale](## "duro, secco") bread, some [cheese rinds](## "croste di formaggio"), and more yoghurt. On the third night, he ate frozen pizza, and the [furry](## "pelosa") family helped themselves to some banana skins and the remains of a salad. On the third, the family looked at him in disappointment — there were hardly any [leftovers](## "avanzi") in the bin, only Greta’s usual pot of [goat](## "capra")’s yoghurt. They [licked it clean](## "leccare fino a lasciare pulito") and moved on up the road, disappearing into the dark.
The thought of the raccoon family eating someone else’s [leftovers](## "avanzi") filled him with a jealousy he hadn’t felt for decades. The next day after work, he went to the supermarket and prepared a splendid buffet. He googled what raccoons eat in the wild and tried his best to replicate their favourite diet: a bunch of grapes, four apples, two [corncobs](## "pannocchie"), six hard‑boiled eggs, and two cartons of orange juice.
But the raccoons wouldn’t touch the catering [laid out](## "disporre") on the [curb](## "bordo del marciapiede"). They went straight back to the [bins](## "bidoni della spazzatura"), then left again, disappointed.
Early the next night, while Greta was still watching some Netflix show or other, he dressed in a black sweater and [sweatpants](## "pantaloni della tuta") and went down the road. As silently as he could, he collected as many [leftovers](## "avanzi") as possible from his neighbours’ [bins](## "bidoni della spazzatura") and placed them in his own bin.
“Hey Rob! Don’t you live up in Catamount Valley?” asked Tony from across the lunchroom at the factory. Everyone turned their heads. “Haven’t you been [raided](## "assaltare, saccheggiare") by the Fattycoon yet?” he said, and [burst out laughing](## "scoppiare a ridere").
“What do you mean?” he asked. He was feeling groggy after five nights in a row [scavenging](## "rovistare") for [leftovers](## "avanzi") and waiting for the raccoons to come for dinner.
“Haven’t you seen the videos?” Tony replied, handing him his phone.
The original video was [blurry](## "sfocato"). Recorded at night, you could hardly discern in it the shape of an animal or a person, with its head in a bin. Someone had filmed the scene from a window and uploaded it with the caption, “[Raccoons](## "procioni") are gigantic now!!!” The news spread rapidly, and in a few hours dozens of memes and parodies [popped up](## "comparire all’improvviso") all over. The internet [jury was still out](## "non c’è ancora un verdetto") as to what the new creature should be called: the Fattycoon was an early favourite along with the [Humongous Snacker](## "Enorme Ghiottone"), [Potbelly Bigfoot](## "Bigfoot Panzuto"), and the [Masked Muncher](## "Divoratore Mascherato"), but Tinyhands McMassive was gaining traction.
That night, a handful of influencers arrived in the neighbourhood, carrying their phones with tripods and lights, hoping to [catch a glimpse](## "intravedere") of Tinyhands McMassive and [hit the viral jackpot](## "vincere la lotteria"). With all that attention, he knew the family would never return. For him, it was back to eating alone or else fasting in company.
