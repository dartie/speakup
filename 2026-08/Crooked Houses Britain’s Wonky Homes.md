The Spanish-American philosopher George Santayana once described England as being a “paradise of individuality, eccentricity, [heresy](## "eresia"), anomalies, hobbies, and humours.” It’s no surprise then, that the isle is home to an impressive collection of houses that look like they’ve had one too many at the local pub! Across the country, you’ll find houses that [tilt](## "pendere"), [lean](## "inclinarsi") and [sag](## "incurvarsi") in ways that would give any [surveyor](## "topografo") a headache. Here’s a tour of some of the best.

## SUFFOLK SWAYS
Suffolk is particularly [well-furnished](## "ben fornito") in [wonky](## "storte") houses. The culprit? Medieval ambition. A boom in the [wool trade](## "commercio della lana") meant houses had to go up fast, so builders used green, [unseasoned timber](## "legno non stagionato") that hadn’t been properly [dried](## "seccare"). Over the centuries the wood [warped](## "deformarsi"), and the buildings [warped](## "deformarsi") with it. The town of Lavenham is full of these houses, and like most others in Suffolk, they are coloured a joyful [rosy hue](## "sfumatura rosata") known as ‘Suffolk pink’. One of them [stands out](## "risaltare") for being orange and particularly welcoming: built in 1395 as part of a grand house for a wealthy wool merchant, past visitors include Queen Elizabeth I and John Lennon. You can book a tour to learn all about its six hundred-year old history.
More crooked houses in the medieval village of Lavenham.
Robert Louis Stevenson was so fascinated by the colourful contortions in Lavenham that he thought the houses “ought to be in a novel.” One of them even ended up in Godric’s Hollow, the magical village in the Harry Potter and the Deathly [Hallows](## "reliquie") film.

## WONKY BUILDINGS
Until recently, Staffordshire’s Crooked House was Britain’s most famous [wonky](## "storte") building. The pub was so badly affected by [subsidence](## "cedimento") that its [lean](## "inclinarsi")ing walls gave rise to optical illusions, such as bottles appearing to [roll upwards](## "rotolare verso l’alto") along tables, which is either a great party trick or a sign you should probably drink somewhere else! It burnt down in suspicious circumstances in 2023 and was then demolished, but South Staffordshire Council has demanded the owner rebuild it exactly as it was. That legal fight is still [ongoing](## "in corso").

Windsor has its own 17th-century crooked house. It sits on the corner of Market Street and has a very confident [lean](## "inclinarsi"). Like the Suffolk houses, the cause is unseasoned green [oak](## "quercia").

## A LITTLE TOO TIPSY
The House of the Golden Key in Tewkesbury has a different problem: it [tips forward](## "inclinarsi in avanti") over the street at an angle that makes [passers-by](## "passanti") slightly nervous. The [tilt](## "pendere") happened in the 17th century when an attempt to add a [storey](## "piano") caused the [ridge piece](## "trave di colmo") to [slip](## "scivolare"). It has stayed that way ever since.

And then there’s Little Moreton Hall in Cheshire, a grand Tudor [manor house](## "casa padronale") that is slowly collapsing on its south side, having been built with virtually no [foundations](## "fondamenta"). [Iron rods](## "barre di ferro") were inserted in the 19th century to slow things down. The National Trust now notes that the [fireplace](## "camino") in the Long Gallery may be the only truly vertical thing in the entire building.
