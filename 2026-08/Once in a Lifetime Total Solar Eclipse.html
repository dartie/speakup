Once in a Lifetime: Total Solar Eclipse [B2]

Hai in programma un viaggio in Spagna? Il 12 agosto, in alcune zone del Paese, sarà possibile assistere a un rarissimo evento astronomico: un’eclissi solare totale.

Whoever happens to be in Northern Spain on 12 August will be fortunate enough to witness a rare natural event. For a brief moment, day will become night; darkness will fall as the Moon will completely cover the Sun. This phenomenon is called a ‘total solar eclipse’ and it is the first time one will be visible in Spain since 1905. What’s more, it is the first of three Iberian eclipses that will occur between now and 2028. To mark the occasion, the European Space Agency (ESA) will broadcast the event live from the Astrophysical Observatory of Javalambre in Teruel, Aragon. The scientific community is also organising a host of activities to engage the public.

THE TWILIGHT EXPERIENCE

A total solar eclipse refers to the complete alignment of the Sun and the Moon, as seen from Earth. When the Moon passes directly between Earth and the Sun, it casts a dark shadow on the Earth’s surface. The sky darkens as if it were twilight, revealing the Sun’s glowing corona. To experience complete darkness, you must be standing exactly in the path of the Moon’s shadow. This is called the ‘path of totality’. Outside of it, you will only see a partial eclipse, where the Sun appears as a crescent. While a total solar eclipse occurs somewhere on the planet approximately every eighteen months, seeing one is incredibly rare. The path of totality is narrow and often falls over oceans. A total eclipse is usually only seen from any point on Earth once every three hundred and fifty to four hundred years. Current Affairs

SENSATIONAL SUNSET

This particular solar eclipse takes place on the evening of Wednesday, 12 August. The path of totality ends in Spain, crossing the country from west to east. It passes through several major cities, including A Coruña, Oviedo, León, Bilbao, Zaragoza, Valencia, and Palma. It reaches totality in Mallorca between 8:31pm and 8:33pm, just as the Sun dips close to the horizon. Sunsets do not get more spectacular than this! A word of warning, though: looking directly at the Sun can cause serious eye damage. To watch the eclipse safely, always use certified eclipse glasses. Only during the brief moment of totality is it safe to look without protection.

MORE OPPORTUNITIES

Professor Carole Mundell, ESA Director of Science, captures the excitement of the event: “A total solar eclipse is one of those rare moments when millions of people can look up together and feel both wonder and curiosity. It is a shared moment that connects us to the universe and reminds us that the desire to explore and understand is one of humanity’s greatest strengths.” If you miss it, don’t worry. The next total solar eclipse visible in Spain will take place on 2 August 2027, followed by an annular eclipse on 26 January 2028. In Italy, however, these events will be more limited: both the 2027 total eclipse and the 2028 annular eclipse will only be visible as partial eclipses across most of the country, with totality in 2027 passing just south of Sicily and missing the mainland altogether. www.trioeclipses.es
