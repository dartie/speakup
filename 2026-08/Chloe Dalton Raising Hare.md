On a snowy January day during the Covid lockdown, Chloe Dalton, a high-level political advisor, found a [leveret](## "leprotto") (a baby [hare](## "lepre")) abandoned near her home in the country[side](## "inciso, parentesi") of northern England. The animal was [tiny](## "piccolissimo"), probably no more than a day old. Dalton was unsure what to do. Should she leave the [leveret](## "leprotto") and hope its mother returned? Should she take the [leveret](## "leprotto") home and try to look after it? Was it right to take a wild animal into a domestic space? Eventually, Dalton did decide to take the [tiny](## "piccolissimo") animal home with her. It was a decision that would change her life profoundly.

## THROUGH A HARE’S EYES
For Dalton, sharing her home with a growing [hare](## "lepre"), as she prepared the animal to return to the wild, involved adjusting her routines and travel plans, and [rearranging](## "riorganizzare") her house and garden. But not only did practical things change; Dalton found her whole relationship with nature was shifting. She began to look at the natural world through a [hare](## "lepre")’s eyes, noticing things that had been invisible before. A new [awareness](## "consapevolezza") of the wild plants and animals around her brought an unexpected joy. And, when lockdown ended, Dalton’s [commitment](## "impegno") to the [hare](## "lepre") forced her to question her priorities in a way that took her by surprise.
Hans Hoffmann’s illustration A Hare in the Forest from the 16th-century.

## RESEARCH
Dalton has spent most of her career working with high-level British politicians as an advisor and speech writer. This often involves travelling to conflict zones around the world. She [acknowledges](## "riconoscere") that for most of her life she has been a city person, spending more time in London and in airports than in the country[side](## "inciso, parentesi") where she grew up. So she didn’t feel at all prepared for the unexpected challenge of looking after a wild animal in her home. The responsibility worried, even obsessed her. In response, she used her research skills to find out all she could about [hare](## "lepre")s — in books and online.

## A RICH MYTHOLOGY
As well as practical information about what kind of food and [shelter](## "rifugio") the [hare](## "lepre") needed, and how to deal with [paw](## "zampa") [injuries](## "ferite"), Dalton discovered a rich and complex mythology surrounding [hare](## "lepre")s. For example, [hare](## "lepre")s have been associated with [witchcraft](## "stregoneria"); in the Middle Ages, it was believed that witches would [shape-shift](## "trasformarsi") into [hare](## "lepre")s. In other traditions, meanwhile, [hare](## "lepre")s symbolised innocence and renewal: seeing a [hare](## "lepre") was thought to be lucky. Dalton is intrigued at why the [hare](## "lepre") occupies such a prominent place in our cultural imagination even though most of us know very little about them in practice.
[Raising](## "allevare, crescere") Hare, Dalton’s first book, is a deeply personal [account](## "testimonianza") of how she raised the [hare](## "lepre") to adulthood and what happened next. In it she describes the challenges, both practical and emotional, of caring for a wild animal in her home. And she explores how her own relationship with the nature around her has changed, giving a detailed [hare](## "lepre")’s-eye-view of the plants and animals of the English country[side](## "inciso, parentesi").

## RAISING HARE
The title [Raising](## "allevare, crescere") Hare is a play on the expression ‘[hair-raising](## "da far rizzare i capelli")’, which means scary or [awe-inspiring](## "impressionante"). Although ‘[hare](## "lepre")’ and ‘hair’ are spelled differently they are pronounced in exactly the same way — they are homophones. For Dalton, the experience of raising a [hare](## "lepre") was often [hair-raising](## "da far rizzare i capelli").

## BACK TO BASICS
Chloe Dalton met with Speak Up to talk about her experiences of looking after the [hare](## "lepre"), engaging with nature in a new way, and about recording those experiences in her book. To write [Raising](## "allevare, crescere") Hare, Dalton had to research things that were completely new to her and she needed to find a whole new vocabulary to express them.
**Chloe Dalton (English accent):** My understanding completely changed because I didn’t know anything about [hare](## "lepre")s. I never thought about the difference between a [rabbit](## "coniglio") and a [hare](## "lepre"), I didn’t understand the different species, that their lives are completely different, that their relationship to humans is completely different. Part of the effect of this experience was for the first time I needed words to describe nature and I realized I didn’t even have vocabulary for colour, that the words to describe what I was observing, even to describe the [fur](## "pelliccia") of the [hare](## "lepre")... and I loved the process of trying to learn about colour and nature and ask the stupid questions that you think you can never ask, like, “What is grass? And what is a rainbow?” And you know, the things that if you turned around and said to a friend in a bar, they would just laugh at you. And I had this opportunity to almost rediscover that innocence of learning about nature, admitting I didn’t know very much, trying to imagine the world through the eyes of a [hare](## "lepre") and in the process seeing the world completely differently.

## LIFE-CHANGING
When Dalton first brought the baby [hare](## "lepre") home, it lived in her office. She changed her habits to [avoid](## "evitare") making a noise or doing anything to disturb it. But even when the [hare](## "lepre") eventually moved out[side](## "inciso, parentesi"), she realised her habits had been changed forever.
**Chloe Dalton:** I’ve acquired some strange habits. One is that I still don’t turn the lights on at night very often, and so if I have friends come to stay with me, they find it very confusing that I’ve got into this habit of just not automatically turning the lights on, because for four years I wouldn’t put the lights on in order not to interfere with the night vision of the [hare](## "lepre"). So that’s just a humorous [side](## "inciso, parentesi"), but some of my habits have definitely changed, even if they’re [slightly](## "leggermente") unusual habits. And I still mark the passage of the hours by the movement of the [hare](## "lepre")s. When I’m at home, there are certain times of day where I leave the out[side](## "inciso, parentesi") to the [hare](## "lepre")s. I don’t disturb them. And then when I know they’ve gone to rest, then I [go out and about](## "andare in giro"). And again, it’s [slightly](## "leggermente") eccentric, but it’s a small adjustment and it makes me happy. Like the early morning hours are the hours where the [hare](## "lepre")s are very active and I watch them from a distance. So yes, there’s a mixture of emotional change and also practical changes in my habits.

## A NEW VOICE
Dalton already has plenty of experience writing political speeches for other people. It is [high-stakes](## "di grande importanza"), challenging work. But in an extraordinary way it is the silent [hare](## "lepre") who has helped her find a new voice as a writer [in her own right](## "a pieno titolo").
**Chloe Dalton:** I could never have imagined this because I never saw myself as someone who could write a book. I made my way in the world writing for other people who had really big, important jobs. And I thought it was an incredible privilege, which it was, to write for them, you know, to write for the British [Foreign Secretary](## "ministro degli Affari Esteri") at the time, to have things that I had written, spoken, you know, at the State Department or the UN Security Council or by other big international figures... [it] felt like all I could possibly ever dream of achieving. But I write in the book about how I realised at some point in this experience that I’d lived my life a little bit like a [hare](## "lepre"). I’d been a bit camouflaged, a bit too hidden. And this is a story in part about how an [utterly](## "totalmente") silent creature helped me find my own voice. And I think lots of different people at different stages in their life can [relate to](## "identificarsi con") that feeling, that you don’t realise perhaps there’s a change that you need.

## ADVOCATING FOR NATURE
Despite the new direction as a writer, Dalton does not want to abandon politics completely, as she believes it has a role in [addressing](## "affrontare, trattare") urgent [issues](## "questioni, problematiche"), including the biodiversity crisis.
**Chloe Dalton:** Part of me is still very strongly [drawn towards](## "sentirsi attratto da") politics. It’s hard when you care about certain [issues](## "questioni, problematiche") to completely step away when you see, you know, for instance, some of the [issues](## "questioni, problematiche") that I worked on in government have been [undone](## "annullare"), quite apart from the new things that have happened in the world that we’re seeing at the moment. And it’s just that reminder, you know, progress isn’t linear, you can’t just [take it for granted](## "dare per scontato"). So I feel a bit [torn](## "combattuta"), I definitely want to write more and follow my interest in animals... I think of it as almost the [hare](## "lepre") left me these [tracks](## "impronte") in the landscape and I’m now following them. But sometimes I think at some point I might put down my pen and go back and be a bit more active in politics because there’s a lot to fight for.
