With its [other-worldly](## "di un altro mondo") landscape, unique culture and [picture-perfect](## "da cartolina") coastline, the Isle of Skye is the most-visited of the nine hundred or so islands that comprise the Hebrides. Skye [juts out](## "sporgere") from the west coast of [mainland Scotland](## "Scozia continentale") like a [rugged](## "aspra, selvaggia") [lobster claw](## "chela d’aragosta") or [winged creature](## "creatura alata") (its Gaelic name An t-Eilean Sgitheanach translates as The Winged Isle). Named after the [Norse](## "norrena") word for ‘cloud’ (skuy), the Isle of Skye is often [wrapped](## "avvolgere") in [low-hanging mist](## "nebbia bassa") or [sea fog](## "nebbia marina") (known as haar), adding to its sense of mystery.

## ARRIVALS
Along with Mull, Islay, Jura, and other smaller isles, Skye forms part of the Inner Hebrides archipelago. To the west, further out in the wild Atlantic Ocean, the Outer Hebrides include Lewis and Harris, Barra and Vatersay, and the uninhabited beauty of St. Kilda. Skye was [settled](## "abitare, stabilirsi") at least 11,500 years ago, with evidence of hunting camps, stone tools and [chambered tombs](## "tombe a camera"). In the first century BC, Gaelic-speaking Irish settlers arrived, followed by Vikings from the 9th century AD [onwards](## "in poi"). Today, the island is home to a population of 13,000 people — and more than one hundred thousand sheep.
A view over Old Man of Storr, a fifty-metre-high petrified lava pinnacle with a cliff behind it that overlooks the town of Portree.

## COLOURFUL CAPITAL
Around 650,000 visitors travel to Skye each year, looking for wildness, wonder, and the difference that Skye offers in abundance. Since 1995, the island has been attached to the Scottish mainland by the Skye Road B[ridge](## "cresta"), making it an easy day trip for tourist [coaches](## "pullman"). But many people still prefer to travel the slow way, by ferry, crossing in half an hour from Mallaig on the mainland to Armadale in the southwest of Skye. A shorter crossing from Glenelg uses a rare [turntable ferry](## "traghetto con piattaforma girevole"). Rotating manually, so that cars can drive on without needing to [reverse off](## "uscire in retromarcia"), the [MV](## "nave a motore ('motor vessel')") Glenachulish is the only one of its kind in the world. An hour’s drive north brings visitors to Portree, capital of Skye, where colourful houses line the harbour alongside active fishing boats. Its shops, cafés, restaurants, and places to stay are about as [busy](## "animati, affollati") as life gets on Skye.

## WILDNESS
Despite the large number of visitors, Skye contains many peaceful, hidden corners. The island is 80 kilometres long (115 kilometres by road) but its coastline is so [heavily indented](## "molto frastagliata") that wildness, and the sea, are never far away. In the northwest, the [lighthouse](## "faro") at Neist Point features views of [towering](## "imponenti") sea [cliffs](## "scogliere") and a chance to [spot](## "avvistare") sea eagles, whales, and dolphins. In the south, the peninsula of Elgol and Camasunary Bay offer access to remote beaches and panoramic views of the Cuillin mountains. Though popular with walkers and [climbers](## "scalatori"), the iconic Black Cuillin [ridge](## "cresta") is not for the inexperienced, with [razor-edged](## "affilati come una lama") paths, challenging [scrambles](## "arrampicate") and [steep falls](## "cadute ripide") on all sides. The nearby Red Cuillin are tough, but less intimidating.

## PREHISTORIC
Located in the northeast of Skye, the Trotternish peninsula is home to some of the island’s more [breathtaking](## "mozzafiato") and unusual [scenery](## "paesaggi"). Most famous is the Old Man of Storr, an eroded volcanic [sentinel](## "sentinella") looking out to sea. On the edge of Staffin Bay, the Quiraing is an unreal landscape of dramatic [cliffs](## "scogliere"), [grassy plateaus](## "altipiani erbosi"), and [towering](## "imponenti") rock pinnacles offering panoramic views across the island. If it all looks a bit prehistoric, that’s no surprise: dinosaur fossils and footprints have been found all around this area.
A fossilised dinosaur footprint on the rocky shore of An Corran Beach in Staffin, Isle of Skye.

## GEOLOGIST’S DREAM
With nearly three billion years of ancient rock record on show, the Isle of Skye is a geologist’s dream. It offers a unique combination of dramatic volcanic landscapes, including the gabbro and granite Cuillin mountains, [layered](## "stratificato") basaltic plateau, and Jurassic [fossil beds](## "giacimenti fossili"). Skye is an epicentre of volcanic activity and the Cuillin are the eroded remains of a sixty-million-year-old magma chamber. In the southeast, the Lewisian Gneiss is almost three billion years old, while the north shows the iconic basalt columns of Kilt Rock and massive [landslips](## "frane") of the Quiraing, still moving today. Like a visible geological map, its sedimentary, metamorphic, and igneous rocks [layered](## "stratificato") together, Skye reveals the forces that have shaped our planet.

## CONTRASTS
Skye is a place of contrasts, and not just in its geology. Looking more like the Caribbean than Scotland, Coral Beach near Dunvegan Castle has a turquoise sea and a [sun-bleached](## "scolorita dal sole") white beach made of crushed, calcified [seaweed](## "alghe"). Further south, close to the implacable Black Cuillin, Glen Brittle provides access to the mythical Fairy Pools: crystal-clear, cold blue [waterfalls](## "cascate") and water pools on the River Brittle. In 1601, this was the site of a battle between the island’s two dominant clans: the MacLeods and the MacDonalds. The MacDonalds were said to have won, but after such a bloody encounter there were few victors.

## THE SKYE BOAT SONG
“Speed, bonnie boat, like a bird on the wing / Onward, the sailors cry! / Carry the [lad](## "ragazzo") that’s born to be King / Over the sea to Skye.” Combining history, heroism and myth, the Skye Boat Song derives from an ancient Gaelic rowing song. Its lyrics, from the late 19th century, describe how Charles Edward Stuart, ‘Bonnie Prince Charlie’, escaped in a small boat after the terrible defeat by the English at the Battle of Culloden in 1746. Disguised as a [serving maid](## "domestica"), he was hidden by the heroic young Flora MacDonald. The song has been rewritten many times through the years; it was used as a theme song for the TV series Outlander, and played by [bagpipers](## "suonatori di cornamusa") at the funeral of the late Queen Elizabeth II.

## GAELIC CULTURE
Skye is a [heartland](## "cuore") of Gaelic-speaking culture through language, storytelling, music and tradition. The southern peninsula of Sleat is home to Sabhal Mòr Ostaig, the National Centre for Gaelic Language and Culture. This is the world’s only higher education institution providing learning programmes entirely in Gaelic. Skye retains a strong community culture built on connections to the land, especially through crofting. For any visitor, it’s worth taking time to connect with the resident Skye community — and stopping to breathe in the sea air, the [raw](## "selvaggia") beauty and the [untamed](## "indomito") spirit of this unique and magical island.
