George Washington: The Father of the United States [B2]

George Washington guidò le Tredici Colonie alla vittoria contro i britannici nella Guerra d’indipendenza americana. Successivamente divenne il primo presidente dei nuovi Stati Uniti d’America. Tuttavia, la sua reputazione è ancora macchiata dalla schiavitù.

George Washington was one of the Founding Fathers of the United States of America. He played a foundational role in the birth and early development of this new country based on republican ideals. His life, achievements and legacy have made him one of the most respected figures in world history. However, his indiscriminate killing of Native Americans and his enslavement of hundreds of African American men, women, and children are a permanent stain on his character.

PROSPEROUS BEGINNINGS

Washington was born on 2 February 1732, in Westmoreland, Virginia, into a prosperous tobacco-growing family. His education ended at eleven when his father died. He then trained as a surveyor in the frontier lands of the British colony, while working as a farmer on the plantation Mount Vernon.

The bosom of America is open to receive not only the opulent and respectable stranger, but the oppressed and persecuted of all nations and religions; whom we shall wellcome to a participation of all our rights and previleges, if by decency and propriety of conduct they appear to merit the enjoyment.

Washington, however, had ambitions to be a soldier. He participated as a commander in the local militia in the French and Indian War (1754-63), the American part of Europe’s Seven Years’ War, in which Britain defeated France and its Native American allies. He gained valuable leadership experience and a reputation for courage.  Washington Crossing the Delaware by Emanuel Leutze painted in 1851. This large-scale painting (approximately 3.8m by 6.4m) depicts Washington leading his troops across the icy Delaware River before a surprise attack in 1776, symbolising courage and determination during the American Revolution.

LOCAL POLITICS

He then decided to enter local politics. He strongly opposed Britain imposing taxes on the colonies, “without representation”, to pay for the country’s war debt. He became a leading figure in the Thirteen Colonies over the next ten years. Years of increasing anger with British rule culminated in the outbreak of the American Revolutionary War in April 1775. He transformed a rag-tag militia into a powerful fighting force that would eventually defeat the British in 1781 with French and Spanish help. Washington was not a great strategist in open battle, but he became expert in brief, daring, lightning attacks, and cleverly kept his army moving and intact. 

THE FIRST PRESIDENT

To general surprise, the most powerful man in the US then resigned his commission, giving up power and returning to his farm. By turning his back on absolute power, Washington helped strengthen the principles of America’s nascent republic. Over the next few years, however, the new country struggled with tax collection and debt payment. Washington decided a new, strong constitution was needed. Under his leadership, the US Constitution was ratified in 1788. The country’s supreme law, it established a federal government based on the separation of powers into legislative, executive, and judicial branches. His hopes of retiring to private life were again dashed when he was elected unanimously by the Electoral College as the first president of the United States in 1789.

POLITICAL DIVISION

Washington had to face numerous problems, including economic instability, foreign conflicts and internal unrest. Trying to administer the government with fairness and integrity, he worked with his colleagues to resolve a debt crisis, sign treaties with Native American tribes, and designate a site on the Potomac River for a permanent capital of the new country. Washington knew that everything he did would set a precedent for future occupants of his position — but he was writing the rules as he went along.

I hold the maxim no less applicable to public than to private affairs, that honesty is always the best policy.

In 1793, Washington was again elected unanimously as President. He had to navigate complex political divisions as factions emerged, soon to become political parties. In foreign affairs, he adopted neutrality, which allowed the country to grow through commerce and westward expansion. He oversaw the ratification of the Bill of Rights, the creation of the first bank of the United States, and the planned entry of five new states. His decision in 1797 to retire completely from politics established a limit of two terms for the presidency. Equally important, the transfer of power was peaceful. 

A TOWERING FIGURE

Washington only had two years to enjoy his retirement. He died of complications following a throat infection on 14 December 1799. His achievements were extraordinary. He had played a fundamental, stabilising role in the creation and survival of a new nation, while helping to shape a national identity. His image of honesty and moral uprightness gave legitimacy to a new and untested system of government. His colleague General Henry Lee spoke for most Americans when he said: “First in war, first in peace, and first in the hearts of his countrymen.”

MYTH, POWER AND CONTRADICTION

George Washington is an American icon, and several myths helped build up the image of a saintly character, but criticism actually began during his lifetime and has continued ever since. In 1796, the famous English political pamphleteer Thomas Paine published an angry letter calling him an incompetent general and a hypocrite. Over the years, many books have talked about his integrity, sense of duty and “commitment to the public good.” That public good, however, did not include America’s African American population. Slavery at that time was an integral part of the US economy, and Washington actually inherited ten slaves from his father. In later life, Washington was mildly critical of slavery, but he did little to change the status quo, possibly for fear of fracturing the young republic. It seems he treated his slaves well, however, and refused to sell them, as it would break up families, and his will freed the 123 slaves that he owned upon his death.  In 1794, he pushed Congress to enact a first, partial ban of the slave trade. On the other hand, he was relentless in trying to recapture slaves who had escaped from his plantation. And some of the teeth in his dentures were pulled from the mouths of slaves, a typical practice at that time. More African Americans fought on the British side in the War of Independence than on the American side. They were fighting for liberty against their white masters. There was actually an Ethiopian Regiment, which included one of Washington’s own slaves. Around twenty thousand African Americans left for England with the British at the end of the war.  Washington felt that Native Americans had to be civilised in the European style if they were to survive in the new nation. He also felt that they were a major obstacle to the growth and wealth that the country could obtain through expansion to the West. His actions, however, were often shockingly brutal. During the Revolutionary War, in 1779, he ordered his generals to carry out a scorched-earth campaign against the Iroquois, who had allied with the British. This meant the total destruction of at least forty villages and their food supplies. Mass starvation and disease killed hundreds of men, women and children. The tribe called Washington the Town Destroyer.
