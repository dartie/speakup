What makes a song memorable? As part of a project exploring musical memory and its links to [conditions](## "patologie, disturbi") such as dementia, researchers collaborating with Manchester’s Museum of Science and Industry launched an experiment to find the most recognisable songs of all time. They produced four interactive games where listeners played against the clock to [guess](## "indovinare") song titles, and then gathered data from over twelve thousand participants, whose responses took an average of five seconds.
Hoping to find the shared characteristics that make a song an [earworm](## "tormentone, tarlo musicale"), [Dutch](## "neerlandese") musicologist Ashley Burgoyne, who designed the game, concluded “very strong melodic [hooks](## "ritornelli") seem to be the most memorable for people” and is continuing to study the therapeutic benefits. 
The museum compiled a list of [the catchiest](## "le più orecchiabili") songs of all time — including the average time participants took to recognise each tune —, making them ideal material for improving your English. 

## 1. WANNABE, SPICE GIRLS  (1996, 2.29 SECONDS)
The best-selling single worldwide by an all-female group, it took listeners just 2.29 seconds to recognise English group Spice Girls’ debut single Wannabe. A global Number One, its lyrics are about the importance of friendship and feature contributions from all five members. It opens with Geri Halliwell’s infectious laugh and the call-and-response “Tell me what you want”, and also uses mixed conditionals and imperatives as well as the [nonsense](## "senza senso") exclamation “zigazig ah”. The band [claimed](## "affermare") this is “whatever you want it to be.”
If you wanna be my lover, you gotta get with my friends
Make it last forever,
friendship never ends
If you wanna be my lover,
you have got to give
Taking is too easy, but that’s the way it is

## 2. MAMBO NO. 5 (A LITTLE BIT), LOU BEGA (1999, 2.48 SECONDS)
When Ugandan-Italian singer Lou Bega sampled Cuban musician Pérez Prado’s 1950 Mambo No.5, he kept the title but added his own lyrics to the original instrumental. It was a huge summer hit, selling millions and spending a record twenty weeks at Number One in France. The song uses the imperative to [outline](## "illustrare") dance movements but is primarily a list of women’s names Bega said were from his past. His favourite? “Sandra, that’s why she was the one in the sun.” 
Jump up and down and move it all around
Shake your head to the sound
Put your hand on the ground[Take one step](## "fare un passo") left and one step right

## 3. EYE OF THE TIGER, SURVIVOR (1982, 2.62 SECONDS)
The [theme song](## "colonna sonora") from the boxing blockbuster Rocky III, Eye of the Tiger’s iconic dead guitar riff was written to perfectly synch with the [punches](## "colpi") thrown in the movie’s opening sequence. The film’s director and protagonist Sylvester Stallone was unable to [secure](## "ottenere") publishing rights for Queen’s Another One Bites the [Dust](## "polvere"), so [commissioned](## "incaricare") this track from American rock band Survivor. [Drawing heavily on](## "ispirarsi a") the film’s storyline, the lyrics follow a boxer’s intense training sessions and the song has become a global sporting [anthem](## "inno").
Risin’ up, back on the street
Did my time, [took my chances](## "rischiare")
Went the distance
Now I’m back on my feet
Just a man and his [will](## "volontà") to survive

## 4. JUST DANCE, LADY GAGA (2008, 2.66 SECONDS)
American superstar Lady Gaga’s debut single was written in a Los Angeles studio in just ten minutes, following a [farewell](## "addio") party in New York the night before. Feeling a little [under the weather](## "indisposta, sotto tono"), she [recalled](## "ricordare") the previous evening’s energy to produce “a happy record” about losing your inhibitions and forgetting your worries on the dance floor, with lyrics mostly in the present simple. It is one of the best-selling digital singles of all time.
What’s going on on the floor?
I love this record baby but I can’t [see straight](## "vedere chiaramente") anymore
Keep it cool, what’s the name of this club?
I can’t remember but it’s alright, a-alright

## 5. S.O.S., ABBA (1975, 2.73 SECONDS)
Swedish pop group ABBA [struggled](## "faticare") to follow their global success with 1974’s Eurovision-winning Waterloo, releasing a [slew](## "serie") of unsuccessful singles. S.O.S. marked the beginning of a hugely successful [run](## "serie") of hits where they became one of the defining bands of pop music. About a relationship [falling apart](## "andare in pezzi"), the lyrics use the past simple to evoke happier times with question forms to reflect the anguish of breaking up. Many consider it the band’s best song and its opening piano intro even influenced the Sex Pistols’ punk-classic Pretty [Vacant](## "vuoto"). Uniquely in pop music, both the song title and artist are palindromes.
So when you’re near me, darling,
Can’t you hear me S.O.S.?
The love you gave me,
Nothing else can save me, S.O.S.
When you’re gone,
How can I even try to go on?
When you’re gone, though I try,
How can I carry on?

## 6.OH, PRETTY WOMAN, ROY ORBISON (1964, 2.73 SECONDS)
American singer-songwriter Roy Orbison’s signature hit was inspired by a [throwaway](## "insignificante") comment from his songwriting partner Bill Dees: when Orbison asked his wife if she had enough cash for shopping, Dees said “a pretty woman never needs any money.” Lyrically the song describes the singer’s admiration for a beautiful woman, using the present simple and the modals ‘would’ and ‘could’. A huge hit in the 60s, it was given [a new lease of life](## "una nuova vita") in 1990 when it was used as the [theme song](## "colonna sonora") of the movie of the same name.
Pretty woman walkin’ down the street
Pretty woman, the kind I’d like to meet
Pretty woman, I don’t believe you,
You’re not the truth
No one could look as good as you[Mercy](## "misericordia, pietà")

## 7. BEAT IT, MICHAEL JACKSON (1982, 2.80 SECONDS)
When producer Quincy Jones suggested to Michael Jackson that he write a song like The [Knack](## "talento")’s My Sharona, the King of Pop came up with “the type of rock song that I would buy.” He then [enlisted](## "arruolare") rock legend Eddie Van Halen for one of the 1980s’ best-known guitar solos and produced an iconic choreographed music video, making Beat It a massive worldwide hit. The lyrics describe [gangland](## "mondo della malavita") rivalry and are almost entirely in the present simple. 
They told him, “Don’t you ever come around here”
“Don’t wanna see your face,
You better disappear”
The fire’s in their eyes
And their words are really clear
So [beat it](## "sparire"), just [beat it](## "sparire")

## 8. I WILL ALWAYS LOVE YOU, WHITNEY HOUSTON (1992, 2.83 SECONDS)
The best-selling single ever by a female artist, this was recorded for Whitney Houston’s film debut The Bodyguard. A cover of a 1974 Dolly Parton song, it was originally written as a [farewell](## "addio") to the singer’s manager, but in Whitney’s version becomes a powerful ode to devotion. Opening with an example of the first conditional with ‘should’, the song largely uses the future ‘[will](## "volontà")’, with initially [restrained](## "contenute") a cappella vocals slowly [giving way](## "lasciare spazio") to the pop-diva’s [soaring](## "salire") dramatic high notes. A huge global hit, it spent a then-record fourteen weeks at the top of the US charts.
If I should stay
I would only be in your way
So I’ll go, but I know
I’ll think of you every step of the way

## 9. DON’T YOU WANT ME, THE HUMAN LEAGUE (1981, 2.83 SECONDS)
English band The Human League were pioneers in electronic sound and this is considered the first hit song to use a [drum machine](## "batteria elettronica"). Although the band were [reluctant](## "restia") to release it, feeling it was too mainstream, it became a huge hit in the UK and, thanks to heavy rotation on the newly-launched MTV, all over the world. Inspired by a story in a teen’s magazine, singer Phil Oakey [claimed](## "affermare") the song is about a couple’s power politics. Opening with the past simple, the song also uses the past continuous and present perfect. 
You were working as a waitress in a cocktail bar
When I met you
I [picked you out](## "scegliere"), I [shook you up](## "sconvolgere")
And [turned you around](## "trasformare")
Turned you into someone new

## 10. I DON’T WANT TO MISS A THING, AEROSMITH (1998, 2.84 SECONDS)
This is the theme from blockbuster disaster movie Armageddon. Veteran songwriter Dianne Warren originally [envisaged](## "immaginare") this power ballad being performed by Celine Dion, but producers first approached U2, who declined, and then the film’s star Liv Tyler’s father’s band Aerosmith. It became their best-known song and only US Number One. Inspired by a quote from Barbra Streisand’s husband, who said he missed his wife when they were asleep, the song uses the [slang](## "gergale") ‘wanna’ (‘want to’) and the conditional ‘would’ clause.
Don’t wanna close my eyes
I don’t want to fall asleep
‘Cause I’d miss you baby
And I don’t want to miss a thing
