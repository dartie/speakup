We are all born naked. But most of us are only comfortable being naked when alone or in private situations. However, millions of people are also comfortable being naked in public, and many of them participate in naturism.

## HARMONY WITH NATURE
De[fine](## "multa")d by the International Naturist Federation as “a way of life in harmony with nature characterised by the practice of communal nudity with the intention of encouraging self-respect, respect for others and for the environment,” the naturism movement began in Germany around the early 1900s, and soon [spread](## "diffondersi") to other parts of the world. A protest against rigid social norms, it promoted an appreciation of the human body through nudity, holistic health, and harmony with nature. Today, there are innumerable global organisations that organise nude events and activities; everything from card games to camping trips. These take place on beaches, in parks and in other places where public nudity is allowed.

## NOT A CRIME
James Shuttleworth is the International Youth Officer with the International Naturist Federation (INF). The INF is the global umbrella organisation for naturist and nudist associations and promotes the recognition of the human right to nudity in a natural environment. In Britain, where Shuttleworth lives, [unlike](## "a differenza di") in some countries, public nudity is generally not a criminal offence, and he encourages anyone interested in participating in naturism to become a member of the federation and help promote the acceptance of public nudity.

## CLOSER TO NATURE
To find out more, Speak Up met with Shuttleworth. He began by clarifying the distinction between naturism and nudism, two terms that are often used [interchangeably](## "indistintamente").
**James Shuttleworth (English accent):** They are [intermingled](## "mescolati, intrecciati"), very much so. Naturism, it’s about being closer to nature. It’s all about the acceptance of oneself and others around you, you know, ‘cause [at the end of the day](## "in fin dei conti"), we’re all born completely different, aren’t we? And so [whatever](## "qualsiasi siano") your insecurities are, they just [melt away](## "svanire, dissolversi").

## ANY BODY CAN DO IT
[Pushing back against](## "opporsi a, contra[stare](## "fissare")") common assumptions, Shuttleworth insists that naturism is not only for exhibitionists or people with conventionally perfect bodies.
**James Shuttleworth:** Pretty much anybody can do it. Everyone and anybody. Just being naked in your own home. Like walking around, like you [can’t be bothered](## "non avere voglia, non darsi la pena di") putting your clothes on today, you know, ‘cause you’re having a chill-out day. You can just do it in your own home. We could be having this conversation nude if we really wanted to. You can take part in organised events once a year. You could go to a [skinny dip](## "fare il bagno nudi") on a holiday... There’s lots of different levels. Or people that just want to live full time at a naturist resort or they live on a farm in the [outback](## "entroterra, zona isolata") somewhere, and they are 100 per cent all year round kind of thing because, of course, unfortunately, we can’t do that in the UK or the British Isles, can we?

## REAL CONFIDENCE
Shuttleworth describes what it actually feels like to be naked among other people, explaining how social norms shift once everyone is undressed.
**James Shuttleworth:**This is what people need to understand is, when you’re all naked, you look the [odd one out](## "intruso, diverso") being clothed. When you can be the only one in the room naked, that’s when the real confidence comes in. So they say within the first fifteen minutes... but after that, you completely forget. If you’d asked me five, six, seven years ago, would you stand on a stage in front of 250 people and sing karaoke naked? I probably would have said no. But I tell you now, I’ve done it. I have done it. I’ve stood in front of those people, [sung my heart out](## "cantare a squarciagola, con passione"), [give it a go](## "provarci, tentare"), you might as well. And you know what? Honestly, it just gives you that [confidence boost](## "iniezione di fiducia") that you can’t really get anywhere else.

## NOBODY CARES
[More broadly](## "in senso più ampio"), Shuttleworth sees naturism as a space that promotes openness and self-acceptance, with real mental health benefits.
**James Shuttleworth:** The people that you actually come into contact with are quite open-minded ‘cause, of course you’ve got to be in order to do this, haven’t you? And so the majority of the people I speak to now on a daily basis are what I call friends, are naturists. I’ve just mentioned the [confidence boost](## "iniezione di fiducia"), the personal development that you get, the fact that your insecurities are [laid bare](## "messe a nudo, esposte"). And then eventually you realise that you don’t really care. Like a [boob](## "seno") [hangs](## "pendere") a bit lower than the other one, or that you’ve got a little bit of excess skin in some places, or your skin’s discoloured in somewhere else. Or I know someone was saying they’ve got an [issue](## "problema") with a [lazy eye](## "occhio pigro"). You’ve just got to accept it because that’s who you are. And by putting it out there, you kind of quickly forget and kind of think, “Oh, if nobody else cares, why should I care?” So yeah, there are [tons](## "un sacco") of mental health boosts and that’s what we keep trying to bang that drum in order to get it more visible. And the more people that are visible about it, then people think, “Okay, maybe it’s actually not a big [issue](## "problema").”

## GO OFFICIAL
For those curious about getting involved, Shuttleworth highlights several ways for newcomers to get started, emphasising the importance of beginning in safe, organised environments.
James Shuttleworth: You can go to organised events, so you can take part in official events [run](## "gestire, organizzare") by organisations, which of course I would encourage you to do, [due to the fact that](## "dato che") if you want to [give it a go](## "provarci, tentare"), not only have you got the safety net, but you know you’ve got someone to turn to and you know it’s going to be an official organisation. So you know they’re going to treat you well. You can go to the local beach if it’s allowed, take part in a [skinny dip](## "fare il bagno nudi") or just chill on the beach if you’ve got the sun. If you’ve got the sun! Or if you live in, for example, the Netherlands, Finland, Germany, they’re massive[ly] into their sauna culture, which, of course, you have to be naked for, don’t you? There are multitudes of different a[venues](## "luoghi"), but I would honestly 100 per cent start off and just have a look at your national federation’s website, because they have the local laws, they have all the local events.

## A LITTLE BIT OF TEXTILE
Recalling his own beginnings, Shuttleworth describes that his first naturist experience felt relatively comfortable to him, while [acknowledging](## "riconoscere") that others may struggle at first.
**James Shuttleworth:** My first event was a swimming competition. Now, I used to swim. I used to play water polo. So stripping down when you’re just in your speedos was, well, I mean, the name of the game, isn’t it? So it wasn’t as big of an [issue](## "problema"), because what is that little bit of extra textiles? But for example, a friend of mine, he [signed up](## "iscriversi") for three or four events, drove miles and miles and miles to the gate of the event, and then decided he couldn’t do it. And then on that last attempt, he then must have had some kind of courage and just did it. And he said, “I regret not doing this the other three times!”

## QUIRKS AND NUANCES
In the UK context, Shuttleworth notes that public nudity is often more tolerated than many people assume.
**James Shuttleworth:** You know, in the UK, every beach [pretty much](## "praticamente, più o meno") is a naturist beach. There are lots of different official, though, beaches. So if people have a bit of a [grumble](## "lamentela"), and the police will probably just say, just move [slightly](## "un po'") a bit further down or something like that. But this is what I mean: the movement is continuously in flux and each country has its own [quirks](## "stranezze, peculiarità") and [nuances](## "sfaccettature") and different [paths](## "vie") that it’s taking.

## GUIDELINES ON NAKED ETIQUETTE 
The International Naturist Federation has its own [guidelines](## "linee guida") to maintain a safe and respectful environment for everyone:
- Maintain a welcoming atmosphere by treating everyone with kindness and courtesy, whether in person or online.
- Respect personal [boundaries](## "limiti") and privacy, allowing everyone to feel safe and [at ease](## "a proprio agio"):
- Keep our spaces respectful and appropriate. Public sexual behaviour is inappropriate and unwelcome and may be a criminal offence.
- Be [mindful](## "consapevole, attento") of cultural differences and local [customs](## "usanze"). Respect the specific rules of the [venues](## "luoghi") you visit, whether they are clubs, resorts, beaches or anywhere else.
- Practise basic hygiene, including by always sitting on a towel.
- Help keep the environment clean and beautiful. Always leave it cleaner than you found it, never leaving [litter](## "rifiuti, spazzatura").

## THE MOST ACCEPTING
When it comes to those who find public nudity morally unacceptable, Shuttleworth reflects on the roots of that discomfort.
**James Shuttleworth:** I’d ask why. Is that because of modern-day ideologies and what society [deems](## "ritenere, considerare") as beauty? Or are you opposed to people just [accepting](## "aperte, inclusive") of who they are and joining a community that accepts you for you? Because I tell you now, it’s probably one of the most open communities, the one that’s most [accepting](## "aperte, inclusive") that you can be in. Because of course, nobody cares where you come from, your background, your faith, your social status, because [at the end of the day](## "in fin dei conti"), it just removes all barriers and all class systems and all religions, and everyone’s just treated as an equal.

## GO ABOUT YOUR BUSINESS
However, there are still many legal and cultural differences regarding how naturism is accepted in different countries. So checking local laws and regulations [beforehand](## "in anticipo") is essential.
**James Shuttleworth:** In the UK, if I wanted to, I could just strip naked and go for a walk around the park or go on a bike ride, [wherever](## "ovunque"). And because [I’m going about my daily business](## "svolgere le proprie attività quotidiane") and I have no intent to [cause harm](## "causare danno, fare del male"), there’s nothing that can happen. Whereas if I was to do that in France, it could be a [hefty](## "pesante") [fine](## "multa").

## NAILS, EYES, HAIR... PENISES
Nevertheless, Shuttleworth points out, there are a couple of clear (if unspoken) rules within naturist settings centred on respect and [awareness](## "consapevolezza") of others.
**James Shuttleworth:** The unspoken rule, I would say, is it’s okay to look, but just never [stare](## "fissare"). At the end of the day, everyone looks at each other’s penises and bodies and [whatever](## "qualsiasi siano"), but the key thing is you would do that anyway, because you’re looking at people’s [nails](## "unghie"), you’re looking at people’s eye colour, you’re looking at people’s hair. So it’s trying to understand who that person is. And then a second one is: make sure you always bring a towel for you to sit on.
