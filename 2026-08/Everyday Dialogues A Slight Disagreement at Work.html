Everyday Dialogues: A Slight Disagreement at Work [B1]

In ufficio può capitare di avere dubbi o di non essere d’accordo su una decisione. Ecco come gestire una situazione simile, in modo diplomatico, in inglese.

James: Right, I’ve reviewed your proposal. It’s good, but I have a few concerns about the timeline. Emma: Sure. What’s on your mind? James: I’m not sure the schedule is realistic given our resources. We may be overcommitting. Emma: I see your point, but the client is pushing for a quick turnaround. James: Then I suggest we manage expectations upfront to avoid issues later. What about delivering in phases? Emma: That could work, but I was hoping to provide everything in one go. James: Hmm, a phased delivery carries less risk, though. It would also allow us to ensure the expected level of quality. Emma: Fair enough. How would you structure it? James: We could prioritise the core features for phase one, then schedule the remaining items for phase two. Emma: That makes sense. It would also give us room to incorporate any feedback from the client. James: Exactly! Let’s align on that and update the proposal. Language

NOW LET’S REVIEW THE VOCABULARY!

A ‘timeline’ shows the dates when the various stages of a plan have to be completed. “What’s on your mind?” is an idiom used to ask someone what they are thinking about, often to enquire about specific worries. Used as a preposition, ‘given’ means ‘considering’ or ‘taking into account’. ‘Overcommitting’ means promising more than you are able to deliver. ‘Pushing for’ is a phrasal verb, meaning ‘to demand something repeatedly’. In this context, ‘a quick turnaround’ refers to the fast fulfilment of an order. ‘Upfront’ means ‘in advance’. (In a different context, it can also mean ‘honest’ or ‘direct’: “He was upfront about his expectations.”) ‘Issues’ are problems. ‘In one go’ means ‘all at once’, when you complete a task in a single attempt. When something carries less risk, it means there is less chance of negative consequences. ‘Fair enough’ is an informal way of saying that something is reasonable. ‘Core features’ are the capabilities of a product, service or system that are essential. Here, ‘give us room’ means ‘allow more time and flexibility’. “Let’s align on that” is corporate jargon used to confirm agreement.
