James: Right, I’ve reviewed your proposal. It’s good, but I have a few [concerns](## "preoccupazioni") about the timeline.
Emma: Sure. What’s on your mind?
James: I’m not sure the [schedule](## "programma") is realistic given our [resources](## "risorse"). We may be overcommitting.
Emma: [I see your point](## "comprendere il punto di vista di qualcuno"), but the client is pushing for a quick turnaround.
James: Then I suggest we manage expectations upfront to avoid issues later. What about delivering in phases?
Emma: That could work, but I was hoping to provide everything in one go.
James: Hmm, a phased delivery carries less risk, though. It would also allow us to [ensure](## "assicurare") the expected level of quality.
Emma: Fair enough. How would you structure it?
James: We could prioritise the core features for phase one, then [schedule](## "programma") the [remaining](## "rimanenti") items for phase two.
Emma: That makes sense. It would also give us room to incorporate any feedback from the client.
James: Exactly! Let’s align on that and update the proposal.

## NOW LET’S REVIEW THE VOCABULARY!
A 'timeline' shows the dates when the various stages of a plan have to be completed.
"What’s on your mind?" is an idiom used to ask someone what they are thinking about, often to [enquire](## "indagare") about specific worries.
Used as a preposition, 'given' means 'considering' or '[taking into account](## "tenere conto")'.
'Overcommitting' means promising more than you are [able](## "in grado di") to deliver.
'Pushing for' is a phrasal verb, meaning 'to demand something repeatedly'.
In this context, 'a quick turnaround' refers to the fast [fulfilment](## "adempimento") of an order.
'Upfront' means 'in advance'. (In a different context, it can also mean 'honest' or 'direct': "He was upfront about his expectations.")
'Issues' are problems.
'In one go' means 'all at once', when you complete a task in a single [attempt](## "intento").
When something carries less risk, it means there is less chance of negative consequences.
'Fair enough' is an informal way of saying that something is reason[able](## "in grado di").
'Core features' are the capabilities of a product, service or system that are essential.
Here, 'give us room' means 'allow more time and flexibility'.
"Let’s align on that" is corporate [jargon](## "gergo") used to confirm agreement.

* **taking into account** = tenere conto
* **able** = in grado di
* **fulfilment** = adempimento
* **resources** = risorse
* **remaining** = rimanenti
* **attempt** = intento
* **jargon** = gergo
* **concerns** = preoccupazioni
* **schedule** = programma
* **I see your point** = comprendere il punto di vista di qualcuno
* **ensure** = assicurare
* **enquire** = indagare
