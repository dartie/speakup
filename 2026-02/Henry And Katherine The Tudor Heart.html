Henry And Katherine: The Tudor Heart [B2]

Questo gioiello, che reca le iniziali di Enrico VIII e Caterina d’Aragona, ci parla del lusso che caratterizzava la corte del re.

In 2019, a metal detectorist unearthed an exceptional find in a field in Warwickshire, central England: a dazzling, heart-shaped pendant made of 24-carat gold, dating back to the 16th century. Inscribed with the intertwined initials ‘H’ and ‘K’, the artifact was made to celebrate Henry VIII’s marriage to Katherine of Aragon. According to specialists, it is a very rare piece of history. 

THE TUDOR BACKGROUND

Henry VIII, who was king of England from 1509 to 1547, is well-known for having six wives in an obsessive quest to have a male heir, and Katherine of Aragon was his first conquest. Katherine was a Spanish princess who was previously married to Arthur, Henry’s older brother and heir apparent to the throne. When Arthur died suddenly in 1502, Katherine became engaged to Henry and they married when Henry became king in 1509.  Katherine was devoted to her husband, and even acted as regent while Henry was away, playing a significant role in the defence of England against an attempted invasion. However, Henry’s quest for a male heir was never fulfilled in his union with Katherine, as only one daughter out of their six children survived. Henry started courting Katherine’s lady-in-waiting, Anne Boleyn, and asked the Pope to annul his marriage to Katherine in 1527. When the Pope refused, Henry decided to break away from the Catholic Church and to found the Church of England. Culture

A ROYAL RELIC

The Tudor Heart is an important historical witness to Henry and Katherine’s relationship, as most objects linked to it have been lost over time. The Heart acts like a window into the luxury of Henry VIII’s earlier reign and the strong union between Henry and Katherine. Researchers believe the pendant may have been created for a tournament that was held in October 1518, to mark their daughter’s engagement to the French heir apparent. Henry was known to commission London goldsmiths to create custom-made jewellery for members of the court to wear at major celebrations and state events.

A MUSEUM APPEAL

The Heart was found and then reported under the Treasure Act of 1996. This law says that anyone who discovers a possible treasure must report it to the authorities. People who do this can also receive a reward for helping to protect and share history. The British Museum now has the chance to buy the Heart so that it can stay in public hands and not be sold to a private collector. To do this, the museum needs to raise £3.5 million by April 2026. It has asked the public to help by making donations. The campaign is supported by British actor Damian Lewis, who played King Henry VIII in the TV series Wolf Hall. If the museum successfully raises the funds, the money will typically be split equally between the finder and the landowner, as this is the normal protocol in such cases.
