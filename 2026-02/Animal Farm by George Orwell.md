George Orwell (1903-1950) began his writing career in the early 1930s documenting the [plight](## "difficoltà") of the poor and the unemployed in England in articles, [essays](## "saggi") and books. In 1936, he moved to Barcelona to fight for the Republicans against Franco’s Nationalists in Spain’s Civil War. A convinced socialist, he was horrified by the murderous activities of Stalinist communists in Barcelona, and recounted his experiences in his memoir, Homage to Catalonia (1938). The rise of fascist and communist dictatorships, and his time in Spain, were the inspiration for Animal Farm: A [Fairy Story](## "fiaba"). 

## POLITICAL ALLEGORY
Animal Farm is a powerfully-written attack on totalitarianism, specifically Stalinism, in the form of a political allegory. It has a quintessentially English setting, a traditional farm. In the late 1930s and early 1940s, many people in the Western world considered that the Soviet communist system was a good example to follow. Orwell disagreed and was worried that this attitude would stop democratic socialism flourishing in the West. He decided to write a story satirising the Russian Revolution of 1917 and the replacement of the Tsarist system of government by a Stalinist autocracy. Orwell knew the horrific reality of life in Stalin’s Russia. His experiences in Spain had given him first-hand experience.

## ANIMAL FREEDOM
The book opens with the anthropomorphic animals [seizing](## "impossessarsi, conquistare") their freedom from the corrupt farmer, Mr. Jones — representing the Russian Tsar —, under the leadership of two young [boars](## "verri, maschi del maiale"), Napoleon and Snowball. They hope to create a free and equal society, independent of human intervention. The farm’s pigs are the intellectuals leading the revolution. Soon, however, the first signs of a new hierarchy start to appear. The pigs begin to take special privileges for themselves, starting with [windfall apples](## "mele cadute dall’albero"), now [plentiful](## "abbondanti") on the ground.

## HUNGER FOR POWER
Worse is to come as the power-hungry pig Napoleon — representing Stalin but also emblematic of dictatorial power — starts to take control. Napoleon decides that all farm questions will be decided by a committee of pigs, presided over by himself. The other animals are not happy. [Squealer](## "spione"), the pig who has become Napoleon’s [mouthpiece](## "portavoce"), tells them that:
“No one believes more firmly than Comrade Napoleon that all animals are equal. He would be only too happy to let you make your decisions for yourselves. But sometimes you might make the wrong decisions, comrades, and then where should we be?”
“Nessuno crede più fermamente del Compagno Napoleone che tutti gli animali siano uguali. Egli sarebbe felicissimo di lasciare che voi prendiate da soli le vostre decisioni. Ma a volte potreste prendere le decisioni sbagliate, compagni, e a quel punto dove finiremmo?”

## EXECUTIONS
Napoleon then increases his power by criticising and attacking Snowball (who represents the intellectual revolutionary Leon Trotsky) with invented stories. Snowball has to [flee](## "fuggire") the farm. Animals accused of helping Snowball in [plots](## "cospirazioni") are killed by Napoleon’s dogs, his protectors (the secret police who enforced Stalin’s infamous purges.)
“Whenever anything went wrong it became usual to attribute it to Snowball […] And so the tale of confessions and executions went on, until there was a pile of corpses lying before Napoleon’s feet and the air was heavy with the smell of blood.”
“Ogniqualvolta qualcosa andasse storto divenne usuale attribuirne la colpa a Palla di Neve. [...] E così la storia delle confessioni e delle esecuzioni andò avanti, finché non ci fu un mucchio di cadaveri ai piedi di Napoleone e l’aria divenne pregna dell’odore di sangue.”

## A CULT OF LEADER
Napoleon’s position becomes [unassailable](## "inattaccabile") when he introduces a cult of leader, or personality. The [praise](## "elogio") and [flattery](## "adulazione") become [ludicrous](## "ridicoli"), with the [hens](## "galline") crediting Napoleon for their laying “five eggs in six days,” and the cows in ecstasy because the leader has made the farm’s water taste delicious.
“Napoleon was now never spoken of simply as ‘Napoleon’. He was always referred to in formal style as ‘our leader Comrade Napoleon’, and the pigs liked to invent for him such titles as Father of All Animals, Terror of Mankind, Protector of the Sheep–fold, Ducklings’ Friend, and the like [...] It had become usual to give Napoleon the credit for every successful achievement  and every stroke of good fortune.”
“Ora non si parlava mai di Napoleone chiamandolo semplicemente “Napoleone”. Si faceva riferimento a lui sempre in modo formale chiamandolo “il nostro Capo, il Compagno Napoleone”, e ai maiali piaceva inventare dei titoli per lui come “Padre di tutti gli animali”, “Terrore del genere umano”, “Protettore dell’ovile”, “Amico degli anatroccoli” e cose del genere.[...] Era diventato consueto attribuire a Napoleone il merito per ogni risultato positivo e per ogni colpo di fortuna.”

## BETRAYING THE REVOLUTION
The Russian Revolution was intended to eliminate the capitalist class system, but it only replaced it with another hierarchy. In Animal Farm’s final pages, the pigs start walking on two legs and wearing clothes. They even reach agreements with local farmers. Orwell’s fable ends with the betrayal of the animals’ revolution. “All animals are equal but some are more equal than others” is Napoleon’s final [decree](## "decreto"), before inviting the local farmers for a game of cards. Outside in the [yard](## "cortile"), the animals watch the game through the farmhouse window. Inside, an [argument](## "discussione") begins.
“Twelve voices were shouting in anger, and they were all alike. No question, now, what had happened to the faces of the pigs. The creatures outside looked from pig to man, and from man to pig, and from pig to man again; but already it was impossible to say which was which.” 
“Dodici voci stavano urlando piene di rabbia, ed erano tutte simili. Ora non c’era nessun dubbio su cosa fosse successo ai volti dei maiali. Le creature che stavano fuori guardavano prima i maiali e poi gli uomini, e poi gli uomini e poi i maiali, poi ancora i maiali e gli uomini; ma era gi�� impossibile distinguerli.”

## DEMOCRATIC PROTESTS
Animal Farm [made Orwell a household name](## "rendere famoso") and an international figure. Since its publication, it has never been [out of print](## "fuori catalogo"). But in 1945 it was actually rejected by many publishers as Russia at that time was an important ally in the war against Nazi Germany. However, with the beginning of the Cold War, the novel would play its part for decades in democratic protests behind the [Iron Curtain](## "cortina di ferro"). This tradition has continued in recent years in countries as diverse as Myanmar, Zimbabwe and Ukraine.
