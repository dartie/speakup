Top 5 Wuthering Heights Adaptations [B2]

Il romanzo di Emily Brontë ha ispirato numerosi artisti: scopriamo alcune delle adattazioni (cinematografiche e non) di questa tormentata storia d’amore.

Emily Brontë’s 1847 novel Wuthering Heights has inspired more than twenty film adaptations and several TV miniseries, from Hollywood classics to international versions in Hindi, French, and Japanese. Each one explores a particular angle of the story’s passion and revenge, but only Peter Kosminsky’s 1992 film attempts to address the whole plot, from the childhood of the protagonists Cathy and Heathcliff, right through to the next generation.

WILLIAM WYLER’S WUTHERING HEIGHTS (1939)

This American adaptation stars British actors Laurence Olivier as Heathcliff and Merle Oberon as Cathy. It was filmed in California and has the vibe of a classic Hollywood romance, with the dirt and violence of the Yorkshire moors played down. Cathy’s line to Nelly Dean: “Nelly, I am Heathcliff,” is a classic cinema moment.

LUIS BUÑUEL’S ABISMOS DE PASIÓN (1953)

Hispano-Mexican director Luis Buñuel takes Brontë’s novel out of Yorkshire and reimagines it in a rural Mexican estate called El Robledal. Alejandro returns to the estate after ten years away and wants to marry Catalina, his childhood love. But Catalina has already married Eduardo. The core elements of the plot are the same, but the setting is very different.

PETER KOSMINSKY’S WUTHERING HEIGHTS (1992)

Starring Juliette Binoche as Cathy and Ralph Fiennes as Heathcliff, this version is easy on the eye, with period costumes designed by James Acheson. It includes the plot from the second half of the book around the characters of Linton (Heathcliff’s son,) Catherine (Cathy’s daughter,) and Hareton (Hindley’s son.) This makes the film less focused than those that concentrate just on Cathy and Heathcliff’s generation. Entertainment

ANDREA ARNOLD’S WUTHERING HEIGHTS (2011)

Arnold uses a soundtrack based on natural sounds — wind, insects, animals — to capture the wild atmosphere of the Yorkshire moors. She spends more time than most adaptations on the relationship between the young Cathy and Heathcliff. And she casts black actors: Solomon Glave as the child Heathcliff and James Howson as the adult.

KATE BUSH’S WUTHERING HEIGHTS  (1978)

This haunting song is written from the perspective of Cathy’s ghost. First, Cathy remembers being together with Heathcliff on the “windy moors” and asks, “How could you leave me / When I needed to possess you”.  The chorus: “I’m Cathy / I’ve come home, I’m so cold / Let me in your window” reflects the moment in the novel when Cathy’s ghost appears to Lockwood.
