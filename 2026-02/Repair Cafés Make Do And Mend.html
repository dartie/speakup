Repair Cafés: Make Do And Mend [B2]

In questa società usa e getta, la vera rivoluzione sta nel riparare e rammendare. Abitudini che fanno bene all’ambiente… e al portafoglio!

It’s a simple concept: fix up an item instead of tossing it in the rubbish. But in today’s throwaway society, the ‘make do and mend’ philosophy of previous generations has been lost. Across the EU, thirty-five million tonnes of repairable goods are discarded annually, along with twenty-two million damaged pieces of furniture and 11,000 bicycles. Thankfully, that trend is changing, helped by a global network of repair cafés that make learning repair skills fun and easy.

VOLUNTEERS

Repair cafés started in 2007 when Martine Postma, a Dutch environmentalist, produced a guide for her local community with tips on how to repair clothes, furniture, electrical appliances, bicycles, crockery, toys and other household items. A Repair Café in Amsterdam soon followed, where items could be brought in to be mended for free. Tools and materials were provided and expert volunteers were available to provide repair skills. There are now more than 2,500 repair cafés worldwide, including 250 in the UK. Current Affairs

WRITTEN INTO LAW

Repair cafés are funded by donations, grants and partnerships. The cafés are intended to complement rather than compete with professional repair services. Consumers are helped by new Right to Repair legislation, introduced by the European Commission and rewritten into UK law when it left the EU. The initiative places obligations on manufacturers to make it easier to find spare parts and instructions for repairing items. The cost of living crisis has also had an impact, with families desperate to save money. In 2024, 55 per cent of UK adults repaired an item rather than replacing it.  Fashion is one of the most polluting industries on the planet. In previous centuries, repairing items of clothing was a basic life skill. But fast fashion and cheap clothing have reduced the lifespan of everybody’s wardrobes. So what can be done? Take your favourite clothing to a repair café or professional repair service. Learn how to darn, patch, and stitch, knit or sew. Extending the life of a garment by one year reduces its environmental footprint by 25 per cent. If we’re serious about reducing environmental damage, we need to make clothes, and many other household items, last much longer.
