Love is a predominant [theme](## "colonna sonora") of almost all musical genres, particularly pop music. A 2018 study found that 67.3 per cent of chart hits from 1960 to 2010 were about the emotional happiness or torment associated with this multifaceted emotion. With tales of [undying](## "immortale, eterna") devotion or of the anguish of a broken heart, bards have used romantic serenades since songwriting began to [pull at listeners’ heartstrings](## "commuovere profondamente") with a subject that almost everyone can relate to. We list a selection of the greatest love songs to help with your English and to fill your heart! 

## A2 PRE-INTERMEDIATE
[Hailed](## "elogiare") by Frank Sinatra as the greatest love song of “the last fifty or one hundred years” (although he mis[take](## "versione")nly attributed it to principal Beatles songwriters John Lennon and Paul McCartney,) it was initially believed that guitarist George Harrison wrote this for his [then-wife](## "allora moglie") Patti Boyd. He later denied this in a 1976 interview, claiming it was about the Hindu [deity](## "divinità") Krishna: “When you love a woman, it’s the God in her that you see.” The simple and evocative lyrics use the present simple to make comparisons.
Something in the way she movesAttracts me like no other loverSomething in the way she [woos](## "corteggiare, conquistare") me
Perhaps Stevie Wonder’s most famous song, it remains the US singer-songwriter’s best-selling single to date, reaching number one in 28 countries worldwide at the time of its [release](## "uscita"). Featuring on the soundtrack of the romantic comedy The Woman In Red, written and directed by Gene Wilder, who also [starred in the film opposite](## "recitare con") Kelly LeBrock, the song won both an Oscar and Golden Globe. Listing all the reasons someone might telephone a loved one, but not actually needing any, this song includes easy and useful vocabulary with the use of ‘to’ for objectives.
No New Year’s Day to celebrateNo chocolate covered candy hearts to give awayNo first of spring, no song to singIn fact, here’s just another ordinary day
Better known as a goth band, The Cure surprised fans with what singer Robert Smith referred to as a “[dumb](## "sciocca") pop song.” In one of their most successful and [upbeat](## "allegre") songs, the singer celebrates his joy of being with his loved one at the weekend, [dismissing](## "respingere") her on weekdays. Using just the present simple and repeatedly listing the days of the week, this song is ideal for low level learners.
I don’t care if Monday’s blueTuesday’s grey and Wednesday’s tooThursday, I don’t care about youIt’s Friday, I’m in loveMonday you can [fall apart](## "cadere a pezzi")Tuesday, Wednesday break my heartOh, Thursday doesn’t even startIt’s Friday, I’m in love
Conceived as the [theme](## "colonna sonora") to a quickly forgotten 1955 film called Unchained, this is one of the most covered songs of all time. On its initial [release](## "uscita"), there were four versions simultaneously in the charts. The [Righteous](## "giusti") Brothers’ [take](## "versione") was originally a B-side, but radio DJs preferred it to the A-side and it became a huge hit. Twenty-five years later, when it soundtracked Patrick Swayze and Demi Moore’s [highly-charged](## "ad alta carica emotiva") [pottery](## "tornio, ceramica") scene in the film Ghost, it became 1990’s biggest-selling single. The song opens with the present perfect and uses simple ‘yes/no’ questions.
Oh, my love, my darlingI’ve [hungered for](## "desiderare") your touchA long, lonely timeAnd time goes by so slowlyAnd time can do so muchAre you still mine?

## B1 LOWER-INTERMEDIATE
Elvis’s publishing deal stipulated that he receive a 50 per cent writing credit, but neither he nor co-credited Vera Matson wrote Love Me Tender. Songwriter Ken Darby, Matson’s husband, used the melody of the 1861 American Civil War song Aura Lee to accompany this sentimental declaration of eternal love. Shortly before its [release](## "uscita"), Elvis performed the song on television, [prompting](## "incitare, indurre") unprecedented pre-sales of over a million copies. The lyrics use imperatives and the present perfect, but begin with two incorrect adverbs — he should be asking her to love him ‘tenderly’ and ‘sweetly’.
Love me tender, love me sweetNever let me goYou have made my life completeAnd I love you so
Irish singer Sinéad O’Connor’s cover of a little-known track by US superstar Prince propelled her to [superstardom](## "status di celebrità") and reached number one in eighteen countries. A [heart-wrenching](## "struggente") [plea](## "supplica") from an abandoned lover, one reviewer called it “the loveliest of love songs.” The lyrics detail the [void](## "vuoto") created by the ending of a relationship, opening with an example of the present perfect with ‘since’. 
It’s been seven hours and fifteen daysSince you took your love awayI go out every night and sleep all daySince you took your love awaySince you’ve been gone, I can do whatever I wantI can see [whomever](## "chiunque") I chooseI can eat my dinner in a [fancy](## "elegante") restaurantBut nothing, I said, nothing can [take](## "versione") away these [blues](## "tristezza")
Written for the film Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves starring Kevin Costner and Mary Elizabeth Mastrantonio, this power ballad soon took on a life of its own. Although it alienated some fans of Bryan Adams’ earlier rock style, it brought him to a whole new audience and this became one of the best-selling singles ever. The song, which Adams claims he “[knocked out](## "buttare giù") in about forty-five minutes”, is a heartfelt declaration of love using imperatives, the future ‘will’ and ‘when’ as a conjunction.
Look into my eyesYou will seeWhat you mean to meSearch your heartSearch your soulAnd when you find me thereYou’ll search no more

## B2 UPPER INTERMEDIATE
[Concerned](## "preoccupato") that 2014’s [corny](## "sdolcinata") romantic ballad Thinking Out Loud would be his defining song, Ed Sheeran [set out](## "decidersi") to write what became the most successful song of his career. A serenade to his [wife-to-be](## "futura moglie"), reflecting on their past, present and future, this captured the hearts of millions of listeners, with duets performed with Beyoncé and Andrea Bocelli bringing it to an even wider audience. The lyrics use the past simple, future, imperatives and descriptive adjectives.
I found a love for meOh, darling, just [dive right in](## "buttarsi, lanciarsi") and follow my [lead](## "protagonista")Well, I found a girl, beautiful and sweetOh, I never knew you were the someone waitin’ for me
Mis[take](## "versione")nly perceived as a romantic [paean](## "inno"), this is in fact a [prayer](## "preghiera"). English poet Brian Hinton compared the lyrics to Dante, naming it “one of the finest love songs of the century.” It was only a minor hit for Morrison but a cover by Rod Stewart was hugely successful in 1993. The song’s title and opening line were [pilfered](## "rubare") from a 1968 soul song and are an example of a question in the present perfect.
Have I told you lately that I love you?Have I told you there’s no one above you?Fill my heart with [gladness](## "gioia")Take away my sadness[Ease](## "alleviare") my troubles, that’s what you do
The best-selling single of all time by a female solo artist, Whitney Houston recorded this pop-ballad cover of a 1973 Dolly Parton country song for movie The Bodyguard, in which she made her debut as an actor [alongside](## "accanto a") Kevin Costner, the quintessential romantic [lead](## "protagonista") of 1990s cinema. The original was a [farewell](## "addio") to Parton’s business manager as she embarked on a solo career, but Whitney turned it into a [tear-jerking](## "strappalacrime") acceptance that her relationship, though still loving, was over. The lyrics use mixed conditionals and modals ‘should’ and ‘would’.
If I should stayI would only be in your waySo I’ll go, but I knowI’ll think of you every step of the wayAnd I will always love youI will always love you
