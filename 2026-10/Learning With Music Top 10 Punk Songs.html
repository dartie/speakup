Learning With Music: Top 10 Punk Songs [B2]

Prima di loro, la musica era tutta uguale. Con i loro look aggressivi e i testi provocatori, questi artisti diedero il via a una rivoluzione.

No other musical revolution in history occurred as quickly as that of the punk-rock revolution in the mid-1970s. Until then, the UK and US music charts had been dominated by bland, easy-listening ballads and classic rock that failed to reflect the disenchantment and alienation of much of these nations’ youth. Bands began to form throughout Britain, each channeling their anger into short, furious three-chord tirades against the establishment. Although not all were gifted musicians, their attitude and energy created the zeitgeist of the times. Many date punk’s emergence to the Sex Pistols’ formation in 1976, but it had already taken root in the US. Two years earlier, in New York City’s legendary CBGB nightclub, the Ramones were causing a sensation. Early pioneers of the genre include Iggy Pop and Patti Smith, who, over fifty years later, still perform.  

1. KICK OUT THE JAMS BY MC5(1969)

Although punk did not explode until the mid-1970s, American rock band MC5’s debut single laid the foundations seven years earlier. The band, managed by political activist John Sinclair, courted controversy and Kick Out the Jams, recorded live, opens with a rallying call featuring possibly music’s first obscenity. Written as a criticism of more famous bands’ lesser performances, the lyrics, largely in the present simple, depict the experience of a concert in a sweaty club.    Yes, I’m starting to sweat You know my shirt’s all wet What a feeling In the sound that abounds And resounds, and rebounds off the ceiling People

Johnny Rotten: Punk’s National Treasure Cantante dei Sex Pistols, band dalla breve durata ma dalla grande influenza sulla musica popolare, con la sua attitudine nichilista ha scandalizzato la società britannica affermandosi come un’icona per i giovani della fine del XX secolo. Daniel Francis , Conor Gleeson

2. I WANNA BE YOUR DOG BY THE STOOGES (1969)

Hailing from Detroit, the same city as MC5, the Stooges’ debut single is a homage to sadomasochism and depravation. Iconic frontman Iggy Pop is now considered the godfather of punk, and with I Wanna Be Your Dog wanted to write about the biggest thing he could imagine, but settled on reversing the word ‘God’. Produced by Velvet Underground founder John Cale, who played a single piano note throughout the song, the simple lyrics accompany a repeated droning three-chord guitar riff. Initially shocking to the public, it has since been recognised as one of the greatest songs of the decade. So messed up, I want you here In my room, I want you here Now we’re gonna be face-to-face And I’ll lay right down in my favourite place

3. GLORIA: IN EXCELSIS DEO BY PATTI SMITH (1975)

A key component of the New York punk movement, singer Patti Smith’s debut single was the result of an improvised jam based on Van Morrison’s 1964 hit Gloria. Another John Cale production, the song incorporates an earlier Smith poem and opens with a deliberately provocative and sacrilegious statement. Although about an encounter with a girl in a bar, Smith later clarified that her writing did not reflect her sexual preferences and is “beyond gender.” The lyrics are full of imagery and use the past, present, and ‘going to’ for future intentions. Jesus died for somebody’s sins, but not mineMelting in a pot of thieves,wild card up my sleeveThick, heart of stone, my sins, my own They belong to me, me

4. BLITZKRIEG BOP BY RAMONES  (1976)

Among the most influential bands of all time, New York band Ramones’ simple guitar riffs and infectious melodies brought punk rock to the masses. Regular performers at iconic punk venue CBGB, their debut single Blitzkrieg Bop describes fans at a concert and opens with the cry “Hey ho, let’s go”, one of rock’s most recognisable chants. Largely in the present continuous, the song’s title — a German military term meaning ‘lightning war’ that describes a strategy based on speed and surprise — has led some to interpret this as battle song. They’re forming in a straight line They’re going through a tight wind The kids are losing their minds The blitzkrieg bop Johnny Ramone (real name John William Cummings) on stage in Berlin’s Neue Welt in September 1978, marking the band’s very first concert in Berlin and Germany.

5. NEW ROSE BY DAMNED (1976)

Although the Sex Pistols were already a driving force on the burgeoning London punk scene, their one-time opening act, The Damned, were the first to release a single, with New Rose recognised as the UK’s first punk song. Recorded in just one day, the song appears to be a love song but is, in fact, about escapism and is entirely in the present simple. Covered by American superstars Guns N’ Roses in 1993, it is considered one of punk’s most important songs.  I got a feeling inside of me It’s kind of strange, like a stormy sea I don’t know why, I don’t know why I guess these things have got to be

6. GOD SAVE THE QUEEN BY SEX PISTOLS  (1977)

Punk’s defining band, London’s Sex Pistols exploded onto the scene with their debut single Anarchy in the U.K., a sneering tirade against the establishment. They followed this with God Save the Queen, released to coincide with Queen Elizabeth II’s Silver Jubilee. Radios refused to play it and many believe the sales charts were altered to keep it from hitting Number One. Its controversial lyrics, in the present simple, compared the monarch to the fascist regime and painted a desolate picture of alienation. God, save the Queen She ain’t no human being There is no future In England’s dreaming

7. WHITE RIOT BY THE CLASH  (1977)

When frontman Joe Strummer was caught up in a violent clash between police and black youths at the Notting Hill Carnival, he wrote what became The Clash’s debut single. Clocking in at less than two minutes, it is a call to arms to the white youth to emulate their counterparts to rise up against the establishment. Described by Billboard magazine as “the most controversial song The Clash ever did,” its lyrics are almost entirely in the present simple.  All the power’s in the hands Of people rich enough to buy it While we walk the street Too chicken to even try it

8. EVER FALLEN IN LOVE (WITH SOMEONE YOU SHOULDN’T’VE) BY BUZZCOCKS  (1978)

In Manchester, the band Buzzcocks were carefully watching the punk movement unfold in London, even borrowing a car to drive down to see the Sex Pistols. Their signature song soon followed and is a mix of the urgency and speed of punk with a pop melody. Inspired by dialogue in the 1955 musical Guys and Dolls, it details an unhealthy relationship and uses different grammar forms. The title includes the present perfect (omitting the auxiliary ‘have’ and subject ‘you’) and the modal ‘should’ for inadvisable past actions. You spurn my natural emotions You make me feel like dirt and I’m hurt And if I start a commotion I’ll only end up losing you, and that’s worse

9. ALTERNATIVE ULSTER BY STIFF LITTLE FINGERS  (1979)

Northern Ireland was a particularly grim place to live during the Troubles, the decades-long period of violence between unionist and nationalist groups, with regular bomb threats and armed military patrolling the streets. Derry-band The Undertones released their debut single Teenage Kicks the same week as Belfast’s Stiff Little Fingers’ Alternative Ulster. The former is an upbeat love song, later covered by One Direction, but the latter accurately reflects the despair of the times, opening with the line “There’s nothing for us in Belfast” and using the present simple and continuous to depict the bleakness of the situation.  Take a look where you’re livin’ You got the Army on the street And the RUC dog of repression Is barking at your feet Is this the kind of place you wanna live?

10. WARSAW BY JOY DIVISION  (1978)

Formed after a Sex Pistols concert, Joy Division was one of the most influential bands of the post-punk movement with their sparse atmospheric style. Their first recording, however, leaned more heavily towards their inspiration, tearing through three verses and the repeated three-word chorus “three-one-G”, referring to Nazi leader Rudolf Hess’ prisoner number. With much of the lyrics in the past simple, the band denied it reflected their political beliefs, saying they simply wanted to remember the sacrifices made in World War Two.  I was there in the back stage When the light came around I grew up like a changeling To win the first time around Click here and listen to the Spotify playlist with the Top 10 punk songs.
