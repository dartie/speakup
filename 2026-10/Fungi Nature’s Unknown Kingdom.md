Fungi — a [kingdom](## "regno") that includes [yeasts](## "lieviti"), [moulds](## "muffe"), and mushrooms — are vast, strange, and endlessly versatile. More importantly, they are essential to life on earth and yet they remain one of the great unknowns of the natural world.

## UNDISCOVERED
For centuries, fungi were classified as plants. It was only in 1969 that scientists finally gave them a [kingdom](## "regno") of their own. In fact, fungi are genetically closer to animals than to plants. Like animals, they cannot produce their own food through photosynthesis and must absorb nutrients from their surroundings. More than 150,000 species have been identified so far, but researchers believe millions more may still be undiscovered.

## MEDICINAL
Almost all plants depend on fungi living around their roots to help them absorb water and nutrients from the [soil](## "suolo, terreno"). Together, they form underground networks so vast and interconnected that scientists have [coined](## "coniare") the term ‘[wood wide web](## "rete micorrizica")’ to describe the way they [link](## "collegare") trees and plants through webs of microscopic [threads](## "filamenti, fili"). Fungi are also nature’s great recyclers. By [breaking down](## "decomporre") dead wood, leaves, and other organic matter, they return carbon and nutrients to the [soil](## "suolo, terreno"), making new life possible. Without them, dead plant material would simply accumulate around us.
As [COO](## "direttore operativo ('Chief Operating Officer')") of the North American Mycological Association, Bruch Reed has spent decades immersed in this world. We spoke with him to find out what makes fungi so fascinating. One area that is attracting growing interest is their potential use in medicine. Reed says that some of the most familiar mushrooms are also among the most promising.
Lingzhi mushroom cultivation. This mushroom has been used in traditional Chinese medicine for over 2,000 years. 
**Bruch Reed:** Mushrooms can be medicinal [across a number of ways](## "in diversi modi"). [Edible](## "commestibili") mushrooms are nutritious and delicious and contain protein, [may or may not](## "possono o meno") contain various substances that help our [gut](## "intestino") health, help brain health. There are mushrooms that have been used for millennia in TCM — traditional Chinese or Asian medicine —, such as the Phellinus [genus](## "genere"), reishi mushroom, which is Ganoderma, various others. [Lion’s mane](## "criniera di leone"), for example, is a culinary mushroom that’s delicious and amazing and versatile and super nutritious and has been shown to be neuroregenerative. So it stimulates the growth of new neurological cells in adult mammals. And it’s being investigated for treatments for Parkinson’s disease, et cetera.

## PSYCHOACTIVE
Psychoactive mushrooms containing psilocybin have been used for centuries by Indigenous cultures and are now being studied for their potential role in treating conditions such as depression and [PTSD](## "disturbo da stress post-traumatico ('Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder')"). While the subject remains controversial, Reed believes it [deserves](## "meritare") serious scientific discussion.
**Bruch Reed:** Psychoactive mushrooms are native to North America. They are abundant, particularly along the coasts. And while my organization will never [take an advocacy position](## "schierarsi a favore") [one way or another](## "in un modo o nell’altro") on those, we do acknowledge and study and document those. They are being extensively used to treat [PTSD](## "disturbo da stress post-traumatico ('Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder')"), depression. There are investigations into neurological [healing](## "guarigione") from those mushrooms because they can really be good for people. So while we’re never going to advocate for them, we are definitely documenting the science about those. And it needs to be discussed. It’s reality.

## DELICACY
Some of the world’s most celebrated foods — including [truffles](## "tartufi"), porcini, [chanterelles](## "finferli, gallinacci"), and [morels](## "spugnole") — are fungi [prized](## "apprezzare") for their distinctive flavours and aromas. According to Reed, mushrooms can evoke an astonishing variety of tastes and textures, ranging from meat and chicken to bread, flowers, and even sweets.
**Bruch Reed:** As a kid, I only liked [morels](## "spugnole"). I wouldn’t eat anything else. I always hated the cream of mushroom soup. [Yuck!](## "che schifo!") And I didn’t like store-bought mushrooms. And I came to find out there are mushrooms that have the taste and texture of chicken that can [freak out](## "sconvolgere") vegans when you serve it [them] to them. There are mushrooms that taste like candy. There are mushrooms that taste like meat, like absolutely like a meat dish, just naturally on their own. There are mushrooms that taste like flowers and bread and [cucumber](## "cetriolo"). There’s this wide variety of flavour profiles and texture profiles that you can experience while enjoying something that provides health benefits and is nutritious. I love eating them. I love it! And most mushrooms are edible. Are they good? Maybe not, but most are edible. Some are toxic. Some of the toxic ones will absolutely end your life. So that’s why education is just so important. The most important component of mycology is propagating the knowledge.

## EAT THEM!
The mushrooms found in supermarkets offer only a [glimpse](## "occhiata, scorcio") of the fungal [kingdom](## "regno")’s culinary diversity. Reed points out that flavour can vary dramatically depending on where a mushroom grows. He encourages people to explore new varieties while learning from experienced [foragers](## "raccoglitori") and experts.
**Bruch Reed:** Some of the reasons I didn’t like the mushroom soup is that those mushrooms are just [boiled](## "bollire"). They have a [narrow](## "limitato") flavour profile. Now, those are Agaricus [genus](## "genere") mushrooms. Wild Agaricus are [a world of difference apart](## "completamente diverso"). One of the ones that tastes so delicious like meat is the state mushroom of Colorado, Agaricus julius. And that thing smells like [almonds](## "mandorle") and umami and tastes like sweet meat. It’s so delicious. And that’s an Agaricus mushroom. The same species that are grown in sterilized [manure](## "letame") on farms, you can find that same mushroom growing [wild](## "selvatico"), and the flavour profile will be incredibly different from the [terroir](## "terroir, territorio d’origine") and the conditions. I will say, I’ve been pleased to see that over the last twenty years, there are increasing varieties of mushrooms, cultivated and [wild](## "selvatico"), that are offered in a lot of grocery stores. They’re very good. The flavour profiles and textures may vary from [wild](## "selvatico"). You can find lion’s mane and [hen of the woods](## "grifola frondosa, maitake") and [chanterelles](## "finferli, gallinacci") and winter [chanterelles](## "finferli, gallinacci") and shiitakes. Oh, shiitake mushrooms are so good! So getting those from the store, great. What a great mushroom to eat. So I encourage people to try them, but I also encourage people to join their mushroom club, learn from their local experts. You can expand your repertoire of mushrooms that you eat on your own. It is important that you always remember only to eat a mushroom if you personally can identify it or it’s identified by someone who is [knowledgeable](## "esperto, competente"). It’s so much fun to walk out into a forest and pick a luxury food that you probably couldn’t afford. I mean, [morels](## "spugnole") are fifty bucks a pound. I’ve never bought a morel in my life, and I wouldn’t. There’s a lot of adventuring to be done safely in the mushroom world, and if you’re a [foodie](## "appassionato di gastronomia"), you should [avail yourself of](## "approfittare di") it. Your life will change if someone properly cooks for you fresh [black trumpets](## "trombette dei morti"). The scent is [intoxicating](## "inebriante"). The flavour is this elusive but delicious umami, sweet forest mushroom essence. They’re sacramental in terms of a culinary [delight](## "delizia"), honestly. Fresh porcini, oh! Fresh [oysters](## "orecchioni"), what a [delight](## "delizia")! What a [delight](## "delizia") it is to eat [woodland](## "bosco") fresh [oysters](## "orecchioni"). Eat it all you want!
