ShakeOut Day: Get Ready for an Earthquake! [B2]

Il 15 ottobre, migliaia di persone in tutto il mondo parteciperanno a questo evento, nato per insegnare come comportarsi in caso di un forte terremoto.

When the world shakes, will you know what to do? No matter where you live, it’s a good idea to participate in International ShakeOut Day, by practising what to do when an earthquake occurs. On the third Thursday of October, millions of people will participate in earthquake drills in their homes, schools, and offices, at precisely 10.15am local time.

DEVASTATION AND DEATH

An earthquake is a natural phenomenon caused by sudden movement along faults in the earth’s crust. The movement releases energy in the form of seismic activity, which causes the ground to shake, sometimes violently. The strongest registered earthquake was in Valdivia, Chile, in 1960. It was 9.5 on the Richter scale and caused immense destruction, devastation, and death. Culture

Molly Malcolm Although we hear about the biggest earthquakes in the news, earthquakes occur every day, and can occur anywhere on the planet. The US National Earthquake Information Center says there are about fifty-five earthquakes a day, or twenty thousand a year, globally. However, most of them are too small for us to feel. And while they can occur anywhere, they mostly occur along fault lines, where tectonic plates interact.

THE RING OF FIRE: UNDERWATER VOLCANOES AND EARTHQUAKE SITES

The Ring of Fire. The Pacific Ring of Fire is a geodynamically active region where plate interactions produce volcanic arcs, trenches and earthquakes, and where approximately 90 per cent of global seismic activity occurs.

THE RING OF FIRE

About 90 per cent of the world’s earthquakes occur along the Ring of Fire. This is a chain of volcanoes and seismically active sites that surrounds most of the Pacific Ocean. It is about 40,000km long and up to 500km wide, and includes countries such as Japan, Indonesia, and Chile, as well as the west coast of the US – including the state of California. In 1906, the Californian city of San Francisco had the deadliest earthquake in US history. It destroyed over 80 per cent of the city and killed over three thousand people.

DROP, COVER AND HOLD ON

California has had several destructive earthquakes since then. And so, in 2008, the Southern California Earthquake Center organized the first ShakeOut Day, and it soon became a global event. Participants in the event practise the Drop, Cover, and Hold On drill, by dropping to the ground, taking cover, and holding on — which is what you should immediately do when an earthquake occurs. Drop, Cover, and Hold On.

THE RICHTER SCALE

The Richter scale was developed in 1935 by American seismologists Charles F. Richter and Beno Gutenberg to objectively measure earthquake magnitude. Because it is a logarithmic scale, the numbers represent massive exponential growth with each step; a whole number increase signifies a ten-fold increase in measured ground shaking amplitude and roughly a thirty-two-fold increase in actual energy released. For example, a massive 9.5 magnitude earthquake does not just feel slightly stronger than a 7.5 magnitude quake, but actually releases roughly a thousand times more energy The Richter scale.
