“The Fountainhead” by Ayn Rand [B2]

Un romanzo che riflette le convinzioni dell’autrice sull’individualismo: il suo giovane eroe rifiuta di scendere a compromessi e di conformarsi a un mondo mediocre.

Born Alisa Zinovyevna Rosenbaum in Saint Petersburg, Russia in 1905, Ayn Rand was the pen name of Alice O’Connor, a Russian-American writer and philosopher. In her novels and plays, Rand developed Objectivism, a form of philosophy that promoted individualism and capitalism. Set in the world of architecture, the novel The Fountainhead, published in 1943, became a bestseller. 

CLIENTS

A classic tale of integrity, courage, and betrayal, The Fountainhead tells the story of Howard Roark, an architect in New York City. Roark is a visionary equipped with skill and determination, who works on building sites to develop his knowledge of construction. As a student, he faces expulsion from college and is called to see the dean, who offers him a way to save his academic career. Roark, however, refuses to compromise: “ ‘I could say that I must aspire to build for my client the most comfortable, the most logical, the most beautiful house that can be built. I could say that I must try to sell him the best I have and also teach him to know the best. I could say it, but I won’t. Because I don’t intend to build in order to serve or help anyone. I don’t intend to build in order to have clients. I intend to have clients in order to build.’” “Io potrei dire che devo aspirare a costruire per il mio cliente la casa confortevole più razionale e bella che io possa costruire. Potrei dire che mi riprometto di fargli accettare quello che secondo me è il bello e il meglio. Potrei dirlo, ma non lo farò. Perché non ho alcuna intenzione di costruire per servire o aiutare alcuno, di costruire per formarmi una clientela. Avrò prima i clienti e poi costruirò.” Scenes from the 1949 film adaptation of The Fountainhead. Directed by King Vidor, the film stars Gary Cooper as architect Howard Roark, Raymond Massey as publishing magnate Gail Wynand, and Patricia Neal as Dominique Francon.

JOB HUNTING

Roark symbolises idealism and modernism. He is caught in the kind of political and stylistic battles that occurred in real-world New York architecture during the Skyscraper Era of the 1920s and 1930s. Peter Keating is the polar opposite of Roark, and his ambitious but less talented college rival seeks Roark’s advice throughout his career. However, while finding a job is easy for Keating, it is almost impossible for non-conformist Roark. When Keating helps Roark get a job with leading New York architect Guy Francon, it doesn’t go well. “‘Am I correct in gathering,’ Francon asked, ‘that you are criticising me and teaching me something about architecture?’ ‘I’m begging you,’ said Roark, closing his eyes. ‘If you weren’t a protégé of Mr. Keating’s, I wouldn’t bother to discuss the matter with you any further. But since you are quite obviously naive and inexperienced, I shall point out to you that I am not in the habit of asking for the esthetic opinions of my draftsmen […] you will follow my instructions as to the Classic treatment of the façade.’ ‘I can’t do it,’ said Roark, very quietly.” “«Sbaglio» chiese «oppure ho ragione di ritenere che lei voglia criticare i miei metodi di lavoro, i miei gusti, e i progetti che escono dal mio studio e che i miei impiegati eseguiscono su ordine mio?» «Io volevo solo pregarla…» «Se lei non mi fosse particolarmente raccomandato da Keating non mi prenderei la briga di discutere neppure per un istante. Ma dal momento che lei è così evidentemente ingenuo e privo di esperienza, le farò notare che non rientra nelle mie abitudini il chiedere l’opinione estetica dei miei collaboratori. […] lei seguirà le mie istruzioni specie per quello che riguarda l’interpretazione classica della facciata.» «Non posso farlo» disse Roark, calmo.” Classic Books

AFFAIR

Francon sacks Roark, and word soon spreads. Feared by the establishment, Roark finds that rivals will stop at nothing to destroy his reputation. He begins an affair with Dominique Francon, daughter of the architect who sacked him. Dominique loves Roark, but, unable to watch the world reject him, she would rather act first: “‘You know that I hate you, Roark. I hate you for what you are, for wanting you, for having to want you. I’m going to fight you — and I’m going to destroy you — and I tell you this as calmly as I told you that I’m a begging animal. I’m going to pray that you can’t be destroyed.’” “«Lei sa che io la odio, Howard» proseguì «la odio perché la desidero, e non posso desiderare che lei. Ma lotterò contro di lei, perché lei sia avvilito, piegato, distrutto. Pregherò perché lei possa soffrire e pregherò - le dirò anche questo - anche se io non credo in nulla e non so a chi rivolgere la mia preghiera.”

INDEPENDENCE

Roark and Keating work together to design a large housing project. However, despite promising not to, Keating makes changes to Roark’s design. When Roark finds out, he is furious and dynamites the site, destroying his work. In court, Roark defends the virtues of creative integrity, thereby justifying his actions: “‘The creator lives for his work. He needs no other men. His primary goal is within himself. The parasite lives second-hand. He needs others. Others become his prime motive. The basic need of the creator is independence. The reasoning mind cannot work under any form of compulsion. It cannot be curbed, sacrificed or subordinated to any consideration whatsoever. It demands total independence in function and in motive. To a creator, all relations with men are secondary.’”  “Il creatore vive per il proprio lavoro. Egli non ha bisogno degli altri. Suo scopo principale è lui stesso. Il parassita ha bisogno degli altri. Gli altri diventano il suo scopo principale. Il bisogno fondamentale del creatore è l’indipendenza. La mente che ragiona non può lavorare sotto alcuna forma d’obbligo. Non può essere contenuta, sacrificata o subordinata ad alcuna considerazione. Esige una totale indipendenza, nelle sue funzioni e nelle sue motivazioni. Per il creatore, ogni relazione con gli uomini è secondaria.

IDEAL MAN

Much more than a novel about architecture, The Fountainhead describes a collision of ideals and the drive to build something meaningful in a world that rewards conformity and mediocrity. Rand herself said that she wrote The Fountainhead to portray her vision of an ideal man. In Howard Roark, she believed she had succeeded.
