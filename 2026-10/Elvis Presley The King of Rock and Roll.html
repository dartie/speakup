Elvis Presley: The King of Rock and Roll [B2]

Con la sua innovativa fusione di gospel, country e rhythm and blues, il “re del rock and roll” rivoluzionò la cultura pop, diventando un’icona senza tempo.

Elvis Presley, known around the world as just Elvis, was one of the most popular, influential artists of the 20th century. He spearheaded the popularisation of a new, Black-rooted musical genre, rock and roll, with his own exciting combination of African-American blues, gospel music and traditional white country, and western music. Confident and charismatic, with an electrifying stage presence, his image of open sexuality and rebellion, manifest in his trademark sneer and curled lip, redefined popular music. It also helped liberate young Americans from the conservative social conventions of the time. In his astonishing breakout year in 1956, the American media began calling him “the King of Rock and Roll”. 

POOR ORIGINS

Presley was born into grinding poverty in Tupelo, Mississippi, on 8 January 1935. His identical twin brother, Jesse, was stillborn. He was a keen amateur singer from childhood, with a unique vocal style, and in 1953 Elvis decided to send an audition tape to producer Sam Phillips at Sun Records, a blues label in Memphis, Tennessee. Phillips invited him in to record. In a jam session on 5 July 1954, Elvis, guitarist Scotty Moore and bassist Bill Black started improvising a startling synthesis of blues, gospel, and country music. Elvis’ unique rock and roll sound was born. When news spread of this exciting new sound, an entertainment promoter, Colonel Tom Parker, signed him up. Elvis Presley with his White Knabe grand piano at Graceland.  In 1955, Elvis and Parker signed a massive contract with RCA Victor. Success was immediate. In 1956, the singer had seventeen songs on the Billboard Top 100 chart, with three reaching Number One: Heartbreak Hotel, Don’t Be Cruel, and Love Me Tender. The three songs defined a genre.

The first time that I appeared on stage, it scared me to death. I really didn’t know what all the yelling was about. I didn’t realize that my body was moving. It’s a natural thing to me.

ELVIS THE PELVIS

Elvis’ fame then skyrocketed after three groundbreaking TV performances on The Ed Sullivan Show. His historic first appearance on 9 September 1956 was watched by an incredible sixty million viewers (82.6 per cent of the TV audience.) Conor Gleeson , Sarah Davison Over the next two years, Elvis dominated music charts around the world, introducing the age of rock and roll and opening the door for both white and Black artists in the US. Elvis always acknowledged the Black contribution to his sound. In effect, he was bringing the Black man’s blues to young white Americans. Racists were outraged, feeling that Elvis was threatening America’s racial status quo.

IN THE ARMY

And then, on 20 December 1957, Elvis received his draft notice to join the Army. Young Americans exploded in grief. Elvis was stationed in West Germany until March 1960. While there, he began a relationship with Priscilla Beaulieu, the fourteen-year-old daughter of an Air Force officer. The couple would marry in 1967. Elvis serving in the US Army in Germany in 1958 John Lennon said that Elvis died when he entered the Army. The singer basically disappeared. On his reappearance, however, he continued as a star, but in the movie world. In eight years, he made twenty-seven movies, including G.I. Blues, Blue Hawaii and Viva Las Vegas. The light comedic romances were box office hits but had little artistic value. No longer controversial, Elvis was now a predictable mass entertainer. However, in December 1968, he surprised the world with an extraordinary TV show, the ‘68 Comeback Special. Suddenly the old Elvis was back, and he proved his enduring vitality in a tour de force of rock and roll and rhythm and blues. 

DEATH IN VEGAS

But then Elvis forgot his origins again, returning to mass entertainment. Beginning in July 1969, the singer gave 636 sold-out concerts in seven years at the International Hotel in Las Vegas. At first an excellent showman, he ended up regularly parodying himself on stage. By now he was in terrible health, surrounded by sycophants in Graceland, his Memphis estate, eating junk food and addicted to prescription pills. On 16 August 1977, he died, officially from cardiac arrhythmia, at the age of just forty-two.

The image is one thing and the human being is another… it’s very hard to live up to an image.

Elvis Presley recorded more than four hundred songs and sold over one billion records. Changing the course of music forever, he inspired countless musicians, from Jerry Lee Lewis and Carl Perkins to Springsteen, the Beatles, Dylan, and Led Zeppelin. Lennon said, “Before Elvis, there was nothing.” Owner of one of the most recognisable voices in human history, he is the greatest rock ‘n’ roller of them all.

ELVIS THE MAN

Elvis was a character of contradictions. Imperious on stage, he was shy, polite and quietly-spoken when not performing. He had an almost comic disbelief in his ability to inspire hysteria or reverence in his fans. The singer basically did everything his manager, Colonel Parker, told him to do. He never performed outside the US, except for three concerts in Canada. It seems that Parker, who was actually an illegal immigrant from The Netherlands, was afraid of being refused re-entry to the US if he travelled abroad. So millions of fans around the world never saw Elvis perform live.  He was legendary in his generosity, gifting cars, houses and cash to friends, family and even strangers. And yet it seems his fear of poverty never left him. He was also very close to his mother, Gladys, who died in 1958 at the age of just forty-six. Some friends say he never recovered from her death.  He had a deep-rooted Christian faith, read the Bible every day, and prayed before concerts. But he was always questioning the reason for his existence and read books all his life, looking for answers. He believed he had been put on earth for some purpose, but he did not know what. Some critics think he took consolation in pills. One of his biographers, Peter Guralnick, thinks that the singer was disappointed in himself, a victim of defeated expectations: “Elvis died of boredom.” John Lennon was equally blunt, putting blame on the singer’s entry into the army: “That’s when they killed him, that’s when they castrated him. The rest of it was just a living death.” In his final years, Elvis had an enlarged colon, glaucoma, heart disease, high blood pressure, hypertension, and liver damage. He was overweight and addicted to drugs. In his last eight months, his personal doctor prescribed over ten thousand doses of amphetamines, narcotics, and sedatives. Once the owner of the most famous voice in pop music, Elvis stumbled through his final concerts, slurring his words. Elvis the Pelvis, who once thrilled fans with his suggestive hip movements, could barely move on stage. When he died, weighing almost 160 kilos, his heart was double the normal size.
