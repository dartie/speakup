# A Short Story: The Deed to Be Done   [C1]

Dopo un colpo in testa, il protagonista di questo racconto non riesce a capire la situazione in cui si trova. È in pericolo? Qualcuno vuole ucciderlo? La verità, però, è molto diversa.

Woof! Woof! Woof!”
When I open my eyes, the pain explodes like a silent flash. A cold, deep wave of agony [spreads](## "propagarsi") from my left temple and [washes over](## "attraversare") my body. It takes me a moment to realise that my head is resting on a hard place. The side of my face is [dripping](## "gocciolare") blood in the rain. I see red all around. Then black again.
“Woof! Woof!”
It takes me a few seconds to figure out where the sound is coming from. Oh, the pain... I try to focus my eyes. It’s a dog — a black and white [shepherd](## "cane da pastore") of some kind. It seems to be [hovering](## "fluttuare") over me in the dark. Of course, it is not. It is me who is underground, lying in a [muddy ditch](## "fossato fangoso"). My head is [throbbing](## "pulsare"), as if something inside were [pounding](## "colpire") against the sides trying to get out of my [skull](## "cranio"). I feel [numb](## "intorpidito") and weak, but somehow I force myself to sit up, with the black dog still barking non-stop. I turn around and my foot hits something that [scrapes](## "raschiare") the rock with the sound of [rusty iron](## "ferro arrugginito") — a [shovel](## "pala"). I [grab](## "afferrare") it by its wooden handle and wave it to scare the dog away. He [barely](## "a malapena") retreats, but at least he stops barking, a [puzzled look](## "espressione perplessa") in his eyes.
In the [dim](## "fioca") light of the [waxing moon](## "luna crescente") I take a moment to try to [make out](## "distinguere") where I’m standing. The ditch is a [rough](## "irregolare") rectangle, about six feet deep and another six feet long, maybe a couple of feet wide. It is — I realise with horror — a [grave](## "tomba"). I wave the [shovel](## "pala") at the dog again. I need to get out of here before my [[grave](## "tomba")digger](## "becchino") comes to finish the job.
Running across the wet grass in rubber boots and a large plastic poncho that [flaps](## "svolazzare") in the cold night wind, I keep [tripping](## "inciampare") and falling. The dog [chases](## "inseguire") me, but still doesn’t try to bite me. It is as if he’s running alongside me rather than hunting me. It is barking non-stop, however, as though he’s trying to say something.

I run towards the only light I see, a small wooden house at the end of a narrow path. [Drowning](## "annegare") in pain, I push the front door open. The dog stays outside, barking no more. Somehow I find the [latch](## "chiavistello") instinctively — muscle-reflex-like — and lock the door. The place is silent except for the sound of the rain on the wooden roof.
I go straight into the bathroom. In the mirror I see a face that the pain [renders](## "rendere") unrecognizable, covered in a pink [hue](## "sfumatura"). It has to be me. Who else? There’s a [gaping wound](## "ferita aperta") of some five inches above my left [eyebrow](## "sopracciglio"); the [throbbing](## "pulsare") in my head is the loudest sound within the stillness of the house. Blood, rain, and mud — that face could be anyone’s. I could be anyone. I find some sort of [bandage](## "benda") in the bathroom cabinet and a few aspirins, which I [swallow in one gulp](## "ingoiare in un solo sorso"). What next? The [numb](## "intorpidito")ing pain clouds my thinking. Is this my [[grave](## "tomba")digger](## "becchino")’s house? Is this his dog? Who [digs](## "scavare") a [grave](## "tomba") in his own [yard](## "cortile")? For whom?
I lock every door and window and try to stay awake. The pain helps with that. From a window I see the dog lying on the porch, a strange tranquillity in his rhythmic breathing. Despite the [throbbing](## "pulsare") in my head I try to listen for steps approaching. Surely my [executioner](## "boia, carnefice") must be looking for me. But why? Why should anyone want to kill me? I try to find an answer in my mind but there’s no [recollection](## "ricordo") that could explain these events. I mustn’t let the anguish take control of me, I tell myself. I must stay alert [until dawn breaks](## "alba, sorgere del sole"), when I can get out of this house and find help. I will figure out the chain of events afterwards.
Dawn breaks and I can see the dog sleeping peacefully on the porch. All around the house, the grass is wet with morning [dew](## "rugiada"). There is no one in sight, so I wait no longer and open the door a few inches. Instantly, the dog sits up on his [hind legs](## "zampe posteriori"), [wagging](## "scodinzolare") his tail as if happily awaiting my next command.
On the path leading to and from the house, still wet and muddy, I can see the footprints of my rubber boots and the dog’s [paw prints](## "impronte delle zampe"). [Oddly](## "stranamente"), they come and go in both directions. On the path up to where my [grave](## "tomba") was dug, I see a continuous track, deeper than the footprints and [rough](## "irregolare")ly two feet wide. I cannot recall walking up that path, but I feel the imperative to follow the track, the dog diligently escorting me, as though that is [the thread I must pull](## "seguire una pista").
It leads me to my [intended](## "prevista") [grave](## "tomba").
Oh, I understand now... I don’t recall anything, yet I understand.
Oh, the horror…!
Knowing is worse than living in fear and ignorance. Better to expect a terrible end than to realise one’s true [duty](## "dovere") — the task at hand that, once commenced, cannot be abandoned. There it is, [wrapped](## "avvolgere") in plastic at the end of the track, on the edge of the [grave](## "tomba").
The [executioner](## "boia, carnefice")’s job. The last part of the [[grave](## "tomba")digger](## "becchino")’s business. The [deed](## "azione") [yet to be done](## "ancora da fare").
